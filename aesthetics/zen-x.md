# Zen-X

**Zen-X** is an aesthetic that emerged as a reaction to the excess of the 1980s, and as a part of the renewed environmentalist movement of the 1990s, alongside [Eco-Beige](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Eco-Beige "Eco-Beige"). This aesthetic is characterized by its usage of Zen Buddhism, natural materials such as bamboo, grass, and flowing water, imagery of yoga and Zen gardens, and the incorporation of Japanese architecture and design elements.

## History

The 1980s was seen as a decade of abundance. A booming economy fostered a culture of materialism and consumption. Music transformed into a visual medium with the launch of MTV in 1981, and the high-stakes investing of Wall Street created the subcultures of [Yuppies](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Yuppie "Yuppie") and [Power Dressing](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Power_Dressing "Power Dressing"). The design trends were neon, loud, and patterned, with heavy makeup, ostentatious jewelry, and big hair becoming the standard.

As a result of this decade of opulence, the 1990s was different -- a little more down-to-earth in some ways, a little more counterculture in others. After the discovery of the Antarctic ozone hole and the ban of the chemical hairsprays of the 80s that contributed to it, combined with the United Nations reporting on climate change and recommending that CO2 emissions be reduced worldwide, the youth of the 90s became much more environmentally conscious as well. In 1990, 76% of Americans identified themselves as "environmentalists."

Zen-X originated as a reaction to the greed and consumption of the 80s. It rejected the consumerist culture of the decade before and asserted that life should be lived simply and honestly, and that the environment deserved protection. Businesses began pivoting their brands to incorporate Green Marketing in an attempt to seem progressive and environmentally friendly. They emphasized their usage of recyclable materials, fair-trade practices, and conservation-focused ideals.

## Visuals

The visuals for Zen-X are meant to evoke a sense of serenity and peace. **Calming, naturalistic colors** (blue, green, brown, yellow, and white) are generally the go-to when it comes to Zen-X imagery. Plants and nature are often a huge focus of the photos within this aesthetic, with **bamboo, blades of grass, moss, and water** being typical. It is also common to see people, most often women, in **yoga and meditation poses**. **Wood** is also a big motif, be it in the form of actual trees or in the form of wooden furniture, detailing and decor.

## Fashion

Though less a fashion trend and more so a design, interior design and lifestyle trend, Zen-X still has a few notable fashion aspects to it. As a key aspect of this aesthetic is personal wellness, the most common clothing choice associated with this Zen-X is **athleisure and active wear**, particularly **women's wear**. Breathable and natural fabrics are prioritized within this aesthetic, so **bamboo, organic cotton, wool and modal** are key, as are performance synthetics like **nylon, polyester, and Lycra,** though these are often less common due to their non-renewable production, their inability to biodegrade, and other aspects with harmful environmental impact.

### Hair and Makeup

Hair is **worn natural and without much product in it** and is oftentimes cut short, such as in a **pixie or bixie cut**, or in a **blunt cut or bob**. Makeup is often intentionally **subtle and matte with a muted-to-dark color scheme**, for a more "bare-faced" look. Nails are kept neat and trimmed, usually **nude or with a French manicure.**

## Philosophy

Zen-X is a product of 90s environmentalism, thus, it operates under the belief that environmentalism and consumer conservation are the biggest responsibility of humankind, both as a whole and as individuals. It argues that individual habits (recycling, avoiding aerosol sprays, conserving water in the house) can save the world, and places equal amounts of responsibility on the consumer as it does large corporations. As a result, the Zen-X aesthetic promotes living a sustainable and environmentally-conscious lifestyle, and holding yourself accountable to doing everything within your power to uphold these ideals.

This aesthetic also prioritizes mental, physical and spiritual wellness, with an emphasis on yoga, meditation, and Buddhism. This is often approached through a secular lens, with a mindset of using these practices for wellness purposes only, but it is common to see people within this trend have spiritual belief systems as well. This ties into the concept of Zen, as well as Japanese Zen gardens, which are carefully landscaped gardens meant to aid in meditation.
