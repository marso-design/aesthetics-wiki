# Girl Internet

**Girl Internet** is an online phenomenon originating on 2020s TikTok that describes various users' identification as a "girl," with that word being used as phrases such as "I'm just a girl," "girl dinner," "girls that x," etc. While "Girl Internet" is not an aesthetic in itself, this culture is highly related to [Coquette](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Coquette "Coquette"), ["Blank" Girl](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Girl), and [feminine TikTok microtrend](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Microtrend) aesthetics.

The rise in feminine aesthetics becoming more popularized and overtly girlish can be related to the celebration of being a girl. Mainstream fashion and internet trends include bows, lace, Kawaii trinkets, etc., making Girl Internet be a factor of the visual look of the 2020s, even in offline culture.

This phenomenon is documented on multiple platforms, such as [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/09/10/style/girls-internet-gender.html), with various people on the internet having both positive and negative reception to the trend.

Critics point out how the usage of the term is often linked to negative stereotypes that reinforce gender roles. For example, "Girl Math" is a phrase that women who participate in this trend use to express their self-admittedly illogical spending habits and lack of mathematical skill, which can be connected to the stereotype that women are consumerist and unintelligent.
