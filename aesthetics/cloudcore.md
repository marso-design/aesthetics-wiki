# Cloudcore

**Cloudcore** is an aesthetic centered on the visual and emotional qualities of clouds and open skies. Its imagery ranges from unedited golden hour photography that captures the natural drama of cumulus formations, to heavily manipulated digital compositions layered with sparkle effects, pastel filters, kawaii illustrations, and motivational text overlays. What unites these strains is a shared investment in the sky as a site of softness, wonder, and emotional release.

Although sky photography has been a fixture of internet aesthetics since the early Tumblr era, Cloudcore became a distinct and named aesthetic primarily between 2019 and 2022, driven by Pinterest boards, TikTok wallpaper accounts, and aesthetic blogs. During this period, the word "aesthetic" itself became an aesthetic, defined in this context as a sensibility characterized by soft editing, dreamy color palettes, and an atmosphere of gentle aspiration. Cloudcore was one of the purest expressions of that moment.

## History

Sky photography has circulated on image-sharing platforms since the early 2010s, frequently tagged across [Kawaii](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Kawaii "Kawaii"), [Softie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Softie "Softie"), [Angelcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Angelcore "Angelcore"), and [Ethereal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Ethereal "Ethereal") blogs without a unifying name. The cloud as a motif appeared in Tumblr's soft aesthetic communities long before the specific hashtag "Cloudcore" originated

The naming and consolidation of Cloudcore as a distinct aesthetic is largely a product of TikTok's wallpaper and aesthetic communities from around 2019 onward. As short-form video made aesthetic content more shareable and searchable, the tag "cloudcore" became a practical organizational tool for a body of imagery that already existed. Pinterest boards, Etsy shops selling cloudcore wallpaper packs, and Tumblr aesthetic blogs all adopted the name simultaneously.

The peak of Cloudcore's cultural saturation (roughly 2020 to 2022) coincided with a broader moment in which "aesthetic" content was among the most shared material on social media platforms. The lo-fi and bedroom pop musical context of the period (discussed further below) was inseparable from the visual aesthetic.

## Visuals

### Photography

Cloudcore is based around sky and cloud imagery captured at moments of maximum natural drama and color: sunrise and sunset, when light refracts through cloud formations to produce pinks, oranges, lavenders, and deep blues. Golden hour shots of cumulus clouds against blue skies are among the most circulated images in the aesthetic, valued for their scale and the sense of vastness they convey.

Cloud formations themselves are a subject of attention independent of color. Particularly dramatic or unusual cloud shapes, like towering cumulonimbus columns, low stratus layers, or wispy cirrus streaks, circulate widely within the aesthetic. The appeal is partly natural wonder and partly the way unusual formations invite imaginative projection.

* Cumulus and cumulonimbus clouds against vivid blue skies
* Sunset and sunrise skies in pink, orange, coral, and lavender
* Low-angle shots emphasizing the scale of cloud formations
* Reflections of cloud-filled skies in water
* Cloud collages and multi-image grid compositions

### Digital Editing

A specific and highly recognizable subgenre of Cloudcore involves heavy digital manipulation of sky photographs. This editing style, dominant during the 2019–2022 peak, applies sparkle and glitter overlays, iridescent light leaks, and soft glow effects to cloud photographs, transforming documentary sky images into something closer to fantasy illustration. The ocean and cloud photographs overlaid with dense multicolored sparkle effects are among the most iconic images of this strain.

[Kawaii](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Kawaii "Kawaii")-influenced illustration is another editing mode: cloud formations are traced or annotated with hand-drawn or digitally illustrated faces, animal shapes (most commonly cats or bunnies), stars, and other cute motifs, creating a hybrid of photography and illustration. These images circulate heavily on Pinterest.

Motivational quote typography, typically in small, lowercase serif or monospace fonts, placed quietly within the negative space of a sky photograph, is a third editing convention associated with the aesthetic's peak moment. These overlays are restrained rather than heavy.

Common editing elements include:

* Glitter and sparkle overlays in iridescent, white, and multicolored finishes
* Soft pastel color grading (cooler tones for daytime, warmer pinks for sunset)
* Light leaks and lens flare effects
* Hand-drawn or digital kawaii illustrations overlaid on cloud formations
* Small lowercase motivational or reflective text overlays
* RGB chromatic aberration effects on cloud edges

### Color Palette

* Dusty lavender and periwinkle
* Rose pink and blush
* Baby blue and teal
* Iridescent white and silver
* Warm golden orange (sunset strain)
* Deep midnight blue (night sky strain)

## Music

Music is an unusually important component of Cloudcore's cultural context, because the aesthetic's peak coincided precisely with the surge in popularity of lo-fi and bedroom pop. Lo-fi hip-hop, which broadened in the late 2010s to encompass slow and cozy sample-based instrumental tracks associated with "study" and "chill" YouTube channels, became heavily associated with this aesthetic (see also [lo-fi art](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Lo-fi_Art "Lo-fi Art")).

### Songs

* "She Share Story (for Spica)" by Linear Waves
* "Imagine" by Lukrembo
* "Death Bed (Coffee for Your Head)" by Powfu feat. beabadoobee
* "Snowfall" by Øneheart x reidenshi
* "Everything Goes On" by Porter Robinson
* "Wish You Were Here" (lo-fi covers)

## Media

Cloud and sky imagery as a visual motif appears across a range of media associated with the aesthetic's sensibility, though Cloudcore itself is primarily a social media aesthetic without strong direct media adaptations.

### Film

* *The Secret Life of Walter Mitty* (2013)
* Studio Ghibli films, especially *Castle in the Sky* (1986) and *Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind* (1984), for their iconic cloud and sky sequences
