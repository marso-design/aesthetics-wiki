# Otome

**Otome** (乙女) is a Japanese fashion and lifestyle that emerged in the late 1970s. It is characterized by casual, feminine clothing with youthful, vintage-inspired visuals and an emphasis on traditional handicrafts and hobbies. The style is widely regarded as one of the predecessors of [Lolita](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Lolita "Lolita") fashion. While the term "otome" was once solely used as name for the aesthetic in Japan, its meaning later shifted due to an increasing association with female-oriented otaku culture, diluting the association with fashion.

## History

One of the most influential forces behind this aesthetic was the Japanese fashion magazine Olive, first published by Magazine House in 1982. Unlike conventional fashion magazines of the period, Olive encouraged its audience to create personal wardrobes through layering, sewing, collecting vintage accessories, and appreciating everyday beauty. The magazine's readers became known as Olive girls (Olive少女), and their style valued individuality, quiet sophistication, and intellectual curiosity over mainstream fashion.

Throughout the 1980s, Olive frequently featured romantic and natural styles that complemented brands such as Pink House, founded by designer Kaneko Isao in 1973, helping popularize a broader "girlhood" aesthetic that emphasized softness and personal expression. Although the brand predated Olive, its designs resonated strongly with readers seeking a nostalgic, storybook-inspired wardrobe. Pink House offers clothing that evoked rural Europe, childhood memories, and handmade craftsmanship rather than contemporary fashion trends.

The influence of Otome fashion extended well beyond the magazine's original publication years. Its aesthetic became one of the foundations for several later Japanese fashion movements, including [Lolita](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Lolita "Lolita") and [Mori Kei](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Mori_Kei "Mori Kei"), all of which inherited elements of romantic silhouettes, vintage inspiration, and an appreciation for craftsmanship. Even after Olive ceased publication in 2003, its aesthetic continued to inspire designers, artists, and fashion enthusiasts both within Japan and internationally.

## Fashion

Otome outfits typically multiple: a blouse, dress or skirt, vest/cardigan, and an underskirt. While the fabrics are usually lightweight, the many layers, long lengths, and decorative details make outfits fairly heavy and warm in summer. Handmade brooches are a common accessory. Mixing patterns and contrasting elements is a signature of Otome fashion. Floral, gingham, and polka dots are often layered together, and cute outfits may be paired with unexpected items like a sukajan or sneakers. Although this "mismatched" look is iconic, coordinated outfits are equally acceptable and are often easier for beginners. Otome skirts and dresses are generally much longer than those in Lolita, sometimes reaching the floor on shorter wearers. Petticoats are optional, but full Lolita petticoats create an unnatural silhouette. If worn, a soft, modest petticoat matching the skirt's length works best.
