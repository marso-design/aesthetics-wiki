# Dark Culture

**Dark Culture** (German: *Schwarze Szene*), or the **Dark Alternative Scene**, is a comprehensive umbrella term for a networked community of overlapping but historically distinct subcultures. Primarily emergent in Germany, the scene integrates diverse stylistic pillars (ranging from [Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Goth "Goth") and [Industrial](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Industrial "Industrial") to [Bondage](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Bondage "Bondage")/Fetish culture) through a shared monochromatic style representing non-conformity and communal social infrastructure. Although these subcultures are often mistakenly referred to simply as "[Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Goth "Goth")" in English-speaking contexts, Dark Culture encompasses a spectrum of [alternative](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Alternative "Alternative") styles unified by the symbolic and aesthetic use of the color black.

Originating in the late-1980s from the Dark Wave and Indie music scenes in Berlin, Dark Culture evolved into a "scene of scenes" rather than a homogenous group. The movement prioritizes individualism and the recontextualization of gothic literary motifs, utilizing aesthetics that focus on gravity, melancholy, and mysticism.

## History

### The "Schwarze Szene" Origins

The term *Schwarze Szene* (Black Scene) first appeared in Berlin in the late-1980s as the boundaries between [Post-Punk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Post-Punk "Post-Punk"), [Gothic Rock](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Goth "Goth"), and the early [Industrial](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Industrial "Industrial") scene began to blur. Magazines like Zillo popularized the term to target a reader base that shared an interest in dark alternative music and fashion regardless of specific genre loyalty. By the 1990s, the term was established in Germany as a sociological marker for this heterogeneous community.

Unlike traditional subcultures that require strict adherence to one sound, Dark Culture functions as a broad social network. This dynamic allowed diverse groups (from the utilitarian [Rivetheads](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Rivethead "Rivethead") to the historically-minded [Victorian Goths](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Victorian_Goth "Victorian Goth")) to coexist in the same nightclubs and festivals. The scene is unified by the consistent use of synthetic and natural high-contrast materials, including PVC, latex, leather, and velvet, as a symbol of rejection toward mainstream culture.

### Global Variations

While the term is most established in German-speaking countries, similar concepts exist globally:

* **Cultura Oscura / Cultura Dark:** Used throughout Latin America and Spain to describe the broader dark alternative community.
* **Dark Scene:** The literal English translation, though "Alternative" or "Goth" are still more frequently (and sometimes inaccurately) used as catch-alls in the UK and US.

## Primary Subcultures

### [Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Goth "Goth")

Originating from the UK Post-Punk scene, Goth utilizes minor-key melodic structures and reverb-heavy production for its music. The fashion draws influences from [punk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Punk "Punk") and 19th-century [Victorian](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Victorian "Victorian") mourning attire. Within the Dark Culture umbrella, "Goth" refers specifically to those centered on Gothic Rock, [Deathrock](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Deathrock "Deathrock"), and Dark Wave.

### [Rivethead](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Rivethead "Rivethead")

Centered on the [Industrial](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Industrial "Industrial") and [Electronic Body Music](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Electronic_Body_Music "Electronic Body Music") (EBM) genres, the Rivethead subculture presents an aggressive and utilitarian contrast to the romanticism-influenced Goth subculture. The aesthetic is predicated on a utilitarian wardrobe consisting of M65 field jackets, tactical flight suits, and steel-toed combat boots.

### [Cybergoth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cybergoth "Cybergoth")

Originating as a fusion of [Rivethead](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Rivethead "Rivethead"), [Rave](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Rave "Rave"), and [Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Goth "Goth") culture in the late 1990s, [Cybergoth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cybergoth "Cybergoth") utilizes a high-contrast palette of black paired with neon accents. Primary accessories include "cyberlox" (corrugated plastic tubing hair extensions), gas masks, and technical materials like UV-reactive PVC and reflective high-visibility tape. Musically, it is tied to high-energy industrial genres like [Aggrotech](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Aggrotech "Aggrotech"), [EBM](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/EBM "EBM"), and [Futurepop](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Futurepop "Futurepop").

### [Victorian Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Victorian_Goth "Victorian Goth")

Victorian Goth prioritizes historical accuracy through the use of 19th-century silhouettes, specifically structured corsetry, crinolines, and silk velvet textiles. Drawing inspiration from the Gothic Revival, the aesthetic focuses on elegance and the macabre. This group often overlaps with the [Steampunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Steampunk "Steampunk") community.

### [Neofolk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Neofolk "Neofolk") & [Martial Industrial](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Martial_Industrial "Martial Industrial")

A traditionalist niche that utilizes acoustic instruments and historical themes to create a somber and sometimes nationalist atmosphere. This subculture is identified by its use of folk melodies and has attracted controversy due to the subversive application of fascist imagery.

### [Bondage](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Bondage "Bondage") & Fetish Culture

The Bondage/Fetish scene is historically and socially linked to Dark Culture, particularly within the German *Schwarze Szene*. While distinct due to its focus on sexual practices rather than music, the shared materials of PVC, latex, and leather creates a significant overlap in nightclubs and festivals.

### Gothic Metal

The early Gothic Metal scene is integrated into Dark Culture through shared fashion sensibilities and its crossover with Darkwave and Neoclassical music. It synthesizes the low-tuned distortion of Doom Metal with the operatic vocal structures of the Dark Alternative scene.

## Music

Dark Culture is musically diverse, united by a shared dark or "cold" atmosphere:

* [Post-Punk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Post-Punk "Post-Punk") & Gothic Rock: The guitar-driven genres that founded the Goth subculture.
* [Electronic Body Music](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Electronic_Body_Music "Electronic Body Music") (EBM): EBM is the rhythmic and structural foundation for the Rivethead subculture.
* Dark Wave, [Minimal Wave](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Minimal_Wave "Minimal Wave"), & Cold Wave: Synthesizer-heavy genres emphasizing atmosphere.
* [Neofolk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Neofolk "Neofolk"): Utilizing acoustic instruments to explore historical and traditional European themes.
* Neoclassical Dark Wave: Orchestral arrangements with a somber tone.

## Notable Festivals

* Wave-Gotik-Treffen (WGT): Held in Leipzig, Germany; the largest dark alternative festival in the world.
* M'era Luna: A major gathering in Hildesheim focused on the crossover between Goth, Industrial, and Metal.
* Whitby Goth Weekend: A biannual UK event celebrating the traditional and Victorian roots of the scene.

## See also

* [Aggrotech](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Aggrotech "Aggrotech"): A harder evolution of [Electronic Body Music](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Electronic_Body_Music "Electronic Body Music") and techno that became a primary genre of the [Cybergoth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cybergoth "Cybergoth") and Industrial club scenes in the early 2000s.
* [Berlin Techno Fashion](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Berlin_Techno_Fashion "Berlin Techno Fashion"): An utilitarian fashion style rooted in Berlin's electronic music scene that shares the emphasis on all-black attire, fetish-inspired motifs, and underground club heritage.
* [Dark Cabaret](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Cabaret "Dark Cabaret"): A genre that blends the aesthetics of German [Weimar-era cabaret](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Weimar_Cabaret "Weimar Cabaret") with Gothic Rock and Dark Wave, emphasizing theatricality and the macabre.
* [Dark Romanticism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Romanticism "Dark Romanticism"): A 19th-century literary and artistic movement that serves as a primary historical and philosophical foundation for the [Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Goth "Goth") subculture and [Victorian Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Victorian_Goth "Victorian Goth") aesthetics.
* [Electronic Body Music](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Electronic_Body_Music "Electronic Body Music") (EBM): A genre originating in the 1980s that provided the rhythmic "hard" musical backbone for the [Rivethead](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Rivethead "Rivethead") subculture.
* [Fetish Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Fetish_Goth "Fetish Goth"): A fashion style emphasizing materials like PVC, latex, and leather.
* [Leather Subculture](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Leather_Subculture "Leather Subculture"): A community historically connected to the LGBTQ+ community and BDSM scene, sharing social spaces and a focus on specialized attire and bodily autonomy.
* [Minimal Wave](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Minimal_Wave "Minimal Wave"): A stripped-back electronic genre from the late 1970s and 1980s that shares the "cold" atmosphere and DIY ethos of Cold Wave and Post-Punk.
* [New Beat](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/New_Beat "New Beat"): A late-1980s Belgian music scene that acted as a bridge between [Acid House](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Acid_House "Acid House") and Industrial music, significantly influencing the development of the European [rave](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Rave "Rave") scene.
* [New Romantic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/New_Romantic "New Romantic"): A flashy 1980s London-born subculture that shared the same club spaces as early Goths, influencing the scene's focus on elaborate fashion and dandyism.
* [Nu-Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Nu-Goth "Nu-Goth"): A modern adaptation of Goth fashion that simplifies traditional elements for a minimalist streetwear-adjacent aesthetic.
* [Post-Punk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Post-Punk "Post-Punk"): The foundational musical movement that birthed Gothic Rock and provided the initial artistic rejection of mainstream culture.
* [Steampunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Steampunk "Steampunk"): A genre of science fiction and fashion that frequently overlaps with [Victorian Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Victorian_Goth "Victorian Goth") due to shared historical inspirations and artisanal craftsmanship.
* [Vampire](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vampire "Vampire"): There is a niche subculture of self-proclaimed "real-life" vampires who engage in consensual blood-drinking or energy-feeding as a social and lifestyle activity, often frequenting the same events as the Dark Culture community.
