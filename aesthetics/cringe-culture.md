# Cringe Culture

[Sensitive Content Notice](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Aesthetics_Wiki:Content_Warning_Policy "Aesthetics Wiki:Content Warning Policy") ⚠️

The following article contains and discusses content that may be distressing to some readers.  
**Reason for Warning:** This page discusses online harassment, bullying, and cyberbullying, as well as communities that have been affected by those behaviors.

Foundational Concept 🏛️

This article describes a **broad cultural concept**, not a specific visual aesthetic. It serves as an educational hub to explain ideas that influence many other styles on this wiki.

**Cringe Culture** is an online phenomenon, prominent in the 2010s, where individuals or communities are subjected to mockery and harassment for engaging in behavior deemed "cringey" or socially awkward. This culture primarily targets harmless expressions of individuality, such as niche hobbies, fan-related activities, and [alternative](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Alternative "Alternative") fashion styles. The term "cringe" evolved from its original meaning of recoiling in embarrassment to become a pejorative label for anything outside of perceived mainstream norms. While the phenomenon was pervasive, the 2020s saw a significant backlash against it, with a movement to reclaim "cringe" as a form of authentic self-expression, particularly within the development of new internet aesthetics.

## History and Impact on Subcultures

Cringe culture largely took shape in the early to mid-2010s on platforms like YouTube, Reddit, and Tumblr. Its rise was fueled by "cringe compilations," which were video montages mocking individuals, and the commentary of internet personalities who built their brands on ridiculing others. The targets were often members of visible and passionate online subcultures, including furries, bronies, cosplayers, and fans of media like *Undertale* and *Five Nights at Freddy's*.

This constant social policing had a chilling effect on creative and alternative expression online. Many individuals, particularly young people, felt pressured to suppress their interests and conform to mainstream tastes to avoid becoming a target of online harassment. This environment created a cultural tension that would directly lead to the development of aesthetics that actively rebelled against the pressure to be "cool" or socially acceptable.

## Reclaiming Cringe: The Rise of Counter-Aesthetics

By the late 2010s and early 2020s, a counter-movement emerged, with the slogan of "cringe culture is dead." This sentiment became a core tenet of several new internet aesthetics that celebrated the very things cringe culture had once mocked: bright colors, overt enthusiasm, "childish" interests, and unapologetic fandom. These aesthetics reclaimed "cringe" as a badge of honor, framing it as a form of freedom and authenticity.

* **[Scenecore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scenecore "Scenecore") and [Glitchcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Glitchcore "Glitchcore"):** These aesthetics directly revive and celebrate the "cringey" visual language of the late 2000s and early 2010s internet, including bright neon colors, chaotic layouts, and early digital art styles. They embrace a maximalist and often intentionally overwhelming sensory experience as a direct defiance of the muted, "tasteful" minimalism that was often positioned against "cringe."
* **[Animecore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Animecore "Animecore"):** While interest in anime was often a target of cringe culture, Animecore represents an unrestrained celebration of the medium. It incorporates 2000s anime motifs into everyday fashion and online identity without the shame or irony that was previously used as a defense mechanism against mockery.
* **[Alt TikTok](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Alt_Kid "Alt Kid"):** The alternative subculture that flourished on TikTok in the early 2020s was built on a foundation of rejecting cringe culture. It created a space where various alternative styles could coexist and thrive. A central value of this community was the acceptance of diverse and unconventional forms of self-expression, directly opposing the conformity demanded by cringe culture.
