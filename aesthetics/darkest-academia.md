_Also known as: Gothic Academia_

**Darkest Academia** (also known as Gothic Academia) is an internet aesthetic that revolves around classic literature, death, supernatural and a passion for knowledge and learning secret, "forbidden" knowledge. The fashion of the aesthetic is very close to classical [Gothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Gothic "Gothic") style and 19th century [Victorian](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Victorian "Victorian") mourning attire, combined with the style of [Dark Academia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Academia "Dark Academia") aesthetics. Visually, the aesthetic is defined by a palette of utterly dark tones and elements such as **candlelight**, **skulls**, **ink**, **old manuscripts**, **supernatural**, **books** and historical European architecture like [**Gothic architecture**](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Gothic_Architecture "Gothic Architecture") together with elements of **Surrealism**.

Unlike the famous [Dark Academia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Academia "Dark Academia"), Darkest Academia is characterised by significantly darker tones with the heavy influence of [Victorian Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Victorian_Goth "Victorian Goth") aesthetic, instead of the "coffee and books" motif. It is usually associated with older times of history and romanticises supernatural and occult, study of magic and mystery instead of classical academic studies. [Renaissance](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Renaissance "Renaissance"), [Baroque](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Baroque "Baroque") and [Surrealism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Surrealism "Surrealism") are the most often associated with Gothic Academia movements of art.

The Gothic academia is usually associated with 16-18 centuries, with books like *Faust* being one of the core examples. One of the main concepts of aesthetic - the border of study of real science and occult magic - has strongest motifs in 16th century literature like *Magia Naturalis* or *De Miraculis Mortuorum*. Works of later authors like H.P. Lovecraft also partially fall into the aesthetic of Gothic Academia, as almost all of them posses relevant elements of forbidden knowledge, mystery, occultism and learning.

Darkest Academia is often incorrectly seen as a subtype of Dark Academia, with many people associating themselves with Dark Academia, although they in fact adore Gothic Academia's aesthetic. It originated slightly earlier, becoming popular and recognised primarily at the same time as [Vampirecore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vampirecore "Vampirecore"), in early 00's, instead of mid 10's.

## Setting

Unlike other styles of academia, Darkest Academia more focuses on individual life of solo-scientists, hermits or very closed off from the outside world, sometimes suffering from mental disorders, uncovering and encountering mysteries of science and magic. The aesthetic is often set around teachers and professors instead of students, though a more typical academia life is also presented. Morals and motifs can range from "Universe is mysterious and magical" (like in *Faust*) to "Universe is incomprehensible and vast" (like *Lovecraft*). Motifs of anatomy, biology and plague, customary to [Plaguecore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Plaguecore "Plaguecore"), can also be found as a part of Gothic Academy.

## Fashion

The fashion of Gothic academia is very similar to [Vampire](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vampire "Vampire") and [Victorian Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Victorian_Goth "Victorian Goth"), sometimes with more archaic elements. It is usually defined by elegant, dark clothes customary to late medieval/early Renaissance scientists, like dark gothic capes, coats, high collars, and draped hoods. The look can range from rich and aristocratic to modest and humble, not necessarily connected to a certain time period.

## Media

Although Gothic Academia does not posses a big separate genre of books, many old authors and modern composers have several works that formed the basis of the aesthetic. Some listed books include direct descriptions of necromancy, demonology, or the border of magic and real science, written by early Renaissance authors. The music is often measured and calm, building the atmosphere of slow and mysterious studies, often inspired by Gothic literature, sometimes with haunting choir and whispers on the background.
