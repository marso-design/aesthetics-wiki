# Claymorphism

**Claymorphism** is a user interface design trend that focuses on 3D visuals mimicking the texture of clay, often achieved through smooth, blob-like shapes, soft shading, and pastel colors. This creates a style that feels welcoming and accessible to general audiences, contrasting with the "cold" nature of traditional [Flat Design](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Flat_Design "Flat Design").

Claymorphism is commonly used in illustrations for advertising, often depicting people and everyday scenarios. It is seen as a natural evolution of specific flat design trends (often compared to [Corporate Memphis](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Corporate_Memphis "Corporate Memphis")), taking the style into a new dimension with a greater level of detail and tactility. The style is characterized by "fluffy" or "inflated" elements that resemble stuffed toys or balloons, differentiating layout elements as discrete, modeled 3D surfaces.

The term "Claymorphism" was coined in 2021 by Michał Malewicz as a portmanteau of "clay" and "[skeuomorphism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Skeuomorphism "Skeuomorphism")".

## History

### Precursors

The roots of Claymorphism can be traced to the need for "tactility" in user interfaces following the dominance of [Flat Design](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Flat_Design "Flat Design") in the 2010s. While [Neumorphism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Neumorphism "Neumorphism") attempted to solve this by extruding shapes from the background, it suffered from accessibility issues due to low contrast. Claymorphism emerged as a solution that retained the tactile, 3D appeal of Neumorphism but introduced "strong color cues" and distinct, floating layers to improve usability.

Artistically, the style draws inspiration from stop-motion animation (such as *Wallace and Gromit*) and the "Toy Faces" NFT series by artist Amrit Pal Singh, which popularized the look of friendly non-photorealistic 3D avatars.

### Emergence (2021–Present)

The trend was formally named and categorized in 2021. It quickly gained mainstream traction as a way to "humanize" tech branding. Major implementations include the 2023 rebranding of Android (updating the "Bugdroid" mascot to a 3D clay style) and Reddit's 3D update to their "Snoo" mascot. It also features heavily in Windows 11's "Fluency" emoji set, which replaced 2D flat icons with 3D clay-textured versions.

## Visuals

Claymorphism is defined by four visual traits that distinguish it from standard 3D rendering:

* **Discrete Modeled Surfaces:** unlike Neumorphism, where elements are extruded from the background, Claymorphic elements float *on top* of the background. They use outer shadows to show separation and subtle inner shadows to create the illusion of volume and thickness.
* **"Inflated" Geometry:** Objects often look like they have been inflated with air or molded from Play-Doh. They lack sharp edges, favoring "chunky," softly sculpted forms.
* **Strong Color Cues:** The style avoids the monochrome limitations of Neumorphism. It uses vivid pastel or bright colors to differentiate buttons and cards, ensuring that interactive elements are clearly visible and accessible.
* **Minimal Typography:** To balance the "whimsical" nature of the clay visuals, typography is usually kept clean, bold, and minimal to ensure the interface remains usable and professional.

## Comparison of Post-Flat Aesthetics

While Neumorphism, Glassmorphism, and Claymorphism all share roots in [Minimalism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Minimalism "Minimalism") and soft UI, they rely on distinct visual metaphors and lighting physics.

| **Feature** | **[Neumorphism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Neumorphism "Neumorphism")** | **[Glassmorphism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Glassmorphism "Glassmorphism")** | ****Claymorphism**** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Material Metaphor** | Extruded Plastic / Embossed Paper | Frosted Glass / Acrylic | 3D Clay / Play-Doh / Inflatables |
| **Relation to Background** | Elements appear to be part of the same surface, extruded upwards. | Elements float *above* the background, separating the UI from the wallpaper. | Elements float *on top* of the background as distinct, solid 3D objects. |
| **Lighting & Shadows** | Requires one light shadow (top-left) and one dark shadow (bottom-right) to create volume. | Relies on background blur and light borders; shadows are subtle and used only for depth. | Uses deep outer shadows for float, and inner shadows to create "puffiness." |
| **Opacity** | Elements are solid and usually monochromatic (same color as background). | Elements are semi-transparent; background context is visible. | Elements are solid, typically using vibrant pastel colors. |
| **Edges** | Soft, blended edges (no borders). | Light, semi-transparent white borders (strokes). | Soft, rounded edges with distinct separation. |
