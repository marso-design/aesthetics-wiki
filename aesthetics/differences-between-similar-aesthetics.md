# Differences Between Similar Aesthetics

Because many aesthetics share many similarities in inspiration, key visuals, and connotations, many of them often get confused for each other, which is exacerbated through the use of mass-tagging, or tagging images with aesthetic names that do not necessarily fit what the image is trying to depict, for the sake of having greater reach.

This page describes the differences between many aesthetics that are commonly confused for each other in a table and a visual comparison with the moodboards from [Find Aesthetic By Image](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Find_Aesthetic_by_Image "Find Aesthetic by Image").

## Preppy vs. vs. Light Academia vs. Old Money

|  | **Location and Date of Origin** | **How is it expressed** | **Visuals Unique to This Specific Aesthetic** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Preppy](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Preppy "Preppy") | early 20th century New England (U.S.A), in real life | [Subcultural Lifestyle](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Subculture) | - Overt references to Americana and locations such as Cape Cod and Martha's Vineyard, as well as connections to the nation's history, such as being related to a colonist on the Mayflower. - As this is a subculture, this is more comprehensive and is generally not able to be mimicked, as it requires having specific knowledge, social circles, and participation in activities/hobbies inaccessible to those who are not wealthy |
| [Light Academia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Light_Academia "Light Academia") | 2016 on Tumblr | [Internet Aesthetic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Internet_aesthetic) | - A campus/academic setting is mandatory, with there being a specific subject being studied present in the visuals, such as in annotated books - Signifiers of wealth are not necessary in this aesthetic. "Participating" in this aesthetic can be done through public libraries, thrifted clothing, and other free/low cost activities, while the other two involve expensive goods. |
| [Old Money](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Old_Money "Old Money") | approximately 2020 on TikTok | [Internet Aesthetic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Internet_aesthetic) | - This trend is new and often involves non-wealthy people imagining the life of such a person, leading to those who are more knowledgeable in fashion pointing out inaccuracies or laughing at the people idolizing it. - Generally more leisure-based with a European setting, featuring settings such as the French riviera  - There is a greater emphasis on luxury and exclusive brands. While Preppy does involve pricier brands such as Ralph Lauren and Brooks Brothers, those can be purchased at a low price point secondhand. This aesthetic, meanwhile, involves haute couture fittings from Parisian fashion houses, something that the upper middle class of Preppy would not do. |
| **Similarities Amongst All** | | | |
| All three aesthetics utilize signifiers of wealth and achievement, specifically in a European and American context, emphasizing tradition and being descended from wealth. Visually, all three feature grand, 19th century buildings, such as those of Paris and/or prestigious colleges, feature clothing that is a more stylish interpretation of business wear, and utilize naturalistic lighting and framing. All three also imply that the "character" living in the aesthetic has a world-class education and is cultured in European art and literature. | | | |

## Morute vs. Medicalcore vs. Traumacore vs. Gloomy Coquette vs. Mochette

|  | **Location and Date of Origin** | **How is it expressed** | **Visuals Unique to the Aesthetic** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Morute](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Morute "Morute") | Approximately 2012 on Tumblr | [Internet Aesthetic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Internet_Aesthetics "Category:Internet Aesthetics") (w/ music) | - Emphasizes religious imagery and guilt more heavily than these other aesthetics, with crosses, images of churches, and angels. - Heavily tied to the music of Nicole Dollanganger, while the other aesthetics are not connected with this artist.  - This is the only aesthetic to reference serial killers and true crime. |
| [Traumacore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Traumacore "Traumacore") | Approximately 2017 on Tumblr | Internet Aesthetic (w/o fashion) | - This aesthetic is defined by the use of edits, which include short snippets of bright red text overlaid on top of images and digital stickers. While the other aesthetics all utilize digital stickers to some degree, this aesthetic has this as the main, rather than minor, imagery. - This is the only aesthetic to include more contemporary imagery of girlhood, such as cartoon and Sanrio mascot imagery.  - There is no fashion component in this aesthetic. Instead, it relies entirely on photography of interiors and landscape with the edited components. |
| [Medicalcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Medicalcore "Medicalcore") | Approximately 2017 on Tumblr | Internet Aesthetic | - Visuals exclusively relate to the setting of hospitals, making this more narrow than other aesthetics, which do include hospitals, but also emphasize other settings - Many of the images cross over with [Yami Kawaii](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Yami_Kawaii "Yami Kawaii") fashion and products. For example, syringe necklaces, nurse-themed outfits, and pixel art are referenced here, but in the other aesthetics, there are no ties to Japanese fashion. |
| [Gloomy Coquette](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Gloomy_Coquette "Gloomy Coquette") | Approximately 2023 on TikTok | Internet Aesthetic | - This aesthetic is a subgenre of [Dollette](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dollette "Dollette"), including the fashion, Lana del Rey's music, and Diet Coke. The other aesthetics evolved independently of that aesthetic. - All images in this aesthetic involve a pale, washed-out filter over every image, while the others use it more sparingly.  - This aesthetic is the least dark, and trauma may not even be referenced. Instead, users often have a darker romantic and sexual motif, such as referencing a crush on a teacher. |
| [Mochette](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Mochette "Mochette") | Approximately 2025 on Tumblr | Internet Aesthetic | - This aesthetic involves more nature-based imagery, such as mold, dirt, and forests. While Morute does reference bugs and decay in the human body, this is more based on death, rather than nature. - Generally, the photos emphasize texture more heavily than the other aesthetics. |
| **Similarities Amongst All** | | | |
| All aesthetics in this table juxtapose imagery associated with girlhood with motifs that most would find distressing, such as violence, trauma, and evil. The people within these aesthetic communities often utilize the aesthetic to create community and express their past traumas, such as self-harm, sexual violence, etc. | | | |

## Fairy Kei vs. Yume Kawaii vs. Jojifuku vs. Cutecore

|  | **Location and Date of Origin** | **How is it expressed** | **Visuals Unique to This Specific Aesthetic** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Fairy Kei](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Fairy_Kei "Fairy Kei") | Japan. Unknown date of origin, but popularized in the early 2010s. | Fashion Style | - What distinguishes Fairy Kei from Jojifuku is the brands. Rather than utilizing children's clothes, these brands designed for adults, taking the sizing and silhouette into account. -There is more explicitly 1980s inspiration, with My Little Pony toys, Barbies, and 80s patterns being incorporated.  - The fabrics used in this aesthetic are more delicate. Tulle, tights, tacked-on bows or toys, and intricate knit sweaters are common, as the intent is for alternative fashion, and there is no fear of snagging or washing like with children's clothes. This is also influenced by the popularity of Cult Party and Mori Kei fashions.  - The makeup involves curled dyed hair and Igari "crying" makeup, which was trendy at that time. |
| [Yume Kawaii](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Yume_Kawaii "Yume Kawaii") | Japan. Unknown date of origin, but popularized in the late 2000s. | Design Aesthetic | - This is the broadest aesthetic, with not only fashion, but also illustration and product and interior design. |
| [Jojifuku](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Jojifuku "Jojifuku") | approximately 2020 on TikTok, but utilizes clothing from 2000s Japan | Fashion Style | - As discussed in the Fairy Kei section, this aesthetic's look depends on using children's clothing, which utilizes more washable fabrics such as denim and jersey (tee shirt) material. - Construction-wise, children's clothing is optimized for running around, so there are no blouses, long peignoirs, or platform shoes like with Fairy Kei.  - The hair and makeup is based on the look of children, with natural dark hair in low pigtails and minimal/no makeup. |
| [Cutecore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cutecore "Cutecore") | approximately 2021 on TikTok and Tumblr, created by a non-Japanese person | [Internet Aesthetic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Internet_Aesthetics "Category:Internet Aesthetics") | - This aesthetic combines Jojifuku clothing and Kawaii items with noticeably creepy visuals, such as edits with blood, imagery of gore, and horror figures. - Much of the culture and visuals reference the Otaku/weeaboo NEET culture tied to 2chan and 4chan. For example, there are visual references to dirty fast food packaging, anime body pillows, and an obsessive amount of merchandise of one specific character. Users also often discuss poor mental health and utilize internet typing quirks originating from that circle, such as Kaomoji. |
| **Similarities Amongst All** | | | |
| These aesthetics all utilize a bright pastel color palette and Kawaii imagery more associated with children, such as mascots, sweets, and toys. | | | |

## French Girl vs. Pink Parisian vs. French Girly vs. Messy French It Girl

|  | **Location and Date of Origin** | **How is it expressed** | **Visuals Unique to This Specific Aesthetic** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [French Girl](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/French_Girl "French Girl") | 1950s, with the look being trends within France, and American cosmetic and fashion companies tropifying it in their marketing | Marketing and journalism phrase and Lifestyle | - What is dubbed "French Girl" is often arbitrary and used to elevate relatively banal lifestyle, cosmetic, and style choices which would not be noticeably different from other nations' trends. - The color palette here is generally brighter and more varied than the below aesthetics, incorporating bright red, yellow, and blue |
| [Pink Parisian](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Pink_Parisian "Pink Parisian") | mid-2010s Tumblr | Internet Aesthetic | -This aesthetic is more escapist and vintage-inspired, with images of vintage Paris, ballet, and Old Hollywood rather than modern women. - The community and visuals often focus on Audrey Hepburn, with GIFs of her movie scenes, snippets from interviews, etc.  - Shared with French Girly, this aesthetic's color palette is solely pink x black x white. |
| [French Girly](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/French_Girly "French Girly") | late 2010s Japanese Instagram | Internet Aesthetic | - Fashion-wise, this aesthetic incorporates girly trends that are more kawaii and Japanese than the West would design. For example, flared mini skirts, lacy bishop sleeves, and bejeweled headbands are in this aesthetic, rather than little black dresses and skinny jeans. - Similarly, the visuals here may occasionally reference childhood visuals, such as teddy bears, Tokyo Disneyland (a status symbol in Japan), and Sanrio characters. |
| [Messy French Girl](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Messy_French_Girl "Messy French Girl") | early 2020s TikTok | Internet Aesthetic | - The aesthetic focuses mostly on a more hedonistic lifestyle, involving partying, getting drunk, doing party drugs, and having casual sex, contrasting the more elegant and subdued stereotype of French women in the other aesthetics. - Visually, to convey the more thrill-seeking image, the pictures are visually darker, are in blurrier quality, and do not shy away from city grime. |
| **Similarities Amongst All** | | | |
| These aesthetics are all romanticized images of women in France, who the rest of the world perceive as stylish and "effortless." All four include visuals that describe deriving pleasure from sensory motifs, such as from perfume, food and wine, and quality fashion goods, which are aspects of French culture that set them apart from other cultures. Part of the French stereotype is that they are more romantic, which is reflected in all of these aesthetics with allusions to romantic success, whether in articles about dating like a French woman with French Girl, or imagery such as lipstick kisses, rose bouquets, and fancy lingerie in the internet aesthetics. Of course, all utilize imagery of Paris, including Haussmann boulevards, the Eiffel Tower, luxury French fashion houses, and cafe tables on the streets. | | | |

## Quality Tumblr vs. VSCO Girl vs. TikTok Preppy

|  | **Location and Date of Origin** | **How is it expressed** | **Visuals Unique to This Specific Aesthetic** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Quality Tumblr](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Quality_Tumblr "Quality Tumblr") | early 2010s Tumblr | Internet Aesthetic/Subculture | - Most of those photos involve holding up a snack, book, or other product to the camera with a blurry background, whereas the other photos do not follow this image format. - This aesthetic emphasizes snack foods more than the other aesthetics, with images of neon gummies, pizza, doughnuts, etc. However, TikTok preppy does reference Crumbl cookies, but to a lesser extent.  - The brands in this aesthetic are: Eos (lip balm), any fancy bottled water, Victoria's Secret (clothing), and Converse (sneakers) |
| [VSCO Girl](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/VSCO_Girl "VSCO Girl") | late 2010s VSCO | Internet Aesthetic/Subculture | - This aesthetic is the only one with an environmentalist message, with slogans such as "Save the turtles" and the use of reusable straws (later influencing the use of Stanley cups in TikTok Preppy.) - The brands in this aesthetic are: Carmex (lip balm), Hydro Flask (reusable water bottle), Vineyard Vines (clothing), and Vans (sneakers) |
| [TikTok Preppy](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/TikTok_Preppy "TikTok Preppy") | 2020s TikTok | Internet Aesthetic/Subculture | - Because this aesthetic is on TikTok, the format is in video form. Often, the videos would be of girls and women in this aesthetic dancing, which is unique to this aesthetic. - This aesthetic emphasizes skin care in a much greater extent than the other aesthetics, with references to retinol, acidic serums, and at least five-step routines.  - The brands in this aesthetic are: Glossier (lip balm), Stanley (water bottles), Aviator Nation (clothing), and Puma (sneakers) |
| **Similarities Amongst All** | | | |
| All three aesthetics share the same demographic of American teenaged girls in the upper-middle class, typically white, who can afford to adopt the latest trends. The "personality" of these aesthetics are upbeat, often associated with extroversion and being a "popular girl." The aesthetic is based on reality and daily lifestyle, with emphasis on viral brands that signify belonging in that subculture. Mostly, the difference between these aesthetics is the brands and the year and platform the aesthetic is hosted on, with all being the same "type" of person, who grow out of the aesthetic with the end of trends and change in priorities with adulthood. | | | |

## Larme Kei vs. Himekaji vs. Dark Girly (Jirai Kei) vs. French Girly

|  | **Location and Date of Origin** | **How is it expressed** | **Visuals Unique to This Specific Aesthetic** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Larme Kei](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Larme_Kei "Larme Kei") | Coined by the Western J-fashion community in 2013 codifying a Japanese fashion magazine that focuses on trends within Girly fashion | Fashion style | - Most garments in general are less noticeably alternative and kawaii than Himekaji and Jirai, with one being able to find similar pieces in non-boutique brands. - Takes inspiration from sources not found in the other aesthetics, such as [vintage Americana](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vintage_Americana "Vintage Americana") as depicted in the film [Lolita](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Nymphet "Nymphet"), lingerie and BDSM, and [grunge](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Grunge "Grunge") fashion.  - "Crying makeup" with pink eyeshadow on the lower lid, but not the fake aegyosal or more extreme trends of Jirai Kei |
| [Himekaji](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Himekaji "Himekaji") | Coined by Western community in 2013 describing Japanese trends in the Gyaru community in 2008-2018. | Fashion style | - The primary neutral color in this aesthetic is brown and cream, rather than white and black - Heavier use of prints, specifically border prints that incorporate florals and sweet foods.  - The makeup here is a toned-down version of gyaru makeup that still incorporates exaggerated lashes, bleached hair, a more tan complexion, and thick eyeliner. |
| [Jirai Kei](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Jirai_Kei "Jirai Kei"), with the fashion being called "Dark Girly" | approximately 2020 | Subculture and Fashion Style | - This is the only fashion that has a personality stereotype and lifestyle connotation, including Cluster B personality disorders, self-harm, drug use, prostitution, [Wota](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Wota "Wota") culture, and partying. - The garments in this aesthetic are the only ones to feature obvious hardware, such as buckles, grommets, faux leather, gem buttons, and dangling charms.  - The color palette and "outfit formula" is more limited. While exceptions exist, the vast majority of the outfits are blouses paired with flared mini skirts in a bicolor color palette, typically pink, white, or black.  - "Pien" makeup including a fake drawn aegyosal and downturned "puppy" eyeliner |
| [French Girly](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/French_Girly "French Girly") | approximately 2020 on Instagram | Internet Aesthetic | - The fashion component incorporates more basics familiar with mainstream fashion for the workplace, such as boucle jackets, solid turtlenecks, and slimmer business casual skirts - This is explicitly luxury French-inspired, with elements of that lifestyle (images of patisserie sweets, Rococo home decor, typography, etc.) being present. In the fashion, berets, Breton stripes, and French phrases on tees can be seen.  - Rather than it being a purely fashion aesthetic, users here also photograph girly interiors, food, non-clothing items, etc. |
| **Similarities Amongst All** | | | |
| All four fashions are a part of the larger fashion of [Girly Kei](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Girly_Kei "Girly Kei") in Japan, which includes hyper-feminine details such as ribbons, lace, exaggerated collars, and the color pink, but it still retains an obviously adult audience with elements of sex appeal (hosiery, sheer cut-outs, and shorter skirts), and unlike other kawaii aesthetics, lacks neon pastels and kawaii illustration. All four aesthetics also share some brands, and like many other fashion styles, some pieces can be styled to fit all aesthetics. In the case of all except Himekaji, the styles utilize a black x pink color scheme. | | | |

## Himekaji vs. 2010s Soft Kawaii vs. Dollette vs. Shoujo Girl

|  | **Location and Date of Origin** | **How is it expressed** | **Visuals Unique to the Aesthetic** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Himekaji](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Himekaji "Himekaji") | Late 2000s, early 2010s Japan | Fashion Style | - The early 2010s version of this style is an evolution of Gyaru and uses heavy eyeliner, false lashes, colored contact lenses, tanned skin, curled dyed hair, and a liberal use of hairspray. - All pieces in this style originate from Japanese boutique brands, such as Ank Rouge, Liz Lisa, and Yumetenbo. These brands combine details that one cannot find in most clothing brands, such as detachable chains, novelty prints, giant bows, and excessive trims. |
| [2010s Soft Kawaii](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/2010s_Soft_Kawaii "2010s Soft Kawaii") | Approximately 2012 on Tumblr | [Internet Aesthetic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Internet_Aesthetics "Category:Internet Aesthetics") | - The fashion in this aesthetic takes a few pieces from Himekaji, but combines it with Korean and western pieces to create a new look. (Or may not even use Himekaji pieces at all.) Those pieces do not have over-the-top details, and the Himekaji piece would be the statement piece in the outfit. - The colors and patterns utilized in this aesthetic were more trendy during the time, including bright mint green, swiss dot, decoden, and stripes.  - As an internet aesthetic, it included images that Himekaji does not, such as of cute food, animals, mascots, etc. |
| [Dollette](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dollette "Dollette") | 2019 on TikTok, anglophone | Internet Aesthetic | - This aesthetic is not inspired at all by Japan, with the Shoujo Girl trend coming into being when some Dollette creators introduced Japanese fashion into the style - The fashion in this aesthetic is less noticeably cute, relying on details to convey the daintiness, rather than it being overtly present in the garment. For example, basic boot cut jeans paired with a lace-trimmed rib-knit camisole is the style, rather than more noticeably alternative pieces originating from Japanese boutiques. The clothes here originate from Brandy Melville, thrifted pieces, and American mall brands. |
| [Shoujo Girl](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Shoujo_Girl "Shoujo Girl") | 2021 on TikTok, anglophone | Fashion Style | - Unlike Himekaji, the style does not utilize Gyaru hair and makeup, instead using loose natural hair with bangs, untanned skin, and simple eye makeup that prioritizes a dainty look. - The style combines unbranded clothing, 2000s pieces from Japan not associated with Himekaji, and certain pieces (but not most) from the original Himekaji.  - The styling here incorporates 2020s TikTok trends, often linked with [Gen Z Maximalism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Gen_Z_Maximalism "Gen Z Maximalism"). For example, legwarmers, dresses over jeans, bags decorates with numerous keychains, and stacked jewelry are some tropes that are not present in the other aesthetics. |
| **Similarities Amongst All** | | | |
| All four fashions share common textures and colors such as cream, light pink, lace, and knit. Some common garments used in all aesthetics are frilly mini skirts, lace trimmed button-up tops, and cardigans. These create a cute, feminine appearance and its audience is all young women in their teens to twenties. | | | |
