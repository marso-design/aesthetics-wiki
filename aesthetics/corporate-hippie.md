# Corporate Hippie

_Also known as: Capitalist Hippie, Corporate Psychedelia, Psychsploitation, Hippie Exploitation_

**Corporate Hippie** (spelt '**Hippy**' in British English), also referred to as **Capitalist Hippie** or **Corporate Psychedelia**, was an aesthetic and marketing tactic prevalent from the Late [1960s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:1960s "Category:1960s") to Early [1970](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:1970s "Category:1970s")s that attempted to appropriate the [1960s counterculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterculture_of_the_1960s) and [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie") movement for corporate applications. The phenomenon emerged out of the mainstream popularity of [Flower Power](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Flower_Power "Flower Power"). This aesthetic bridged the gap from the [Pop Art](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Pop_Art "Pop Art") and [Psychedelia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Psychedelia "Psychedelia") of the late 1960s to the [Earth Tones](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Earth_Tones "Earth Tones") of the 1970s.

## History

### Cultural Origins (c. 1965-1967)

> *For more information see*: [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie"), [Psychedelia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Psychedelia "Psychedelia"), [Flower Power](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Flower_Power "Flower Power")

The [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie") and [psychedelic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Psychedelia "Psychedelia") movements began in the Mid-[1960s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:1960s "Category:1960s") (c, 1965-1967), promoting values such as anti-war, peace & love, recreational drug use, and rising to popularity largely in the [psychedelic music](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Psychedelia?so=search#Music) scene. The scene primarily emerged from the American West Coast psychedelic scene with the earliest recordings being psychsploitation releases such as Kim Fowley's "The Trip" (1965), which sought to exploit the growing counterculture for profit. The Hippie movement reached new heights with the beginning of the [Summer of Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_of_Love), which gave corporate entities incentives to appeal to the movement.

### Mainstream Adoption (1967-1973)

By 1967, [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie")-influenced fashion and music had begun to become widely recognized in the mainstream. This saw corporations attempting to appeal to the broader public by using visuals associated with [Psychedelia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Psychedelia "Psychedelia") and [Flower Power](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Flower_Power "Flower Power"), spawning **Corporate Hippie**. These attempts at appealing to the youth was in direct conflict with the [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie") subculture's distain towards the "establishment", **Corporate Hippie** being largely consumed by people who adopted [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie") style and fashion but had little connection with the subculture as a whole.

### Decline (Post-1973)

By 1970, the zeitgeist of the [1960s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:1960s "Category:1960s") and [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie") movement had begun to see decline, especially in the United States. This was largely due to the fact that the [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie") movement was no longer an underground subculture and had become mainstream, prominent motifs such as long hair or [psychedelic rock](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Psychedelia#Psychedelic_Rock) music being adopted by general public; this diminished the exclusiveness the [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie") subculture embodied. Events such as [Tate–LaBianca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate–LaBianca_murders) murders committed by [Charles Manson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Manson) and [his cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manson_Family) in August 1969 also tarnished the reputation of the [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie") subculture in the eyes of some Americans, further contributing to its decline.

By 1973, **Corporate Hippie** and the [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie") subculture as a whole largely fell out of mainstream popularity, replaced by the [Earth Tones](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Earth_Tones "Earth Tones") style in advertising.

## Visuals

**Corporate Hippie**'s visuals can be described as a more watered-down, mainstream version of [Psychedelia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Psychedelia "Psychedelia"), both containing the same core visuals. **Corporate Hippie**, however, is less abstract and more consumer-friendly.

Common visual motifs include:

* Bright, saturated colors
* Bold, (sometimes) distorted text
* Subtle drug iconography (eg. mushrooms)
* Distorted visuals
* Kaleidoscopic and abstract patterns
* Flat, yet maximalist graphics
* Flowers, flourishes
* Themes of love

The people depicted in **Corporate Hippie** advertisements usually sport fashion and clothing reminiscent of [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie") fashion.
