# Mallgoth

_Also known as: Spooky Kid, Alternative Kid_

**Mallgoth** is a youth subculture that was prominent primarily in North America from the late 1990s to the mid-2000s. The term, a portmanteau of "mall" and "goth," was initially used pejoratively by members of the original [Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Goth "Goth") subculture to criticize teenagers who adopted a commercialized version of gothic fashion, often purchased from accessible mall retailers like Hot Topic and Spencer's.

The aesthetic is a cultural hybrid, blending simplified Goth elements with the baggy, [industrial](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Industrial "Industrial")-influenced silhouettes of the [Nu-Metal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Nu-Metal "Nu-Metal") scene and the angst of early [Emo](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Emo "Emo") culture. Despite the "goth" in its name, the subculture is musically distinct from the [post-punk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Post-punk "Post-punk") origins of the Goth scene and is instead centered on nu-metal, [industrial metal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Industrial_Metal "Industrial Metal"), and shock rock artists.

The aesthetic has experienced a significant revival in the 2020s as part of the broader 2000s nostalgia trend.

## History

The Mallgoth subculture originated in the late 1990s in the United States, emerging as a cultural hybrid during a period when alternative fashion and music were becoming highly commercialized. The style was born from a conflict: members of the traditional [Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Goth "Goth") subculture coined the term "Mallgoth" as a pejorative to criticize teenagers who adopted a simplified, dark aesthetic by purchasing mass-produced clothing from accessible mall stores like Hot Topic and Spencer's.

The aesthetic evolved as a response to and fusion with concurrent youth movements, blending simplified Gothic elements with the baggy silhouette of the [Nu-Metal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Nu-Metal "Nu-Metal") scene (bands like Korn and Slipknot) and the emotional angst of early [Emo](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Emo "Emo") culture. This combination often placed the Mallgoths at the center of a cultural battle, being criticized by both traditional Goths (for lack of authenticity) and mainstream society (for their dark image).

The subculture's relevance faded in the mid-2000s, but the aesthetic saw a significant resurgence in the mid-to-late 2010s. This revival was driven by [Y2K](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Y2K "Y2K") nostalgia and the influence of [Emo Rap](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Emo_Rap "Emo Rap") artists like Lil Peep and Lil Uzi Vert, who incorporated signature Mallgoth garments, such as bondage pants, into their "[sadboi](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Sadboi "Sadboi")" personas. This style also became a direct fashion influence on the [E-Girl](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/E-Girl "E-Girl") and [E-Boy](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/E-Boy "E-Boy") subcultures in the late 2010s and early 2020s.

## Fashion

### Clothing

The Mallgoth aesthetic is a highly accessible uniform of teenage rebellion, characterized by exaggerated silhouettes, mass-produced dark accessories, and a specific makeup style. The definitive look is characterized by a "big pants, little shirt" silhouette, blending [punk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Punk "Punk"), [goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Goth "Goth"), [rivethead](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Rivethead "Rivethead"), and [metalhead](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Metalhead "Metalhead") hallmarks.

The style heavily features oversized trousers; the staple piece is the wide-leg Tripp pants (often by the brand Tripp NYC), which are characteristically adorned with numerous chains, straps, and zippers. Oversized hoodies and cargo pants are also common. Above the waist, outfits often consist of black band tees (displaying allegiance to [Nu-Metal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Nu-Metal "Nu-Metal") or [Industrial Metal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Industrial_Metal "Industrial Metal") bands), paired with ripped fishnet stockings or simple tops. Footwear typically consists of chunky platform boots or sneakers, such as those by Demonia or Buffalo, which reinforces the influence from the [industrial](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Industrial "Industrial") music scene.

### Accessories and Makeup

Accessories and detailing are used to signal alternative status. The look is heavily accessorized with studded belts, spiked chokers and bracelets, and occult-themed jewelry like ankh necklaces or inverted crosses, all items widely available at mall retailers.

The aesthetic prioritizes a dramatic yet simple presentation that conveys teen angst and non-conformity. Makeup is kept minimal but dramatic. Heavy black eyeliner is a staple, often applied in a thick smoky style described by one former adherent as "raccoon-esque," usually paired with simple black or dark lipstick.

Hair is frequently dyed dark colors, often using products like Manic Panic, though split-dyed or two-toned hair also became common in the style's later years. The subculture's visual aesthetic is heavily influenced by popular culture, particularly the works of Tim Burton (*The Nightmare Before Christmas*, *Edward Scissorhands*) and cult horror films like *The Crow* and *The Craft*.

## Culture

The Mallgoth scene is characterized by its reliance on accessible spaces and its distinct musical taste, which caused friction with established alternative subcultures. The "Mall" in the name explicitly refers to the subculture's social hub. Activities center around frequenting shopping malls for socialization, browsing stores like Hot Topic, dining, visiting cinemas, and playing arcade games. The subculture's later years were heavily sustained by the rise of the internet, with members being early adopters of social media platforms like LiveJournal and MySpace.

## Music

Despite the visual aesthetic's debt to [Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Goth "Goth"), the musical identity of Mallgoth is different and significantly heavier. The adherents' musical preferences is dominated by [Nu-Metal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Nu-Metal "Nu-Metal") (Korn, Slipknot, Linkin Park) and [Industrial Metal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Industrial_Metal "Industrial Metal") (Marilyn Manson, Nine Inch Nails, Rob Zombie), later overlapping heavily with Emo Pop (My Chemical Romance). This musical preference was the primary source of animosity with traditional Goth subculture members, who often considered Mallgoths to be "[posers](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Poser "Poser")" interested only in the fashion and having no connection to the [post-punk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Post-punk "Post-punk") bands that originated the true Goth scene.
