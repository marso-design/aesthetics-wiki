# Atompunk

_Also known as: Raypunk_

**Atompunk** is a subgenre of science fiction that centers around a view of the future from the perspective of the [1950s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:1950s "Category:1950s") and [1960s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/1960s "1960s"), a darker version of [Retrofuturism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Retrofuturism "Retrofuturism"). Modern depictions tend to use a distinct, brightly-colored art style but it can also appear just as it does in the page's main image. It often depicts imagery associated with "traditionally American" values, particularly a belief in the nuclear family and the suburban lifestyle. But it's important to note that this isn't the [Raygun Gothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Raygun_Gothic "Raygun Gothic") aesthetic; it does not center itself around a utopian future but rather a dystopian nightmare. **Atompunk** is the dark underbelly of 1950s and 1960s sci-fi characterized by potential dark futures such as *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep*, satirical parodies of typically [suburban](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/50s_Suburbia "50s Suburbia") American life such as *Dad's Nuke* and more recently satirical parodies of the [Raygun Gothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Raygun_Gothic "Raygun Gothic") aesthetic such as *Futurama*.

## History

The **Atompunk** aesthetic originated from the [Raygun Gothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Raygun_Gothic "Raygun Gothic") and Googie aesthetics being born from the eccentric but brilliant mind of Philip K Dick with the book known as [*Solar Lottery*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_Lottery). It is the dark underbelly of that aesthetic and was created as a way to vent frustration at 1950s and 60s American society, acting as the counterculture to the typically utopian or otherwise non-dystopian stories that were popular at the time.

The term **Atompunk** was coined after [Cyberpunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cyberpunk "Cyberpunk") was invented, but it has existed long before the aesthetic made dystopian stories popular, similar to [Dieselpunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dieselpunk "Dieselpunk") and works such as [*Metropolis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_(1927_film)) and [*Things to Come*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Things_to_Come).

What differentiates **Atompunk** to the non-dystopian 50s/60s retrofuturism of [Raygun Gothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Raygun_Gothic "Raygun Gothic") is the ['-punk' suffix](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Punk_Suffix "Category:Punk Suffix"). While some consider its meaning to just being connected to the [Punk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Punk "Punk") movement, like [Cyberpunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cyberpunk "Cyberpunk"), that's a misattribution. Instead, the term *-punk* refers to how countercultural it was in its context. [Steampunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Steampunk "Steampunk") was countercultural in the sense that it was the development of a completely unique and new genre that oftentimes defied societal norms. [Dieselpunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dieselpunk "Dieselpunk") consists of two aesthetics: [Ottensian Dieselpunk/Decopunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Decopunk "Decopunk") and Piecraftian Dieselpunk (every other part of the Dieselpunk genre like [*Brazil*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_(1985_film)), *Metropolis*, *Things To Come*, etc.) which were created as counterculture in their respective genres; Ottensian after World War 2, and Piecraftian during and before World War 2. Overall, while Raygun Gothic is more of a celebration of its technological advancements and not something designed to push against the culture, **Atompunk** was designed as counterculture to that zeitgeist. The most well-known example of this aesthetic in the present day is the [*Fallout*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallout_(series)) series, which takes influences from early-to-mid **Atompunk** media such as [*2000 AD*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_AD_(comics)) and [*Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do_Androids_Dream_of_Electric_Sheep%3F).

## Visual

**Atompunk** typically envisions a satirical or dark twist on the [Raygun Gothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Raygun_Gothic "Raygun Gothic") aesthetic such as post-apocalyptic [Space Age](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Space_Age "Space Age") settings like *Dad's Nuke* and *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep*, though it will sometimes take the form of the simple Googie aesthetic, just with punk sensibilities.

## Fashion

**Atompunk** fashion tends to draw heavy inspiration from how people in the 1950s/1960s viewed how the future was going to look, similarly to [Raygun Gothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Raygun_Gothic "Raygun Gothic")'s fashion, so a lot of the outfits seen in pulp sci-fi of the time tend to be the primarily focus, as well as T-shirts adorned with graphics invoking the covers of pulp sci-fi comic books of the time, which tended to be sexually suggestive from time to time, being part of its fashion.

While most modern day uses the [retrofuturistic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Retrofuturism "Retrofuturism") aesthetic of *Futurama*, this is a different facet to the aesthetic; one that utilizes the Populuxe aesthetic but adds a touch of metropolis to it.

Atompunk can also take elements of [Art Deco](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Art_Deco "Art Deco"), primarily influenced by the [Dieselpunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dieselpunk "Dieselpunk") aesthetic of *Metropolis*.
