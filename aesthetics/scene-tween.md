**Scene Tween** is an aesthetic that emerged approximately in the early 2010s, combining elements of [Kidcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Kidcore "Kidcore") and the [Scene](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scene "Scene") subculture. This fusion arose as mainstream culture began marketing the original [Scene](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scene "Scene") aesthetic towards children, much like [Teenpunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Teenpunk "Teenpunk") with its [emo](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Emo "Emo")-inspired imagery, but targeting an even younger audience. Scene Tween retains many of the visual elements of [Scene](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scene "Scene"), such as animal patterns and bright colours, but with a distinctly childish and feminine twist.

## History

In the late 2000s when the Scene emerged among teens, it began to be stripped of its original roots in order to appeal to young children of the time, with the style peaking in tween brands in the 2010s such as Justice.

It too got deemed as a "diva/sassy" type of aesthetic and was more youthful and feminine much like its precursor.

## Visuals

Scene Tween and Scene, as mentioned above, share many similar motifs and visuals, such as:

* Bright colors
* Animal print patterns
* Animals (especially in fashion)
* Rainbows
* Neon colors
* Glitter
* Peace signs
* Hearts
* Positive messages
