# Dadcore

_Also known as: Divorced Dadcore, Adam Sandler Style_

**Dadcore** is an aesthetic and fashion trend based on the intentional adoption of clothing traditionally associated with middle-aged fatherhood and suburban domesticity. The style uses functional garments that are typically perceived as unfashionable or outdated, recontextualizing them as a reaction against the fleeting [microtrends](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Microtrend "Microtrend") of modern fast fashion.

Even though Dadcore is rooted in the broader "[Normcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Normcore "Normcore")" movement of the early 2010s, Dadcore originated between 2017 and 2018 when luxury fashion houses integrated these utilitarian silhouettes into their high-fashion collections. By 2026, the aesthetic evolved to include various sub-branches that deal with themes stability, adulthood, and existential apathy in response to geopolitical uncertainty.

## History

Dadcore is an evolution of [Normcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Normcore "Normcore"), a sociocultural fashion trend shift that embraced average or conventional attire as a form of stylistic rebellion. Where as Normcore focused on blankness and anonymity, Dadcore specifically targeted the wardrobe of a stereotypical suburban father, drawing inspiration from late 20th-century sitcoms and family photography. The transition from internet meme to high fashion occurred notably in 2017 when Balenciaga, under the creative direction of Demna, showcased a Spring/Summer collection featuring oversized parkas, stonewashed denim, and robust sneakers. This move recontexuatlized the aesthetics of the working and middle classes as consumer anthropology for a luxury audience.

By the early 2020s, Dadcore expanded beyond a simple parody of bad dressing. The global uncertainty of the decade prompted a shift toward clothing that offered a sense of safety and grounding. The figure of the father became a totem of tangible reality in an increasingly internet-based and unpredictable world. In 2025 and 2026, a specific variation known as "Divorced Dadcore" emerged on TikTok. This sub-branch focuses on a more melancholic and domestic-existential presentation, documenting the experience of post-separation adulthood through slouchy sweaters and worn leather jackets.

## Fashion

Dadcore is based on intentional imperfection and a rejection of traditional tailoring. It utilizes a silhouette often described as being in a no man's land between fits, neither strictly oversized nor slim. This manifests in shapeless jeans with long rises and pleated trousers that emphasize comfort over form.

Footwear is the most important element of the aesthetic. Thick-soled white sneakers, frequently referred to as "ugly sneakers" or "orthopedic shoes," are frequently used. Brands such as New Balance, Reebok, and Fila are the most common models, characterized by nylon overlays and chunky midsoles. These are frequently paired with white athletic ankle socks, creating a look reminiscent of 1990s tourist attire.

Upper-body garments consist of faded graphic t-shirts, particularly those featuring promotional logos from local businesses, NASCAR events, or defunct bars. Layering involves polar fleece half-zips, oversized flannel shirts, and utilitarian windbreakers. Accessories include rectangular cycling glasses, braided belts, and washed denim baseball caps. The color palette remains muted and conservative, dominated by heather grey, khaki, and navy, symbolizing a disinterest in the brighter or neon colors associated with other contemporary fashion trends.

## Philosophy

Dadcore represents a pushback against the perceived algorithmic tyranny of social media, where perfectionism and high-status signaling are the default. Choosing to dress like a boring father is then seen as a rejection of aspirational and elitist aesthetics such as [Old Money](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Old_Money "Old Money")/Quiet Luxury. It celebrates the wear and tear of everyday life, prioritizing the durability of garments over planned obsolescence.

The aesthetic also engages with a specific type of nostalgia for a security that feels real through material contact. For younger generations, inhabiting the paternal wardrobe is a way of claiming a maturity that feels distant or stolen due to economic precariousness. It uses apathy as armor, signaling an indifference to public approval. This "anti-sexiness" becomes its own form of authenticity, positioning the wearer as someone who has opted out of the competitive laws of late capitalism in favor of a simpler, more grounded existence.

## Music

The Dadcore aesthetic, especially within the "Divorced Dadcore" niche, centers on a specific collection of 1970s and 1980s rock and pop artists. These tracks are often compiled into playlists that evoke a sense of nostalgic domesticity or the atmosphere of a long car trip.
