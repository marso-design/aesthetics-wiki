**Chinese Folk Horror** (中式民俗恐怖) is the [Folk Horror](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Folk_Horror "Folk Horror") sub-genre based around traditional Chinese folk beliefs, funeral and marriage customs, folklores, and regional taboos. With a long history, it gained wide visibility on platforms like Bilibili, Douyin, and Steam since the 2020s. Visually, it relies on dim rural spaces, ritual symbols, and decayed old objects. The genre centers on the blurry boundary between the living and the dead, ancestral ethics, and karmic retribution, creating psychological horror by subverting familiar cultural customs.

## History

Well-known *zhi-guai* (志怪) literature from the Spring and Autumn Period through the Qing dynasty is a source of inspiration for modern Chinese folk horror. Some must-read works of the Republic of China era, such as *Diary of a Madman* by Lu Xun, employed horror as a metaphor for social realities, inspiring the critical perspective in modern Chinese folk horror.

The 1980s marked the golden age of [Jiangshi fiction](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Jiangshi_fiction "Jiangshi fiction"). These productions blended the folk custom of corpse escort, Taoist talismans, feng shui and fortune-telling with comedy and action, and were not purely folk horror. Since the 1990s, Hong Kong and Taiwan have produced numerous well-known folk horror films with a mature artistic style. Due to policy restrictions in mainland China that prohibites ghosts from appearing in movies, horror films are mainly psychological horror, suspense, and [dark fantasy](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Fantasy "Dark Fantasy").

Chinese folk horror works in mainland China are mainly video games, web fiction, and social media content. After several hit works emerged in 2019, Chinese folk horror content saw an explosive growth in popularity.

## Philosophy

Chinese folk horror draws from local moral ideas and Buddhist reincarnation beliefs. Many paranormal events revolve around making amends for past misdeeds, getting revenge and settling old scores.

Influenced by traditional folklore, romance is a common theme.

Old Hong Kong horror movies about haunted manors and village houses usually tell stories of family strife, grabbing family assets and defying ancestral rules. Supernatural disasters befall people who grow arrogant, flout ancient taboos, and break customs to cast black magic for their own gain.

Beneath the supernatural guise, many recent Mainland Chinese films and indie horror games criticize backwards and oppressing malpractices: son preference, arranged marriages, superstitious healing, fraud by fake mystics, village-wide apathy and blind conformity. The dread comes not only from supernatural entities, but also from humanity’s cold hearts and ignorance.

Common settings include **deserted villages, old mansions, foggy nights, graveyards,** and **mountain temples.** Typical architecture features **old houses with distinct local characteristics, adobe rooms, [ancestral halls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_shrine) (祠堂), opera stages,** and **ancient wells.** Core objects include **[*zhi ren* (纸人) paper figurines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_supernatural_beings_in_Chinese_folklore#Zhi_ren_(纸人)), [joss paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joss_paper) (纸钱), incense, peach-wood swords or talismans, red embroidered shoes,** and **family trees.**
