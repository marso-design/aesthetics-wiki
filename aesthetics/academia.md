**Academia** encompasses a group of aesthetics centered around the themes of learning, study, research, and reading, often drawing visual inspiration from historical academic settings like older universities (e.g., Oxford) and associated styles of dress, such as "librarian chic." While not solely focused on assigned studies like [Studyblr](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Studyblr "Studyblr"), Academia aesthetics often evoke the atmosphere of 19th and 20th-century boarding schools, as popularized by the emergence of [Dark Academia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Academia "Dark Academia") around 2014. [Light Academia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Light_Academia "Light Academia") later developed as a contrasting aesthetic, emphasizing the lighter, more joyful aspects of learning and life, yet sharing stylistic elements with its darker counterpart.

## Main Aesthetics

### [Dark Academia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Academia "Dark Academia")

**Dark Academia** is an aesthetic centered on the romanticization of higher education, classical literature, and the "Gothic" pursuit of knowledge. It is characterized by an emphasis on existentialism, moral ambiguity, and the themes of mortality found in the liberal arts. Visually, the aesthetic is defined by a palette of muted tones (specifically charcoal, forest green, and deep browns) and elements such as tweed, candlelight, and historical European architecture. The subculture often explores the tension between intellectual enlightenment and psychological decay.

### [Light Academia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Light_Academia "Light Academia")

**Light Academia** serves as a visual and philosophical counterpart to Dark Academia, prioritizing a positive and sentimental view of scholarly life. Its primary themes include curiosity, community, and the joy of learning. The aesthetic is characterized by an airy, sun-drenched visual style, utilizing cream, white, and tan color palettes. Common motifs include open-air libraries, botanical sketches, and handwritten correspondence. Unlike its "dark" counterpart, Light Academia focuses on growth and the emotional warmth of intellectual discovery rather than existential dread.

### [Green Academia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Green_Academia "Green Academia")

**Green Academia** integrates academic romanticism with environmentalism and the natural sciences. This subculture emphasizes the intersection of formal education and the outdoors, specifically focusing on subjects like botany, herbalism, and ecology. Visual markers include forest settings, pressed flowers, and organic materials such as linen and wood. The color scheme is dominated by earthy greens and browns. Philosophically, Green Academia promotes the idea that comprehensive wisdom requires a synthesis of book learning and a practical understanding of the natural world.

### [Chaotic Academia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Chaotic_Academia "Chaotic Academia")

**Chaotic Academia** focuses on the disorganized and high-pressure realities of student life, contrasting the more polished portrayals of other academic aesthetics. It highlights the frantic nature of obsessive study, often featuring imagery of cluttered desks, stained notes, and caffeine consumption. The aesthetic acknowledges the psychological toll of intellectual pursuits, including burnout and sleep deprivation. Visually, it is defined by a lack of coordination, utilizing mismatched clothing and over-stimulated environments to represent a mind preoccupied with information over aesthetics.

### [Romantic Academia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Romantic_Academia "Romantic Academia")

**Romantic Academia** is a sub-aesthetic that applies the values of the 19th-century Romantic movement to the modern scholarly experience. It prioritizes emotional sensitivity, the "sublime" beauty of art, and idealized interpersonal relationships within academic settings. Its visual identity consists of soft pastels, warm neutrals, and dreamlike lighting. Common tropes include poetry, wax-sealed letters, and the study of the Fine Arts. The aesthetic views education as a spiritual and emotional journey, rather than a purely clinical or intellectual endeavor.

### [Darkest Academia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Darkest_academia "Darkest academia")

**Darkest Academia** is a subtype of Dark Academia, which focuses on significantly darker tones of occult knowledge, magic, death and immortality. It is usually associated with earlier periods of 16-18 century literature and art, but contains several elements of later authors like Lovecraft. It is more focused on direct research and science rather than the process of learning, with Gothic and Victorian elements being the second strongest after Academia.

## Minor Variations

The extensive fragmentation of the Academia aesthetic into numerous specific sub-genres represents a shift in internet culture toward identity-based micro-labeling. Following the 2020 surge in popularity of Dark Academia, the community began adapting the main visual style to align with specific academic disciplines or personal hobbies. Cultural commentators have noted that this taxonomic expansion functions similarly to a personality test; rather than representing distinct design movements with unique histories, labels such as "Art Academia" or "Classic Academia" serve as individualized identity markers that allow users to curate the aesthetic to their specific self-image.
