# Cayetana

_Also known as: Cayestyle, Pija_

**Cayetana** is a Spanish fashion trend and social archetype associated with the daughters of upper-class families, characterized by understated, classically elegant clothing and an intentionally natural appearance. The term originates in Seville, where the given name "Cayetano" has long been common among wealthy, landowning families and, separately, among bullfighters from the city. By extension, "cayetano" and its feminine form "cayetana" became informal shorthand for a particular type of privileged Spanish youth long before any organized aesthetic emerged around the term.

The concept entered broader cultural consciousness through the 2018 song "*Cayetano*" by Madrid indie band Carolina Durante, a satirical portrait of the archetype that catalogued its markers with dry precision. The song became sufficiently popular in Spanish youth culture that it served as a pre-existing framework when TikTok users began organizing the aesthetic around 2021, producing a wave of "*baja un dedo si...*" (put a finger down if...) videos that codified membership in the archetype through dress, schooling, music taste, and even political alignment.

The aesthetic is closely related to the older [Pijo](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Pijo "Pijo") archetype, which is its masculine counterpart, and shares similarities with [Preppy](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Preppy "Preppy") in the United States and [Sloanie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Sloanie "Sloanie") in the United Kingdom.

## Fashion

The Cayetana look is built around a principle of studied casualness: garments are basic and well-cut rather than showy, and the overall effect is one of effortless composure rather than visible effort. Silhouettes are consistently loose and unstructured, with flared or wide-leg trousers, light blouses, simple shirts, and oversized knits forming the backbone of the wardrobe. Makeup is kept minimal and natural, manicures are short and understated, and hair is worn in an unforced style. The aesthetic specifically rejects anything conspicuously fitted, heavily made-up, or visually loud.

Jewelry tends toward delicate gold pieces or strings of colored beads, worn in small quantities. Footwear preferences run to loafers, ballet flats, and cowboy boots for dressed occasions, with Converse, Veja, and Golden Goose as the primary casual sneaker choices. The designer handbag most consistently associated with the aesthetic is Zadig & Voltaire, alongside black Ray-Ban sunglasses as a near-universal accessory. In colder months, the wardrobe extends to wool coats, tweed trousers, knit dresses, heeled boots, blazers, and midi skirts in wool, maintaining the same neutral palette of beige, white, navy, and black throughout the year. Favored brands include Scalpers, Renatta & Go., Zadig & Voltaire, Zara, and Mango, with Ralph Lauren polos frequently cited as a crossover staple.

## Criticism

### Promoting Classism

The most common criticism of the Cayetana aesthetic concerns its explicit class dimension. On TikTok, "cayetanas" are consistently defined in contrast to "Jennys," a pejorative term used to describe working-class Spanish girls, and the "put a finger down if..." format that popularized both labels made this opposition concrete: Cayetana-coded behaviors included attending private or state-subsidized private schools, supporting capitalism, holding right-wing political views, and wearing a bracelet bearing the Spanish flag (often associated with right-wing or far-right Spanish nationalism), while Jenny-coded criteria centered on perceived appearance, piercing and tattoo choices, and musical tastes. The juxtaposition was not neutral: Cayetana traits were presented as aspirational, while Jenny traits were made to look embarrassing or undesirable, a dynamic reinforced by videos in which creators pledged to dress as a "Jenny" if their video reached a certain number of likes, treating it as a penalty.

Researchers have noted that the Cayetana/Jenny binary mirrors comparable class-coded archetypes in other European countries, particularly the "[chav](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Chav "Chav")" stereotype in the United Kingdom, where the same logic of pathologizing working-class femininity through dress and behavior has a much longer documented history.

### Association with Conservatism and Spanish Nationalism

The Cayetana aesthetic on TikTok became associated with an explicitly conservative political identity, distinguished by visible symbols of Spanish nationalism. Videos featuring the Spanish flag worn as an accessory or incorporated into clothing (especially bracelets, which are controversial in Spain due to their association with the far-right political party VOX), performances of the national anthem, and content critical of the left-wing coalition government of Pedro Sánchez circulated widely under Cayetana-coded hashtags. The "#tagdelcayetano" challenge, which went viral in summer 2020 following the caceroladas protests in Madrid's Núñez de Balboa neighborhood, solidified this association, listing private schooling, support for bullfighting, and conservative political views alongside fashion markers as the defining criteria of the archetype.

By the mid-2020s, the trend had largely receded from its peak visibility, with major Spanish influencers associated with the aesthetic stepping back from it.
