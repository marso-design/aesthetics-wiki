# 2010s Dark Fandom

_Also known as: Insanitycore, EXEcore_

Unofficial Name 🏷️

This article title is a retrospective or descriptive label coined by the wiki community for cataloging purposes. While you may not find this specific term used elsewhere, the aesthetic it describes is a verifiable real-world phenomenon supported by external visual evidence. It serves to document a cohesive style that lacks an official name in mainstream culture.

**Reason for Warning:** This page discusses and displays graphic themes including depictions of blood, gore, mental instability, and fictional violence. Reader discretion is advised.

**2010s Dark Fandom** is a retrospectively named internet aesthetic that peaked approximately between 2010 and 2016. It is characterized by a fascination with mental instability, gore, and the "corruption" of innocent media, blending Western Creepypasta folklore with Eastern anime tropes (specifically the [Yandere](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Yandere "Yandere") archetype).

The aesthetic traces its origins to platforms like YouTube, DeviantArt, and Facebook (via the Spanish-speaking "Desmotivaciones" site) during an era of the internet that allowed unrestricted access to mature content. Its visuals are defined by an aggressive high-contrast palette of black and blood red, often depicting characters "snapping" or entering a violent, manic trance.

While the style was often dismissed as a teenage "edgy phase," the aesthetic served as a genuine emotional outlet for its predominantly young demographic, allowing them to explore themes of anger, jealousy, and alienation through fictional horror proxies.

## History

### Origins

The aesthetic emerged from the "Wild West" era of Web 2.0, where algorithmic safety filters were less advanced, allowing shock content to proliferate alongside child-friendly media. This environment birthed the Creepypasta phenomenon; digital folklore and urban legends spread via copy-and-paste text on forums. Stories like "The Russian Sleep Experiment" and characters like "Slenderman" and "Jeff the Killer" became viral cultural icons, with Slenderman peaking in popularity between 2012 and 2014.

### The Yandere Boom

Simultaneously, the anime community popularized the "[Yandere](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Yandere "Yandere")" trope; a character who is lovingly obsessive to the point of homicidal violence. The character Yuno Gasai from the 2011 anime *Mirai Nikki* (Future Diary) became the "codifier" of this trope, transcending the series to become a standalone symbol of the aesthetic.

By 2015, the game *Yandere Simulator* exploded in popularity on YouTube. Although the developer intended the game for a mature audience, internal data revealed that 43% of the fanbase was aged 11–15, confirming the aesthetic's status as a distinct youth phenomenon of the time.

### Decline

By the late 2010s, the popularity of the aesthetic began to wane due to a combination of cultural shifts and platform changes.

A significant turning event occurred with the 2014 "Slenderman stabbing" incident in Waukesha, Wisconsin. The event, in which two 12-year-olds attacked a classmate to allegedly appease the fictional character, triggered a mainstream moral panic regarding internet horror culture. This brought intense scrutiny to the *Creepypasta* community from parents and media, forcing a re-evaluation of the "roleplay" aspect of the fandom. The incident broke the immersion of the aesthetic; the blurring of fiction and reality, which had previously been a source of thrill, was now associated with severe real-world consequences.

The rise of "[Cringe Culture](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cringe_Culture "Cringe Culture")" (ironic commentary) around 2016 led to a widespread mockery of "edgy" internet behavior. The sincere and melodramatic, if performative, expression of insanity that defined the aesthetic became a target for ridicule, causing many adherents to abandon the style to avoid social stigma. Simultaneously, the pillars of the aesthetic faltered; *Creepypasta* narratives suffered from oversaturation and a perceived drop in quality, while *Yandere Simulator* faced prolonged development delays and controversy.

Furthermore, the 2017 YouTube "Adpocalypse" significantly impacted the propagation of the aesthetic. As YouTube's algorithms began to demonetize and restrict violent or "shocking" content to appease advertisers, the production of gore-heavy AMVs and horror-themed edits became financially unviable for creators.

## Visuals

A defining practice of the aesthetic is the "Evil Variant," where bright, cheerful characters from childhood media (such as *Pokémon*, *Sonic the Hedgehog*, or *My Little Pony*) are reimagined in a black-and-red palette to signify their "dark side". Examples include "Zatsune Miku" (a dark fanmade version of Hatsune Miku) and "Dark Link." Characters are frequently depicted with a "shadowed upper face" (obscuring the eyes) or with glowing, dilated pupils to convey a loss of control. The "Jeff the Killer" carved smile is also a recurring motif.

A significant portion of the aesthetic was propagated through "*Desmotivaciones*" (Demotivational) posters; images set in a black frame with white Times New Roman text. These captions often featured pseudo-philosophical quotes about madness, pain, or the "monster inside." Visuals often include "broken data" effects, such as static, JPEG artifacts, and "Zalgo" text. Hyper-realistic blood splatters are frequently superimposed over 2D anime characters to create a jarring, uncanny effect.

## Music

The music associated with this aesthetic included high-energy aggression, melodrama, and lyrical themes revolving around insanity, trauma, and being a "monster." The aesthetic was associated with various genres, ranging from Alternative Rock and [Nu-Metal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Nu-Metal "Nu-Metal") to Vocaloid and [Brostep](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Brostep "Brostep").

Notably, a significant portion of this music was consumed almost exclusively through the Nightcore format (speeding up and pitching up a track). The Nightcore remix style was ubiquitous in the community, as the faster tempo suited the editing style of Anime Music Videos (AMVs) and the higher pitch mimicked the youthful, "anime-esque" vocals associated with the characters.

## Media

### Television

* **Mirai Nikki (Future Diary):** The character Yuno Gasai is central to the aesthetic. Her fluctuations between cuteness and lovesick fury defined the visual shorthand for "insanity" in this era.
* **Tokyo Ghoul:** The protagonist Kaneki Ken, particularly his transformation and the "finger crack" gesture, became a symbol of tragic madness. Visual motifs like the red spider lily and the zipper-mask are derived from this series.
* **Elfen Lied**: Although released earlier, this series was highly influential for the aesthetic. The protagonist, Lucy, served as the archetype for the "tragic monster," and her split personality (alternating between the innocent "Nyu" and the murderous "Lucy") paved the way for the "corruption of innocence" theme found in later series like *Mirai Nikki*.
* **Higurashi When They Cry**

### Video Games

* **Yandere Simulator:** A stealth game that acts as a "simulator for sociopathy," allowing players to manipulate, stalk, and murder rivals. It popularized the "Yandere Meter" and the "Yandere Vision" aesthetic mechanics.
* **Five Nights at Freddy's**: A survival horror game franchise revolving around haunted animatronics, linked with child murder. Around the time of FNaF2’s release, Purple guy was a fan favourite character, mainly popularised by artist Rebornica’s portrayal of the character named “Vincent”. Other popular variants of him would be “Peter” from the “Five Nights at Freddy’s : Hidden lore” series by MrCreepyPasta, “Richard” made by InvertedMindInc and Dfox999’s version of the same name.
* **Alice: Madness Returns**: The sequel to American McGee's Alice.
* **Can Your Pet?:** A deceptive flash game that presents itself as a simple virtual pet simulator. After a short period of caring for a chick, the game forces a violent twist where the pet is processed through a "bicycle" (a concealed industrial saw). The game was a viral sensation in the 2010s, serving as a primary example of "shock bait" media that weaponized a cute and juvenile aesthetic to deliver sudden graphic horror.
* **Doki Doki Literature Club:** A horror game that presents itself as a regular anime dating sim. It became popular due to the same reasons as ‘Can Your Pet?’. The characters Sayori and Yuri were favourited due to the relatability of Sayori’s story and Yuri’s personality being depicted as a yandere.
* **(Mario) The Music Box**

### Other

* **Creepypasta:** Collaborative horror stories that blurred the line between fiction and reality. The most popular stories include Slenderman, a faceless entity that became a "modern urban legend", and Jeff the Killer, whose bleached face and carved smile became a visual staple. This also includes Grimdark MLP fanfiction, such as Cupcakes and Rainbow Factory.
* **Flipnote Hatena:** The online community for the Nintendo DSi animation app was a major hub for this aesthetic. Due to the app's limited color palette (consisting of black, red, and blue), users naturally gravitated toward the high-contrast "Black and Red" visual style. The platform popularized "MV" (Music Video) animations featuring Nintendo characters (particularly Mario and Luigi) in melodramatic, violent, or depressive storylines.
* **FNAFHS (Five Nights at Freddy's High School):** A web series by Mexican YouTuber Edd00chan that reimagined FNAF characters as teenagers. The series appealed to the aesthetic through the "Shadow" characters (specifically Fred), who were depicted as parasitical "split personalities" or manifestations of inner darkness. The series was historically significant for sparking a massive "Anti-FNAFHS" movement, where critics attacked the show for perceived unoriginality and copyright infringement, highlighting the growing tension between sincere fandom and "cringe culture."
* **Vocaloid:** Darker songs by producers like Neru (e.g., "*Tokyo Teddy Bear*") and Machigerita (e.g., "*Rotten Girl, Grotesque Romance*") were heavily featured on edits, often dealing with themes of abandonment and identity crises.
* **Hotdiggedydemon and the .MOV Series:** Max Gilardi's Pony.MOV series took the innocent world of My Little Pony and injected it with extreme violence, grotesque body horror, and psychological breakdowns. While explicitly labeled for adults, the algorithm pushed it to the juvenile MLP fanbase, making it a foundational pillar of the Grimdark MLP subculture alongside fictions like "Cupcakes."
