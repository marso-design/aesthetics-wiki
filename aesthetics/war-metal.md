**War Metal**, also known as **Bestial Black Metal** or **War Black Metal**, is an aggressive, chaotic, and cacophonous subgenre of [heavy metal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Metal "Metal") that fuses first-wave [black metal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Black_Metal "Black Metal") with the brutal intensity of [death metal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Death_Metal "Death Metal") and grindcore. The genre and its associated aesthetic are defined by a primitive, lo-fi, and overtly hostile atmosphere, with lyrical and visual themes centered on war, nuclear holocaust, chaos, and Satanism.

## History

The foundations of War Metal were laid in the mid-to-late 1980s by pioneering extreme metal bands. The 1987 debut album *I.N.R.I.* by the Brazilian band Sarcófago is considered a major and foundational influence on the genre's development.

The genre was then formally pioneered in the late 1980s and early 1990s by a handful of key international bands. In Canada, Blasphemy's 1990 debut album *Fallen Angel of Doom* is widely credited with codifying the War Metal sound. Simultaneously, a scene in Finland was developing a similar style with bands like Beherit and Archgoat. The term "war metal" itself was reportedly coined by members of the Australian band Bestial Warlust, another early and influential act in the scene.

## Visual Aesthetic

The visual aesthetic of War Metal complements the music's primitive and aggressive nature. It is typically raw, stark, and confrontational. Album covers and band photos often use grainy, high-contrast, black-and-white photography. The fashion associated with the bands and fans is an extreme version of classic metal attire, with a heavy emphasis on bullet belts, large spikes, leather, and often corpse paint. The look is designed to be intimidating and chaotic, rejecting any sense of polish or subtlety in favor of a raw, bestial appearance.

## Music

The sound of War Metal is intentionally abrasive and chaotic. It is differentiated from other styles of black metal in several key ways.

Instead of the trebly, tremolo-picked lead guitars common in Norwegian black metal, War Metal bands typically rely on heavy, downtuned power chords. The vocals are not the ghostly shrieks of black metal, but rather a low-register growl more akin to death metal. The song structures are often short (around three minutes) and punctuated with abrupt, grindcore-like bursts of speed and blast beats.

The genre is also known for its distinctive production style. In contrast to the thin and trebly lo-fi production of many black metal bands, War Metal is produced in a muddy, bass-heavy, and cacophonous fashion. This creates a chaotic wall of sound in which individual riffs are often difficult to discern.
