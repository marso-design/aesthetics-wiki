# Monochrome Luxe

**Monochrome Luxe** is a semi-minimalist style of interior design that arose in the 80s and extended into the 90s. Originally a high-end style derived from high-tech architecture, over time it shed that association and became a style that was entirely appropriate for middle class design. Because it simplicity gives it a certain timelessness. It is still found in use occasionally.

## Visuals

* A preference for smooth and glossy, but not heavily reflective materials: melamins, plastic, metal, glass (both clear and frosted), leather
* Matte or painted metals preferred over chrome
* Two-color interiors with accents. The palette usually of white and black, but sometimes another color replacing either of these
  + In particular, avoidance of natural beige and brown tones. Material like varnished or untreated wood (wood floors occasionally show in later treatments) and raw linen are to be avoided
* Color blocking (e.g. in kitchen cabinets) over patterns
* Avoidance of large reflective surfaces (much more common in the art deco revivals of the period
* Lighting is mostly incandescent rather than colored, neons are rarely used as decor elements (in contrast with [Laser Grid](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Laser_Grid "Laser Grid"))
* The light fixtures themselves are frequently articulated (desk-lamp style or spotlights), or they are postmodern in design, with heavy used of flat, cut glass for dramatic light refraction patterns
* Geometric furniture with simple, yet bold shapes
* Shapes are generally abstract. If present, patterns are simple and geometric: stripes, checkers...

Early elements that had faded out of the style by the early-mid nineties include:

* Steel trusses as architectural (e.g. to hang lights) or furniture elements
* Other industrial elements like embossed sheet steel
