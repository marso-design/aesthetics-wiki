# Sinofuturism

_Also known as: Chinese Sci-Fi Futurism, Socialist Cyberpunk_

**Sinofuturism** (中华未来主义) is the aesthetic that explores future visions and expressions tied to Chinese cultural identity.

## History

The concept of Sinofuturism was coined by artist Lawrence Lek, born in Frankfurt, Germany and now based in London, in his 2016 essay film. His discourse centers on seven stereotypes that Western observers hold about contemporary China: Calculation, Replication, Gaming, Learning, Addiction, Labor, Gambling. This futurist movement is also inspired by [Afrofuturism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Afrofuturism "Afrofuturism").

As a wave of Chinese science fiction works represented by The Three-Body Problem has evolved into an undeniable presence in the global sci-fi cultural landscape, Sinofuturism has taken on richer interpretations.

### Terminology

**Sinofuturism** is a Western-theorized art movement and often ironic or critical of stereotypes, emphasizing technology as social structure (computing, labor, gaming). **Chinese Sci-Fi Futurism** (中国科幻未来主义) refers to the themes "characterised by faith in kindness, optimism, collectivism, profound homeland and national sentiments" seen in Chinese science fiction since the 20th century. Despite the differences, the two terms are interchangeable when referring to the aesthetic.

## Philosophy

Proponents seek to highlight its emphasis on national culture, especially modern indigenous technological experiences, and employ the concept to encapsulate the overall developmental state of current Chinese science fiction. Artists view the explosive growth of contemporary China following the reform and opening-up from an accelerationist perspective.

## Visuals

Whether in terms of **urban development** or **the admiration for technology, infrastructure and space exploration**, we can all discern the imprint of the Soviet Union and Eastern Europe. However, the Sinofuturism aesthetic is more rooted in the advancement of **digital technology, electronics** and **artificial intelligence**, shifting its design imagination centered on space and rockets toward more realistic and actionable domains such as **smart cities** and **digital surveillance**.

On the one hand, old industrial cities in northern China have gradually become economically hollowed out, degenerating into **ghost towns** of a bygone era. On the other hand, emerging first-tier cities have morphed into **intelligent metropolises** in a classic futuristic style. Against this backdrop, the audio-visual landscape of China’s urban and rural areas increasingly takes on the **surreal streetscape** aesthetic seen in sci-fi films.
