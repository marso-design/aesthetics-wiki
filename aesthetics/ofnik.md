# Ofnik

_Also known as: Офники_

**Ofniki** (Офники) is a Russian and Eastern European youth subculture that developed during the mid-to-late 2010s. It emerged from VK (VKontakte) communities alongside street-level youth culture, combining branded sportswear, group street fighting, and a code of physical dominance, with videos of fights circulated and edited within VK communities.

## Cultural Code

### Poyasni za shmot (Explain your clothes)

The defining rule of the subculture is "poyasni za shmot," or "explain your clothes." Wearing specific brands functioned as a marker of belonging, and if a person wore these items without belonging to the subculture or without being prepared to defend that choice, they could be confronted by Ofniki and asked to justify wearing them.

## Fashion

Clothing operated as a form of identification within the subculture. The Stone Island compass patch, buttoned onto the left sleeve, held particular status among members. C.P. Company jackets and caps, built with glass lenses over the brim or hood, were also sought after; pulling the hood or brim down obscured the wearer's face. Napapijri anoraks, recognizable by their Norwegian-flag patch, were commonly worn as outerwear during colder months. For footwear, retro Adidas models such as the Gazelle, Samba, and Campus were worn for their flat soles and low-profile design, which suited them to street confrontations.

## Subcultural Activities

### Zabiv culture

Central to the Ofniki lifestyle is the "zabiv" (забив), a bare-knuckle group fight between rival youth gangs. Weapons were generally prohibited by informal rule, though this restriction did not always hold in practice. The goal of a zabiv was the physical defeat and public humiliation of the opposing group.

### The Battlegrounds: Lesopolosa and Urban Chaos

The location of a fight depended on the circumstances that led to it. Larger, pre-arranged clashes between gangs commonly took place in the lesopolosa (лесополоса), the forest belts surrounding residential suburbs, chosen for their distance from police presence. Fights also broke out in urban spaces, including residential courtyards, school grounds, and areas near shopping malls, often prompted by a "poyasni za shmot" confrontation or another provocation.

### The VK Media Weapon

Fights, whether planned or spontaneous, were frequently filmed by bystanders on smartphones. This footage was edited with music and posted to VK communities dedicated to the subculture. Circulating footage of a rival's defeat functioned as a way of establishing status within the subculture.
