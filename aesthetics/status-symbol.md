# Status Symbol

A **status symbol** is a visual element or object that signifies wealth or social status. Different aesthetic communities adopt status symbols that can be incredibly variable, and whose meaning is known within that aesthetic community. So, while traditional signifiers of wealth (luxury products, mansions, and travel) can be prominently featured, subtler signifiers that look innocuous to outsiders, but carry across a more specific meaning can also be status symbols within a certain community. (See the gallery below for certain examples.)

Often, status symbols can be contradictory through different times, and are often reactions towards old status symbols. This is often because as the trend becomes adopted by more people, the status symbol becomes less special, and the [early adopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_adopter) move on and find new ones. However, this can also be because a new demographic or social group becomes the new determiners of what is "cool" through the mainstream-fiction of their subculture; subcultures are often a reaction against the past mainstream culture, which is why these signifiers are often contradictory.

Status symbols can also signify things beyond wealth. Depth of knowledge, personality traits, dedication to a specific subculture, etc. are some values that different visuals try to symbolize. In fact, these visuals can arguably be seen as the entire basis for many aesthetics in general.

Aesthetic culture itself is also a status symbol. In [Microtrend culture](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Microtrend), certain social media users' ability to adapt to various microtrends demonstrates the ability to know/predict the latest trends and having the money to purchase the items necessary for these outfits. Connecting to the previous paragraph, being fully a part of the subculture, as opposed to simply occasionally participating in it, is a symbol for being more involved. Aesthetics that are more niche or have elements that are harder to acquire (such as wearing [J-fashion](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Harajuku_Fashion "Harajuku Fashion") in the West) also symbolize a person's ability to find unusual knowledge and acquire the things necessary to partake in a visual, real-life aesthetic.

## Examples of Status Symbols with Explanations

[Old Money](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Old_Money "Old Money") is an aesthetic that heavily relies on status symbols. Here, a man poses with an expensive car and wears designer clothing.

How one styles and depicts status symbols also affects interpretation. Here is "[New Money](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/New_Money "New Money")," which openly flaunts these signifiers in excess, as opposed to "quietly" using them in Old Money.

A Repeating Ita Bag is a status symbol in Fangirl/[Weeaboo](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Weeaboo "Weeaboo") culture. Rare/limited edition pins can resell at expensive prices, and the ability to find these pins is an overt symbol of dedication to a certain character.

Within [Soft Grunge](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Soft_Grunge "Soft Grunge") during 2014 Tumblr, branded plastic water bottles were a status symbol, as it demonstrated an aloofness to spending money on "useless" things.

[Hipsters](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hipster "Hipster") have an element of competition where having more knowledge on older and lesser known music makes one "more of a Hipster."

Body and beauty standards are an example of a status symbol. [Heroin Chic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Heroin_Chic "Heroin Chic") is a signifier of being "cool" by having low appetite because of drugs and prioritizing fashion, rather than food.
