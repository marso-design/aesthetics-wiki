# Mochette

_Also known as: Forgottencore, Grosscore, Little Ugly, Lostcore, Nauseous Nostalgia, Ugly Cute, Usedcore, Vomitcore_

[Sensitive Content Notice](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Aesthetics_Wiki:Content_Warning_Policy "Aesthetics Wiki:Content Warning Policy") ⚠️

The following article contains and discusses content that may be distressing to some readers.  
**Reason for Warning:** The following imagery featured in this page can be considered distressing or disturbing to some readers. Viewer discretion is advised.

**Mochette** is a niche internet aesthetic that juxtaposes 'ugly' and 'gross' imagery, usually decay or disease, and cute, feminine imagery. Its name comes from combining 'moche,' the French word for 'ugly,' with the suffix -ette, meaning 'little.'

While it has similar aesthetic origins to [Morute](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Morute "Morute"), Mochette focuses on contrasting [cuteness](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cute "Cute") with the 'disgusting,' 'ugly,' or 'grotesque,' while [Morute](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Morute "Morute") utilizes the 'dark,' 'creepy,' or 'morbid.' Additionally, Mochette has a higher emphasis on decay and rot, particularly outdoors and in nature, and the colors green and brown. Where [Morute](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Morute "Morute") may inspire feelings of fear or unease, Mochette intends to instead cause revulsion and nausea.

## History

### Surrealist Films

Perhaps the earliest origins of Mochette are surrealist films, particularly European ones from the 20th century, which are often posted by Mochette bloggers. The most popular ones include *Valerie and Her Week of Wonders* (1970), *Daisies* (1966), *Alice* (1988), and *Little Otik* (2000). These films often have a whimsical atmosphere, and handle dark or disturbing concepts with a light tone, and pretty, feminine imagery, and are strikingly similar to what bloggers would create intentionally to invoke the aesthetic.

### Katie Jane Garside

Some of Mochette's earliest origins can also perhaps be traced to the fashion, photography, and art of multidisciplinary artist and musician Katie Jane Garside. While she is often associated with [Kinderwhore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Kinderwhore "Kinderwhore"), particularly as member of [Grunge](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Grunge "Grunge") rock bands Daisy Chainsaw and Queenadreena, her personal brand of the aesthetic contained more blood, dirt, nudity, and torn clothing than her peers did, and was known for her stage antics, where she reportedly would do things like drilling holes into dolls' heads, strangling or otherwise mutilating herself, tearing off her clothes, and drinking from baby bottles.

In the latter half of the 2000s, she both formed the duo Ruby Throat, and released her debut solo album, *Lullabies in a Glass Wilderness,* both featuring a combination of soft, whispery, at times pretty and at times creepy, vocals, and disturbing or unsettling lyrics. One of her most popular solo songs, for example, is called "Roadkill."

In 2007, Katie Jane Garside presented a multimedia art exhibition titled *Darling, They've Found the Body.* While there are very little photos from it online, the exhibition featured artworks, photography, short films, and handmade/upcycled clothing all made by Garside herself, all of which were similar to and later influenced Mochette bloggers.

### 2010s

In 2010-2012, there became an offshoot of the [Pale](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Soft_Grunge "Soft Grunge") and [Delicate Sweet](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Delicate_Sweet "Delicate Sweet") aesthetic movements where users would post images that had similar faded filters, light colors, and pink, feminine elements, except with more grotesque elements, particularly bugs, insects, and worms, and injured or sick people. The most famous of these bloggers was Nicole Dollanganger, whose usernames at the time (@pinkcum, @babylarvae, and @bugsinricepudding) reflected this contrast between 'gross' and unsanitary elements, and more cutesy and sweet ones.

As this aesthetic developed, and Dollanganger's personal aesthetic and music evolved, the more disturbing and disgusting elements of it were slowly replaced with more gothic and morbid ones, becoming what is known as [Morute](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Morute "Morute").

However, many of these bloggers, often friends or fans of Dollanganger, continued to err on the 'disgusting' and grotesque side of the aesthetic, continuing into the late 2010s. An earlier version of what became Mochette, containing things like abandoned rural homes, burned childhood bedrooms, and battered and dirty dolls and toys, began appearing under tags like 'forgottencore,' 'lostcore,' and 'usedcore' in the latter half of the 2010s, with the most graphic of these images (often containing gore, nudity, or vomit and other bodily fluids) being tagged as 'grosscore' and 'vomitcore.'

### 2020s

In early 2022, now-deactivated Tumblr user @frowup coined and defined the term Mochette, and gave a brief overview of its most common themes, motifs, and visuals, including a collage and a playlist, leading to the term having widespread use on sites like Tumblr and Pinterest.

## Visuals

Mochette visuals typically consist of 'gross' or rotting imagery, and pretty, feminine imagery. This is often accomplished by simply posting cute things, like dolls and stuffed animals, and 'gross' things, like mold and bugs, on the same blog. However, many Mochette bloggers intentionally find images which combine both elements, either by cute-ifying 'ugly' things (for example, photoshopping a bow onto a picture of a worm) or by 'tainting' cute things (for example, ripping or staining a pretty dress). Soft, pastel colors (especially pink) are commonly contrasted with 'sickly' shades of green, yellow, or brown to evoke this.

Common Mochette visuals include:

* Abandoned or burned homes (particularly rooms with pink or pastel walls)
* Bugs, insects, worms, larvae, slugs, and snails (particularly when they are juxtaposed with something pink or otherwise feminine, or are pink themselves)
* Human anatomy (particularly bones, hands, and eyeballs)
* Rodents and vermin
* Mold and fungi (particularly when growing on or near 'pretty' things)
* Decaying and rotting things (buildings, homes, food, corpses, anything)
* Furniture and other antiques worn with age
* Dried flowers, or vintage/antique fake flowers
* 'Haunted' places and things
* Dolls and their parts or accessories (particularly when broken, taken apart, or dirty)
* Lace (clothing, tablecloths, anything; particularly when they are stained or yellowed with age)
* Dangerous and/or rabid animals
* Weapons and tools (particularly when rusty or dirty)
* Saints and church iconography (particularly when old, dirty, faded, or abandoned)
* Ball-jointed dolls, particularly fairy or elf-ish ones
* [Vulture Culture](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vulture_Culture "Vulture Culture") (particularly wet specimens)
* Dirty, stained, or torn clothing and toys
* Vomit and other bodily fluids
* 'Gross-looking' food, or food with poor lighting or surroundings that make it look 'dingy' or unappealing
* Sexual or pornographic imagery (particularly when it has 'weird' or 'gross' elements, or has poor lighting or surroundings that make it look 'dingy' or unappealing)
* Nonsexual nudity of injured or sick people (particularly things like skin diseases, bug bites, bruises, spider veins, surgical stitches and scars, or bite marks)
* Creepy or weird art, made from broken or dirty toys and other objects
* Rusty or tarnished vintage jewelry
* Raw meat

## Fashion

Mochette fashion generally consists of feminine, dollish clothing in pale, 'sickly' colors, or that has been stained, torn, or worn with age. Popular choices for clothing items include dresses with milkmaid or babydoll silhouettes, satin or lacy lingerie, pajamas, or hospital gowns. Generally, Mochette clothing does not have many patterns, and sticks to solid colors, but patchwork, embroidery, and cross stitch can sometimes be seen. White- or cream-colored lace is extremely popular. Many Mochette bloggers choose not to 'ruin' clothing by tearing or staining it, and instead choose to wear it while posing with abandoned and decaying structures to get a similar effect.

Due to the fact that Mochette as a fashion style is very fluid and relies on contrast more than it does specific clothing elements, it frequently overlaps with other styles. Often, Mochette bloggers repost images of antique Edwardian or Victorian clothing, or of JFashion styles such as [Lolita](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Lolita "Lolita"), [Mori Kei](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Mori_Kei "Mori Kei"), or, in particular, [Cult Party Kei](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cult_Party_Kei "Cult Party Kei"), due to their childlike femininity. Sometimes, more [Grunge](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Grunge "Grunge"), [Kinderwhore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Kinderwhore "Kinderwhore"), or even [Fairy](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Fairycore "Fairycore")-like elements are used, such as combat boots, striped stockings, or messy hair and makeup, likely due to Katie Jane Garside's influence on the aesthetic.

## Media

Mochette media often overlaps with [Morute](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Morute "Morute"), as they, broadly, both combine 'pretty' or 'cute' elements, and dark or disturbing ones. However, Mochette typically has less of the [Gothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Gothic "Gothic") horror elements of [Morute](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Morute "Morute") media, in favor of more 'raw' and 'gritty' ones. In addition, it is often more weird or surreal than many pieces of [Morute](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Morute "Morute") media.

### Music

While [Morute](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Morute "Morute") music has evolved to encompass many different genres, even including electronic and pop music, Mochette has grown/evolved very little since its conception. Artists popular with Mochette bloggers are generally confined to folk and singer-songwriter, grunge, metal, early industrial music, 'outsider' music, and avant-garde/experimental music, with Nicole Dollanganger and Katie Jane Garside being fan favorites.
