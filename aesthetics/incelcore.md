# Incelcore

_Also known as: E-punk, Losercore, Internet punk, Bedroom punk_

[Sensitive Content Notice](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Aesthetics_Wiki:Content_Warning_Policy "Aesthetics Wiki:Content Warning Policy") ⚠️

The following article contains and discusses content that may be distressing to some readers.  
**Reason for Warning:** This page documents a music genre characterized by themes of misogyny, anti-feminist sentiment, self-harm, and social alienation. It contains references to real-world violence (e.g., the 2019 murder of Bianca Devins) and controversial internet subcultures (incels). The content is presented for encyclopedic documentation and does not represent an endorsement of these ideologies.

**Incelcore** (also known as **E-Punk**) is an Internet rock microgenre and visual aesthetic that originally emerged from online imageboards (specifically 4chan's /r9k/ and /mu/) in the late 2010s. Musically, it blends the lo-fi home-recorded instrumentation of bedroom pop and indie with the aggressive, simple chord structures of classic [Punk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Punk "Punk"), [Grunge](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Grunge "Grunge"), Pop-Punk and Alternative Rock, while drawing influences from Internet culture. The music is characterized by a style of home-recorded internet rock commonly made using digital drum machines, stock plugins and amp simulators in a Digital Audio Workstation (DAW).

The aesthetic is defined by the appropriation of stylistic conventions associated with online communities and movements as well as 4chan culture, the "[incel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incel "wikipedia:Incel")" subculture, Internet gaming culture, edgelord culture and "[Doomer](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Doomer "Doomer")" memes. Lyrically, songs often express themes of 21st century alienation, particularly, social isolation, self-hatred and depression. These themes are delivered through layers of heavy irony that blur the line between genuine belief and "shitposting". Some American incelcore artists have referenced right wing politics in their music and aesthetics through an irony poisoned lens which has led to associations with online alt-right movements. The scene has also had a degree of overlap with [Scenecore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scenecore "Scenecore") with early incelcore artists such as xo\_willow (crucify april). The Doomer wave microgenre which grew out of [Vaporwave](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vaporwave "Vaporwave") would also have an influence and overlap in the scene.

Negative XP largely distanced himself from the scene in 2022 after disavowing the term "incelcore" and coining E-punk on Twitter, releasing an album annually at the end of each year. Other artists, including Anhero, Gjallahornit, Gezebelle Gaburgably, Hard Christ, Spree, and Owen Adamcik, also abandoned the scene. By 2024–26, the scene remained largely inactive until it was revived by the success of Fried By Fluoride’s 2023 single "The Love I Lost" on TikTok.

## History

### Origins (2016-2019)

The term "incelcore" was coined by Alan S. Kim, known professionally as Negative XP, who began making music in 2016. He first learnt to play guitar at age 24 and recorded an early demo for his song "Cry For Help" under the alias 'kilmaan', posting it on Soundcloud that same year. Kim later made Vaporwave under the alias Ecco Plus and DaDa\_Digital, initially gaining recognition in the underground [Vaporwave](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vaporwave "Vaporwave") scene with his material being reposted on the infamous Vaporwave archive channel "Vapor Memory". He then changed his name to "school shooter" and "shooterdude69", releasing music under both aliases before settling on Negative XP. In 2018, he coined the term "incelcore" to describe his style of music on Twitter. According to a LastFM page, Kim had released songs under the School Shooter moniker in 2015, with some fans stating lost media material dated back to 2014.

Kim also had a side project in 2019 named "ROPEFUEL" which focused on incelcore songs sang from the perspective of a woman. Though the name "incelcore" suggests a focus on "involuntary celibacy". Despite usage of incel terminology, most of the scene does not consider themselves a part of the online incel community nor subscribe to "blackpill" ideology, though a small minority do. The genre acts similar to something like “goregrind” where violent imagery is used to repel and offend outsiders, those in this particular scene use incel aestheticism and imagery to offend people, and as an artistic device without really being active in that particular movement, as most artists do not visit incel forums. Which has led to members in the incel subculture to view the genre as "fakecel-core". The label was chosen as a deliberately provocative gatekeeping mechanism, with no consistent lyrical focus across incelcore—songs range from misanthropic and satirical to introspective or purely stylistic. The scene is difficult to delineate strictly by sound, besides being predominantly internet-driven. Though a rock based sound is most popular, the genre has equal examples in hip-hop, post-punk and electronic music.

### Virginfest, Wizard Tower and E-Punk / Bedroom Punk (2020-2022)

In 2020, Pacific Purgatory coined the term "bedroom punk" as an early form of rebranding the incelcore moniker. The term would be adopted by artists such as Sodaboy64 and Greens on Toast. However, various artists criticized Purgatory for attempting to sanitize the genre which in turn led him to lose respect in the scene and ultimately abandon incelcore. UhOhSlater released the song "Fuck Bedroom Punk" in 2020 as a response to Purgatory, which was later covered by Belowground.

In 2021, the scene's attempt to move offline resulted in the infamous "Virgin Fest" concert in Atlanta, Georgia. The concert was hosted and booked by Athena Raven Rapp at her place of work Toki Tattoo, however, minutes before artists were scheduled to perform, local Atlanta Antifascists phoned into the establishment claiming the artists had far-right ties and so the show was cancelled and moved to an outdoor performance in the woods. Artists who performed at Virginfest later formed their own offline subset of the scene known as "Wizard Tower" which was at odds with the online artists. WT would come to an end after various forms of infighting in the scene which led to most offline artists abandoning incelcore.

By 2022, while the community initially embraced the “incelcore” label for shock value, Negative XP disavowed the term and coined the label “E-punk” on Twitter. He issued copyright strikes against several incelcore fan edit music video channels, which led to their termination, most notably *Up to the Wolves*. Negative XP had always been a devoted Christian, with many artists in the scene overlapping with the Internet based conservative "Trad Cath" movement. This event led to a fracture in the community, with several artists adopting the E-punk label, such as Sodaboy64, Hard Christ, Greens on Toast, and Zen Michelin, as a way to rebrand and distance themselves from the negative connotations associated with incelcore. While others such as h4rtbrkr, UhOhSlater, Cameron Phlodge, Fried By Fluoride, NEET, Crustsox and Tzarbombah saw e-punk as sanitizing and commodifying the authenticity of the movement's culture. Redpilled Rockstars released the song "Fuck E-Punk" in January 2022.

### The SoundCloud Online Indie Music Boom / Losercore (2024-26)

In 2023-24, the incelcore scene was generally inactive in the wider music sphere until the success of Fried By Fluoride's song "The Love I Lost" which reintroduced broader interest in the genre. A figure in the scene Ofigz would start the "e-cel" podcast in efforts to centralize the movement again, however, he would abandon the scene shortly after musician Lil Watermark's death with the E-Cel discord server being deleted by Discord due to Antioch school shooter Solomon Henderson being discovered as having been a member.

Subsequently, underground rap artists such as jaydes and wifiskeleton rose to prominence, the latter formed the Gothangelz collective alongside keepsecrets, and witchbox. Wifiskeleton collaborated with Fried By Fluoride in 2023 on the song "twenty sucks ＜/3 bury me in liquor". These artists wrote alternative rock / bedroom pop songs that although were influenced by the movement, were frequently erroneously tagged as "incelcore" on TikTok. They drew influence from indie artists such as Alex G. The style is defined as a strain of online indie music relying on text-to-speech samples and trap-based influences and has been referred to as “losercore,” “incelcore,” or “wifiskeleton-clone music.” The visual aesthetic of this strain of the genre has frequent overlap with [Olderbrothercore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Olderbrothercore "Olderbrothercore"), [Femcel](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Femcel_(anime_subculture) "Femcel (anime subculture)") or [Hikikomori](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Otaku "Otaku") culture and other forms of TikTok influenced 2000s revivalism.

Artists such as blehh, chickentender, bleeder and without a life have been active in reviving the original incelcore sound.

## Visuals

The Epunk aesthetic can be characterized as "post-Internet" as it's visually radically distinct from traditional [Punk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Punk "Punk") or [Emo](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Emo "Emo") due to its reliance on "terminally online" imagery. It rejects the fashion-focused rebellion of [Scenecore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scenecore "Scenecore") in favor of a "give up" or "Lay Down And Rot" (LDAR) aesthetic. The primary visual language of the genre are 4chan symbols such as Pepe the Frog or the Wojak (or "Feels Guy") meme. Album covers and promotional art frequently feature crude MS Paint drawings. Associated iconography include the act of "bedrotting".

To mimic the look of early 2000s imageboards, visuals often use "dithered" black-and-white filters or low-bitrate compression. A recurring setting is the cluttered bedroom of a "NEET" (Not in Education, Employment, or Training), featuring piles of trash, energy drink cans, old CRT monitors, and gaming consoles. This serves as a visual metaphor for the artist's social isolation.

## Fashion

A common trend among Epunk artists (e.g., Fried by Fluoride, Negative XP) is the wearing of deliberately provocative or taboo clothing. This serves as a visual signal of the genre's "anti-PC" and "contrarian" ethos. Fans and artists of the aesthetic, heavily incorporate "Criminal Chic," such as the infamous FTP (Fuck The Population) "Columbine Physical Education" shirt or merchandise featuring Charles Manson and Varg Vikernes (Burzum). 4chan culture accessories such as the "Please be patient, I have autism" baseball cap (often black or blue) are also worn. While artists such as Fried by Fluoride wore Trump / MAGA clothing as a form of provocation.

## Music

### Characteristics

The original incelcore / e-punk rock sound commonly features drum programming rather than live percussion with artists favoring fake sounding MIDI drums and a DAW oriented rock aesthetic. Cymbal beats are typically absent. Songs tend to rely on a limited set of chord progressions, most notably I–III–IV–V, I–III–IV–II, I–III–vi–IV, I–vi–IV–V, and I–vi–III–IV. Melodic parts frequently consist of eighth-note lead patterns. Synthesizers are sometimes common, particularly Moog-style sounds inspired by [Egg Punk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Egg_Punk "Egg Punk") groups like Gee Tee.

### Related Terminology

"Male Manipulator music" is a related internet slang term often overlapping with the Epunk fanbase. It refers to a canon of mainstream indie/alternative rock bands (e.g., Weezer, The Smiths, Radiohead, Deftones) that are stereotypically associated with "pretentious" or emotionally manipulative men. While musically distinct from the lo-fi punk of the Epunk genre, these bands share the same themes of male self-pity and romantic obsession, often appearing alongside Epunk tracks in "Doomer" or Incelcore playlists.

## Criticism

The Epunk/Incelcore genre is one of the most controversial internet aesthetics of the 2020s. The central criticism is the lyrical content, which often features violent fantasies about women (e.g., XO\_Willow's track referencing the murder of Bianca Devins) or glorifies school shootings (e.g., the Columbine massacre). Defenders argue this is "post-ironic" venting or satire of the incel mindset, while critics argue that ironic usage of alt-right language, hate-speech and symbols paves the way for serious alt-right / extremist presence in the scene.

The ADL and other extremism watchdogs such as the Atlanta Antifascists have noted that the genre often serves as a "soft" entry point to extremist "Blackpill" ideology. The 2025 Antioch shooter reportedly referenced Negative XP, highlighting the real-world danger of the subculture's rhetoric.

## Media

The Epunk/Incelcore aesthetic relies heavily on a curated list of films, video games, and graphic novels. These works are frequently sampled in songs, used as album art, or referenced lyrically to signal an alignment with themes of male alienation, nihilism, and "anti-hero" narratives.

### Films

* ***Scott Pilgrim vs. the World* (2010):** The central cultural touchstone of the genre, largely due to the Negative XP song "*Scott Pilgrim vs. the World Ruined a Whole Generation of Women*." The film's love interest, Ramona Flowers, is viewed by the subculture as the archetype of the "Manic Pixie Dream Girl" or "[E-Girl](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/E-Girl "E-Girl")" who manipulates the male protagonist, serving as a symbol for the community's romantic frustrations.
* ***Zero Day* (2003)** & ***Elephant* (2003)**: Films depicting school shootings (specifically utilizing the "found footage" style of the Columbine perpetrators) are frequently used in fan edits and music videos. The aesthetic fetishizes the "disturbed outcast" imagery found in these movies, aligning with the genre's early association with the "School Shooter" moniker.
* ***Joker* (2019)** & ***Taxi Driver* (1976)**: These films are central to the broader "Doomer" subculture, representing the "lonely man pushed too far by society." Imagery of the protagonists (Arthur Fleck and Travis Bickle), including Wojak depictions of them, is ubiquitous in the scene.
* ***Blade Runner 2049* (2017)** & ***Drive* (2011)**: Often referred to as "Literally Me" movies. Ryan Gosling's characters in these films represent the silent, stoic, and socially isolated male ideal that the community idolizes.

### Video Games

* ***Postal 2* (2003)**: A controversial FPS game known for its extreme violence and nihilistic humor. It is a main reference point for the genre's "edgy" irony and hatred of modern social norms.
* ***Hotline Miami* (2012)**: The game is popular among the Incelcore demographic for its [Neon Noir](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Neon_Noir "Neon Noir") aesthetic, hyper-violence, and themes of dissociation.
* ***Manhunt* (2003)**: Often referenced for its gritty, VHS-style violence and themes of urban decay.
