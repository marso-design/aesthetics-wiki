# Utopian Scholastic

**Utopian Scholastic** is an aesthetic that was prevalent from the late 1980s to the early-mid 2000s, manifesting primarily in the visual identity and presentation of edutainment media such as children's books, early digital encyclopedias, nature/science/history documentaries and scientific outreach programs. It has a significant overlap with other 1990s aesthetics, such as [Memphis Design](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Memphis_Design "Memphis Design")/[Memphis Lite](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Memphis_Lite "Memphis Lite"), [Global Village Coffeehouse](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Global_Village_Coffeehouse "Global Village Coffeehouse"), [Frasurbane](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Frasurbane "Frasurbane"), and [Neoclassical PoMo](https://cari.institute/aesthetics/neoclassical-pomo).

## History

Utopian Scholastic was used in 1990s and early 2000s edutainment software, encyclopedias mainly published by Dorling Kindersley, documentaries, children's science/social studies, and nature museums. This coincided with the rise of home computers and [CGI](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Silicon_Dreams "Silicon Dreams"). Common educational themes included wild animals (particularly dinosaurs), the solar system, natural phenomena, history (especially Ancient Egyptian), and geography.

In the mid-late 2000s, this aesthetic was replaced by [Frutiger Aero](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Frutiger_Aero "Frutiger Aero") in educational settings. In the 2020s, Utopian Scholastic has seen a revival in the [Vaporwave](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vaporwave "Vaporwave")/Utopian Virtual scene, by artists including Trndytrndy and Daniel White, but also as an object of nostalgia for early digital technology and, more broadly, the scientific curiosity of childhood.

## Visuals

Utopian Scholastic is often referred to visually as the "kid" version of [Frasurbane](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Frasurbane "Frasurbane"). This particular style which has been further popularized as "Kid Frasurbane" is characterized by a minimalist composition with a white background, classic-style typography, and incorporated pictures.

However, it was also manifested as a visual representation in an artistic style (related to [Romanticism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Romanticism "Romanticism")). The visual cues of the artistic (non-Frasurbane) style of Utopian Scholastic frequently include, but are not limited to:

* Depictions of natural phenomena or locations such as the sea, cloudy skies, storms, etc.
* Living organisms such as plants and animals
* A certain degree of [Surrealism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Surrealism "Surrealism") in the way elements are combined
* Early CGI 3D Models or environment ([Silicon Dreams](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Silicon_Dreams "Silicon Dreams")/[Early Cyber](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Early_Cyber "Early Cyber"))
* School supplies (ex. books, rulers, pencils, clocks, etc.)
* Mathematical and musical notes
* Scientific objects (ex. laboratory materials, atoms, periodic table elements, etc.)
* Classical architectural elements
* Historical artifacts of all kinds
* Patterns (ex. checkered floors, piano keys, etc.)
