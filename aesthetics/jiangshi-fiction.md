**Jiangshi (僵尸) films** are a horror-comedy and martial-arts film genre mainly established and popularized in Hong Kong cinema from the 1980s to the mid-1990s. This genre mixes Chinese folk horror, slapstick comedy, and kung-fu. Visually, it uses classic folk symbols such as Qing Dynasty official robes, yellow Taoist *[fulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulu)* (符箓), and glutinous rice.

## Visuals

Typical settings are **dim ancestral halls, desolate mortuary houses, walled courtyard mansions, rural Lingnan villages,** and **dark night forests**, all tied to Chinese ghost and funeral lore. Key recurring items are **yellow Taoist fulu, glutinous rice, peachwood swords, copper coins,** and **eight-trigram mirrors**, all taken from real Taoist exorcism traditions. The genre’s most iconic figure is the jiangshi, **a reanimated corpse in a formal Qing Dynasty mandarin robe with pale, green-tinged skin, rigid outstretched arms, and a fixed hopping movement caused by stiff limbs.**
