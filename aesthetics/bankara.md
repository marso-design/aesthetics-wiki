**Bankara** (バンカラ) was essentially the opposite of of [Taisho Roman](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Taisho_Roman "Taisho Roman"). They were opposed to so called haikara (“high collars") that were seen as becoming weak through westernizing. The word *Bankara* is formed by combining barbarian with haikara, describing the beliefs and personality of the people who were part of it. Bankara were defined by not being concerned with outward appearance: wearing ragged clothing, cloaks, and long hair. Masculinity, nationalism, and strength were core parts of the movement, and they were very similar to the militaristic views that led to World War 2.

While the movement ended by the 1950s, the fashion and ideology of the Bankara lived on and eventually evolved into the [Yanki](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Yanki "Yanki"). Ironically, the counter-culture ideas of masculinity also continued into movements such as the Japanese New Left. Additionally, various Japanese high school cheerleading teams have adopted the style as a uniform. Although the style has almost gone extinct, Bankara culture lives on through being passed down through these cheering teams.

## Ideology

The ideology of Bankara can be represented by "Ah, Flowers in a Jade Cup", a dormitory song for the First Higher School, the most prestigious prep school in Japan. Fascist leaders of Japan graduated from this school, such as Nobusuke Kishi.

I.

*嗚呼玉杯に花うけて　綠酒に月の影やどし*  
Ah, catching flowers in a jade cup, reflecting the moon in green wine.

* The song ironically opens with classical Chinese imagery. This is what these students are rejecting.

*治安の夢に耽りたる　榮華の巷低く見て'*
Lost in dreams of peace and order, we look down upon the streets of prosperity and glory.

* Refers to Tokyo and the masses.

*向ヶ岡にそゝりたつ　五寮の健児意気高し*  
Towering over the hill of Mukōgaoka, the valiant youths of the Five Dormitories stand tall.

* The "Five Dormitories" were completely self-governed by the students.

II.

*芙蓉の雪の精をとり　芳野の花の華を奪ひ*  
Taking the essence of the snow of Mt. Fuji, capturing the brilliance of Yoshino cherry blossoms,

* Japanese symbols representing loyalty to the Emperor.

*清き心の益良雄が　剣と筆とをとり持ちて*  
The noble men of pure heart, wielding both sword and pen,

* Refers to samurai concepts like Bunbu Ryōdō.

*一たび起たば何事か　人生の偉業成らざらん*  
Once they rise up, what thing could stop them? What great feat in life could they fail to accomplish?

* The students believed that they were destined by their intellect to accomplish great deeds.

III,

*濁れる海に漂へる　我國民を救はんと*  
Our people are adrift in the turbulent sea, intending to save them...

* Refers to Western Imperialism.

*逆巻く浪をかきわけて　自治の大船勇ましく*  
Parting the churning waves, the great ship of self-governance sails bravely on...

*尚武の風を帆にはらみ　船出せしより十二年*  
Catching the wind of martial valor in its sails, twelve years have passed since the ship set sail.

* Connecting the dorms to the martial prowess of the Empire. The song was made in 1902, the 12th anniversary of the dormitory.

IV.

*花咲き花はうつろひて　露おき露のひるがごと*  
Flowers bloom and fade, like dew that falls and vanishes in the morning light.
*星霜移り人は去り　梶とる舟師は變るとも*  
Seasons pass, people depart, and though the helmsman may change,

* Metaphors for individual lives.

*我のる船は常へに　理想の自治に進むなり*  
The ship we sail remains eternal, ever advancing toward ideal self-governance.

* The State is eternal.

V.
*行途を拒むものあらば　斬りて捨つるに何かある*  
If there be any who bar our path, what harm is there in cutting them down and casting them aside?

* Justifying aggressive imperialism, essentially Fascism.

*破邪の剣を抜き持ちて　舳に立ちて我よべば*  
Drawing the sword that vanquishes evil, when we stand at the bow and call out.

* Moral crusade to cleanse evil, justifying imperialism.

*魑魅魍魎も影ひそめ　金波銀波の海静か*  
The demons and monsters hide their shadows, and the sea of golden and silver waves becomes calm.

* The song concludes with an idea of a "Pax Japonica", bringing peace to the world.
