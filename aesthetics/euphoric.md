# Euphoric

_Also known as: Euphoria Aesthetic, Euphoriacore_

[Sensitive Content Notice](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Aesthetics_Wiki:Content_Warning_Policy "Aesthetics Wiki:Content Warning Policy") ⚠️

The following article contains and discusses content that may be distressing to some readers.  
**Reason for Warning:** This page discusses an aesthetic that is heavily associated with and criticized for the glamorization of drug abuse, alcohol abuse, self-harm, and other mature themes. Viewer discretion is advised.

The **Euphoric** aesthetic is a visual style and cultural phenomenon directly inspired by the cinematography, makeup, and fashion of the HBO teen drama series *Euphoria* (2019–present). The aesthetic is not a single, monolithic look but a collection of distinct character styles unified by a specific mood and visual style that is dark, moody, glamorous, and emotionally charged.

The style became a major cultural touchstone for Gen Z, with its expressive and often over-the-top approach to makeup and fashion spreading rapidly through social media platforms, especially TikTok. It is characterized by its dramatic use of color, light, glitter, and nostalgic fashion references, all used to visually represent the intense inner lives of the show's characters.

## Visuals

The foundation of the Euphoric aesthetic is the show's highly stylized cinematography. The visual style is defined by dramatic, moody lighting, often using a saturated color palette of deep blues, purples, and magentas. This creates a dreamlike, almost psychedelic atmosphere that blurs the line between reality and the characters' emotional states. The camera work is often dynamic and elaborate, with long, sweeping motion shots that contribute to the immersive and theatrical quality of the show's world.

## Fashion

The fashion of *Euphoria*, curated by costume designer Heidi Bivens, is an eclectic mix of styles that draws heavily from youth subcultures and nostalgic trends. The aesthetic is not one singular look, but rather a collection of distinct wardrobes that represent the different identities of the main characters.

* **Maddy's Y2K Style:** Embodies the "It-girl" persona with a wardrobe full of [Y2K](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Y2K "Y2K") and [McBling](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/McBling "McBling") influences. Matching sets, body-con dresses with dramatic cut-outs, and brands like I.AM define her look.GIA.
* **Rue's Skater Grunge Style:** An androgynous and lo-fi look that combines [Skater](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Skater "Skater") and [Grunge](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Grunge "Grunge") aesthetics. Her style is characterized by oversized t-shirts, vintage workwear, and her late father's signature burgundy zip-up hoodie.
* **Jules's Anime Punk Style:** An ethereal and playful look inspired by anime and 90s pop-punk. Her style involves extensive layering of sheer fabrics and mesh tops, often in a pastel color palette. Fezco, Rue's drug dealer, describes Jules, who came into his store, as looking "all *[Sailor Moon](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Magical_Girl "Magical Girl")* and shit."
* **Kat's Vamp Style:** Reflecting her journey of sexual self-discovery, Kat's style evolves into a "vampy" aesthetic. Corsets, leather, harnesses, chokers, and other elements of [BDSM Gothic fetish](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Bondage "Bondage")-inspired fashion define it.
* **Cassie's Cutesy Style:** A more traditionally feminine and revealing look, characterized by baby blue and soft pink colors, which describes her as the [soft, innocent girl next door](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Soft_Girl "Soft Girl"). Her outfits are designed to attract male attention, such as her viral pink cut-out swimsuit. In season 2, her clothing becomes more sluttier and chaotic and almost similar to Maddy and Jules’s style as she fights for Nate to even glance at her at school.
* **Lexi's Preppy Style:** A [Preppy](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Preppy "Preppy"), [Twee](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Twee "Twee"), and more conservative style that symbolizes her shy, quiet innocence and bookish manners, showing off that she's into her studies, in contrast to her sister Cassie, Rue, and peers' non-innocent behavior and clothing. Her style is showcased through tartan prints, Peter Pan collars, and knitted cardigans, as well as bold and bright colors throughout seasons 1 and 2, complemented by her red lipstick.

## Makeup

The makeup of *Euphoria*, created by makeup department head Doniella Davy, is arguably the most influential aspect of the aesthetic. It is an expressive and non-conformist style where makeup is used as a form of emotional storytelling rather than for simple beautification. The most iconic element is the liberal use of glitter, rhinestones, gems, and pearls, which are often applied around the eyes to create the effect of "glitter tears." Other key features include bold, graphic eyeliner, sharp lines, and the use of vibrant neon colors. Each character's makeup is distinct and evolves to represent their personal journey and psychological state.

## Criticism

The Euphoric aesthetic has faced criticism for its perceived glamorization of teenage trauma. Critics argue that the show's beautiful cinematography, high-fashion styling, and artistic makeup serve to aestheticize deeply serious issues like tobacco and drug addiction, violence, and mental health crises. The show has been accused of romanticizing self-destructive behavior for its young and impressionable audience by presenting these traumatic experiences in a visually appealing and "cool" manner.
