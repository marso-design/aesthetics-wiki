# DORFic

_Also known as: PolySunk_

Unofficial Name 🏷️

This article title is a retrospective or descriptive label coined by the wiki community for cataloging purposes. While you may not find this specific term used elsewhere, the aesthetic it describes is a verifiable real-world phenomenon supported by external visual evidence. It serves to document a cohesive style that lacks an official name in mainstream culture.

**DORFic** is an aesthetic featuring [Abstract Tech](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Abstract_Tech "Abstract Tech")-esque minimalist imagery that was popular from the mid-to-late 2000s to the mid 2010s. The term DORFic was coined on this wiki in April 2023 as an acronym for **Daylight, Orange, Red, Futurism, and Graphic** (shortened to "fic"), to document this specific, industrial subset of the [Frutiger Aero](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Frutiger_Aero "Frutiger Aero") design movement.

## History & Usage

Potential DORFic predecessors include [Retrofuturism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Retrofuturism "Retrofuturism") and [Atompunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Atompunk "Atompunk") of the [1960s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:1960s "Category:1960s"), and the rise of mobile technology and some street-art designs in the Late-[1990s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:1990s "Category:1990s"), DORFic began to become prominent in the Mid/Late-[2000s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:2000s "Category:2000s") and was predominantly used in video games and corporate design. It lasted until the Mid-[2010s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:2010s "Category:2010s"), 2016 being the last year DORFic saw any significant usage.

## Visuals

DORFic is industrial and highly minimalist, featuring stark white cityscapes primarily accented with bright oranges and grey. It borders on monochromatic clothing, including "techwear" and business casual in an effort to look sharp, clean, and casual. In some imagery, black takes the place of the white backgrounds, creating a dark alternate version of the aesthetic. This style of imagery tends to lean much more into [Cyberpunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cyberpunk "Cyberpunk") and [Dark Aero](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Aero "Dark Aero") rather than [Frutiger Aero](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Frutiger_Aero "Frutiger Aero") such as how the lighter imagery does.

Occasionally there is a presence of military and factory iconography, utilizing a balance between sterile scientific gear. Generally, DORFic largely overlaps with [Frutiger Aero](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Frutiger_Aero "Frutiger Aero") due to them both having a very sterile and corporate feel to them; however, DORFic can usually tends to focus more on industrialism, and often leans into more of an [Abstract Tech](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Abstract_Tech "Abstract Tech") style as opposed to the [nature](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Naturecore "Naturecore") characteristics of [Frutiger Aero](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Frutiger_Aero "Frutiger Aero"). DORFic has quite a few similarities to [Cyberpunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cyberpunk "Cyberpunk") as well due to their shared use of heavy tech, and sometimes corporate corruption, although DORFic is majorly more sanitized and less dependent on neon gear and holographics. Parallel and symmetric geometry also plays a large role, parts of explosive visuals and low polygonal composition is often central, especially in the communication of motion; it is best exemplified in 3D video designs.
