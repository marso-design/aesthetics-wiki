# Mid-Century Medieval

_Also known as: 1960s / 1970s Medieval Revival_

**Mid-Century Medieval** (also known as **Medieval Revival**) is an aesthetic that reimagines the visual iconography and fashion associated with the Middle Ages through kitschy mid-20th century designs. Originally emerging in the 1950s, the style rose to prominence in the [psychedelic era](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Psychedelia "Psychedelia") of the 1960s and 1970s, particularly in the U.S. and UK's [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie") scene and [Peacock Revolution](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Peacock_Revolution "Peacock Revolution"). The style was associated with psychedelic and progressive pop, folk and rock.

## History

During the 1950s, elements inspired by Renaissance and Middle Ages iconography were recontextualized with contemporary [kitschy](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Kitsch "Kitsch") designs which were often seen in media such as film and television, most notably in 1955's *The Court Jester*. By the mid-to late 1960s, the psychedelic era sparked a fashion movement known as the [Peacock Revolution](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Peacock_Revolution "Peacock Revolution") which led to widely liberalized styles of dress. Inspiring revivals of previous styles such as [Art Nouveau](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Art_Nouveau "Art Nouveau"), [Victorian](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Victorian "Victorian") and [Baroque](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Baroque "Baroque") fashions, along with the medieval era. In 1963, Phyllis and Ron Patterson held the Renaissance Pleasure Faire, an event meant to mimic an Elizabethan country fair, as a fundraiser for the radio station KPFK. It ended up being successful with eight thousand people attending the event, causing the rise of renaissance fairs, where people could participate in medieval-themed activities as a form as escapism.

## Visuals

The style is characterized by kitschy imagery inspired by the Middle Ages. The color palette consists of bright hues, mostly reds, yellows, greens and blues.

## Fashion

Mid-Century Medieval fashion is based on silhouettes from the Middle Ages, often combined with contemporary 60s/70s fashion elements.

* Flowing dresses/gowns
* Tunics
* Embroidery
* Cloaks
* Chokers
* Hoods and capes
* Trumpet sleeves
* Bodices
* Bell sleeves
* Short hemlines
