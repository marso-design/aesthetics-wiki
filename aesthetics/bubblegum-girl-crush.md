# Bubblegum Girl Crush

**Bubblegum Girl Crush** is a K-pop subconcept of Girl Crush, blending the high-energy, vibrant sweet sound and pastel-colored aesthetics of "bubblegum pop" with the brash, fierce and confident sound/attitude of the "[Girl Crush](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Girl_Crush "Girl Crush")" genre. It features catchy, upbeat music paired with polished, stylish, and confident visuals, popularised by groups like Girls' Generation "I Got a Boy", After School "AH", 4Minute "Hot Issue" and f(x) "Electric Shock".

## History

The "Bubblegum Girl Crush" concept in K-pop emerged in the 2010s as a trendy fusion of the Bubblegum sound with the Girl Crush concept, which emphasizes confidence, rebellion, and female empowerment. This blend was popularized by groups and artists such as 4Minute with songs like "Hot Issue," f(x) "Electric Shock" and "NU ABO," and Girls' Generation with "I Got A Boy," trending this crossover. Bubblegum Girl Crush also acted as an more Pop accessible, counteract to it's raw, darker and hoydenish/androgynous predecessor due to it's controversial, brash unladylike qualities with bold avant-garde visusls that typically rebelled against the very strict social beauty standards of the industry.

The Girl Crush concept itself began gaining recognition following the debut of 2NE1, or especially on a more global scale after the their iconic release "I Am The Best," highlighting themes of androgyny and strong female identity, and has since evolved through various styles, from hoydenish and androgynous looks, to more hyperfeminine or royal and diva-like presentations, while the term "Bubblegum Girl Crush" specifically refers to the combination of sweet and vibrant, pop catchy upbeat EDM sounds with raw Girl Crush tendencies, it's part of a broader trend that has seen K-pop girl groups exploring diverse concepts to connect with their audiences.

Though Bubblegum Girl Crush peak in popularity during the 2010s era of Kpop, and has since then fizzled away from modern trends, a new trend with very similar qualities known as "[Teen Crush](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Teen_Crush "Teen Crush")," aka the bratty teenage offspring to traditional Girl Crush, has took the Kpop world by storm during the 2020s era, sparked by "ITZY" and their brat-like prowls known as the Teen Crush wave. However, though the 2 concepts share vibrant elements and birth from traditional Girl Crush, Teen Crush is more kick-ass and delivers a far less sweet, with a overall sour bitter taste and flavor with a hyper-looney, bratty rebellious sound and intensity.

## Visuals & Fashion

Bubblegum Girl Crush merges bright, saccharine aesthetics with a bold styling and confidence edgy attitude. Visually, this style features vibrant pastel colors (specifically bubblegum pink and white), Y2K-inspired fashion, high-energy, polished, and stylish looks. Key elements include glitter, candy themes, roller skates, and a mix of soft, feminine, and tomboyish, rebellious, and sometimes punk-influenced clothing with bubblegum elements. A mix of "coquette" (bows, pastel colors) with edgy "Girl Crush" fashion like avant-garde streetwear, denim, and sometimes elements from 2000s American pop culture. Also, some groups who anticipated in Bubblegum Girl Crush, incorporated darker wardrobe fashions as well, but usually correlating with bubblegum color palette settings and themes surrounding it.

## Music

Musically, Bubblegum Girl Crush is an addictive masterfully blend of sonically sweet, candy Bubblegum sounds/cheeful deliveries, with the bold and brash essence of the Girl Crush aesthetic, characterized by its infectious candy sweet melodies, upbeat rhythms with a bit of Girl Crush abrasiveness, and lyrics that exude confidence, independence, and a dash of sass and brash. The music often features catchy Pop hooks, playful vocal runs, and a fusion of gritty electronics/EDM elements that make it impossible to get out of your head. Lyrically, Bubblegum Girl Crush songs often focus on themes of self-empowerment, female friendship, embracing individuality, or even love (unlike traditional Girl Crush), all delivered with a vibrant and cheeky, unapologetic attitude.

## Comparisons

Often as of late, Bubblegum Girl Crush and Teen Crush have received a fair share of heavy comparison amongst Kpop fans, since both are known for being a vibrant colorful version of traditional Girl Crush, with newer 2020s Kpop fans often claiming many Bubblegum Girl Crush tracks could pass off as Teen Crush in modern Kpop.

However, Teen Crush and Bubblegum Girl Crush differ primarily in attitude and sound; with Teen Crush offering more of a "Teenage" brash, relentless, rebellious, and chaotic "bratty" sound like raging, raving "cheer squads" (e.g., ITZY, NMIXX, Kelper and Weki Meki,) while Bubblegum Girl Crush blends more of a tamed, sugary upbeat bubblegum sound, and brightly colored pop aesthetics with abrasive EDM productions, but still has more of an mature vibe and delivery/vocal delivery than what Teen Crush has offered.

The difference between the 2 further becomes very apparent, when some Teen Crush groups wear darker, edgier wardrobe fashions and themes for "revo song" purposes or revo style Teen Crush anthems; (e.g., Loona - So What, Kepler - We Fresh, WA-DA-DA, Yum, as well as ITZY - Voltage, Ringo or Blah-Blah-Blah.)
