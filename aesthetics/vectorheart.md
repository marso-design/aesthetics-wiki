# Vectorheart

**Vectorheart** is an aesthetic that emerged in the mid-late [1990s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:1990s "Category:1990s"). It is characterized by striking vector shapes, 45- and/or 60-degree diagonal lines, futuristic fonts, and flat, (usually) high contrast colors. The exact origin of the Vectorheart aesthetic is hard to define, but pioneers of the style include design firms such as [Bionic Systems](https://www.bionic-systems.com/) and [The Designers Republic](https://www.thedesignersrepublic.com/). Often considered a subgenre of [Y2K Futurism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Y2K_Futurism "Y2K Futurism"), Vectorheart grew in popularity during the early-[2000s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:2000s "Category:2000s"), but went out of fashion in the mainstream by the early-[2010s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:2010s "Category:2010s"). Since then, it has seen [some small resurgence beginning in the late-2010s](https://cari.institute/aesthetics/neo-vectorheart). It was popular alongside [Vectordelia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vectordelia "Vectordelia"), [Vectorflourish](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vectorflourish "Vectorflourish"), [Gen X Soft Club](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Gen_X_Soft_Club "Gen X Soft Club"), and other aesthetics of the [2000s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:2000s "Category:2000s").

The *Wipeout* series, in which the Designers Republic was involved with for the first three games, is commonly associated with Vectorheart and thus the aesthetic is also occassionally known as Wipeout Design.

## History

### 1990s

The exact origin of the Vectorheart aesthetic is hard to define, but pioneers of the style include design firms such as [Bionic Systems](https://www.bionic-systems.com/) and [The Designers Republic](https://www.thedesignersrepublic.com/). Vectorheart began to appear in the Late-[1990s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:1990s "Category:1990s"), often associated with [Y2K Futurism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Y2K_Futurism "Y2K Futurism") due to its sleek, cutting edge style. This style complimented the futurism that Y2K Futurism promoted, leading to a lot of overlap between the two. Vectorheart also strongly resembles [Gen X Soft Club](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Gen_X_Soft_Club "Gen X Soft Club"), another [Y2K](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Y2K "Y2K") subgenre, incorporating the same abstract flourishes and design philosophy.

### 2000s

Vectorheart maintained its popularity into the Early-[2000s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:2000s "Category:2000s"), and the into the Mid-[2000s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:2000s "Category:2000s"). During the Mid-[2000s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:2000s "Category:2000s"), other adjacent aesthetics such a Y2K Futurism began to fall into decline. Despite this, Vectorheart maintained its popularity into the [2010s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:2010s "Category:2010s"), being popular alongside aesthetics like [Vectordelia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vectordelia "Vectordelia") and [Vectorflourish](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vectorflourish "Vectorflourish"). During this time, Vectorheart was used for purposes like advertising, art, web design, magazine covers, and more.

### Early-2010s

Starting at the Early-[2010s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:2010s "Category:2010s"), Vectorheart saw a considerable decline in its popularity. This was due to design philosophies such as [Flat Design](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Flat_Design "Flat Design") becoming mainstream. [Flat Design](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Flat_Design "Flat Design") largely omitted the visually complex and cutting edge visuals of Vectorheart for a more simple and easy to understand look. This, combined with other adjacent aesthetics such as [Vectorflourish](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vectorflourish "Vectorflourish") seeing decline, contributed to Vectorheart falling out of mainstream usage and popularity.

## Visuals

Vectorheart is characterized by striking vector shapes, 45- and/or 60-degree diagonal lines, futuristic fonts, and flat, (usually) high contrast colors. The style is loosely inspired by Swiss [modernism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Modernism "Modernism"), utilitarian industrial design, brutalism, and to an extent, Peter Saville's album cover designs for Factory Records. The look is considered maximum-minimalist by some, and is often mixed with the typical look of Y2K Futurism, [Gen X Soft Club](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Gen_X_Soft_Club "Gen X Soft Club"), and [Metalheart](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Metalheart "Metalheart"). Vectorheart's sleek maximum-minimalist design philosophy led to it being popular in depictions of the future, or things that were trying to look futuristic.

In addition, many Vectorheart artists, most notably the Designers Republic, often incorporate references to Japanese culture, for example kanji characters and ties to anime-influenced artwork. Vectorheart may also contain themes of anti-establishment and anti-consumerism, and it is not uncommon for Vectorheart artists to include exaggerated depictions of consumer culture, for example the over-abundance of logos, in-your-face advertising and liberal usage of trademark (™) and copyright (© and ®) symbols, as seen in the *Wipeout* games.
