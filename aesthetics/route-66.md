# Route 66

_Also known as: Route 66 Kitsch, Mid-Century Roadside Americana, Mother Road Aesthetic, Radiator Springs (pop-culture reference)_

**Route 66** is a visual aesthetic centered on the romanticized era of American autotourism along the historic [U.S. Route 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_66 "wikipedia:U.S. Route 66") (decommissioned in 1985). The style primarily reflects the short-lived boom of roadside culture that flourished from the 1950s to the 1960s. The aesthetic is a nostalgic celebration of freedom, autonomy, and the kitschy, exaggerated architectural styles designed to lure the newly mobile American traveler off the highway.

The aesthetic often overlaps with [Greaser](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Greaser "Greaser") culture and the broader [Hot Rod](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hot_Rod "Hot Rod")/[Kustom Kulture](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Kustom_Kulture "Kustom Kulture"), utilizing motifs like classic cars and "greasy spoon" diners.

## History

### Cultural Context

The aesthetic romanticizes the period when the Mother Road was the symbol of westward expansion and the American Dream; the promise of a better life in California, particularly for Dust Bowl migrants (cemented by The Grapes of Wrath). Today, its appeal is not necessarily based on genuine nostalgia, but rather the appreciation of [kitsch](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Kitsch "Kitsch")—the visible, commodified imitation of a simpler past, which has itself become a unique modern tourist attraction.

## Visual Aesthetic

The visual canon of the Route 66 aesthetic is defined by the juxtaposition of the vast, dusty American Southwest with garish, eye-catching artificial structures.

### Roadside Architecture

The core iconography of the aesthetic is based on attention-grabbing architecture designed to be seen at high speeds:

* **Programmatic/Mimetic Architecture:** Buildings intentionally constructed to resemble the product they sold (e.g., hot dogs, donuts, enormous catsup bottles) or regional elements (like **teepee motels**).
* **[Googie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Googie "Googie") Design:** A futuristic, exaggerated architectural style featuring sharp angles, starbursts, and swooping parabolic curves, often seen in diners and service stations to communicate technological optimism.
* **Giant Statues:** Oversized figures, such as the famous fiberglass **Muffler Men**, used as quirky landmarks to generate tourist curiosity.

### Color and Iconography

The palette juxtaposes natural and artificial colors. **Neon signs** are paramount, with highly detailed, often animated mid-century signage contrasted against the deep black of the desert night. The aesthetic is otherwise dominated by a desert palette of dusty browns and reds, accented by the **pastel hues (pink, mint green)** and **chrome silver** of the period's automobiles.

## Music

The aesthetic is heavily associated with the earliest forms of rock 'n' roll and music that explicitly celebrates the American road trip.

### Songs

The sonic backdrop is defined by the early Rock 'n' Roll era (1950s) and instrumental music that evokes the feeling of driving:

* "Route 66" by Nat King Cole (the definitive song of the route)
* "Rock Around the Clock" by Bill Haley & His Comets
* "Shake, Rattle and Roll" by Big Joe Turner / Bill Haley
* "Life is a Highway" by Rascal Flatts (modern anthem of road travel)
* "On the Road Again" by Willie Nelson
* Early works by Elvis Presley, Chuck Berry, and Jerry Lee Lewis
