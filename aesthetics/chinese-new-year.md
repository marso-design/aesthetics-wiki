# Chinese New Year

_Also known as: Chunjie, Spring Festival_

The **Chinese New Year** (新年, pinyin: Xīnnián), also known as the ***Spring Festival*** (春节, pinyin: Chūnjié), is the most important holiday celebrated by the Chinese culture. It involves many traditional celebrations, decorations, and foods that are based on bringing luck in the new year, as determined by the Chinese lunisolar calendar established since ancient times.

Aesthetically, the holiday is very consistent with its use of red and gold, which are colors said to scare away evil spirits and represent wealth. And like other holidays, because of the highly traditional activities, the celebrations are consistent throughout the Chinese diaspora with a certain air of festivity.

## History

*Under Construction*  
Red embodies yang. From pre‑Qin antiquity through the Han Dynasty, red was used during the New Year to subdue yin energy, dispel the bad luck of the outgoing year, and welcome the yang energy of the new spring. Beginning in the Tang Dynasty, red paper replaced peach wood for writing blessing inscriptions. The imperial court granted red silk and red prayer slips for New Year celebrations. No longer only used to ward off evil spirits, red took on meanings of liveliness and festivity. In the Ming Dynasty, the [Hongwu Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongwu_Emperor) decreed that every household must paste Spring Festival couplets written on red paper on New Year’s Eve. Since then, red has become the definitive visual symbol of the New Year.

The name Spring Festival was first used during the Republic of China to distinguish the Chinese New Year from the Gregorian New Year. The most typical lantern style used for the New Year, nicknamed the 'big red lantern' (大红灯笼), was designed in 1949 for the Proclamation of the People's Republic of China, and later became widely used in all Chinese communities.

Through industrialization, New Year decorations were quickly commercialized. Today's Spring Festival sees a festive vibe intertwined with vibrant consumption. From delicious feasts to cultural tourism, from offline bazaars to online shopping, the advanced manufacturing and logistics industries have helped unify the New Year aesthetic across China, making it a specific, reproducible aesthetic. One of which is the gold color, which only became widely used in all forms of New Year decorations after the implement of modern gold foil stamping.

## Philosophy

*Construction Needed*

* **Wealth** and **abundance**. The tradition of **renewal** and the **ritual consumption** it evolved into.
* **Festivity** and **celebration**. The Spring Festival allows people to play non-daily roles through festive decorations and celebratory activities. The Chinese New Year aesthetic has helped other eye-catching aesthetics that draw on traditional Chinese culture, such as *Hanfu*, gain mainstream recognition.
* As the world's largest human migration, one core philosophy of the Spring Festival is **returning home**: confirming an individual's place within the kinship network through crowding, fatigue, and eventual **reunion**. The Chinese New Year aesthetic romanticize the physical gathering of the family as the highest form of happiness. Even if there are family conflicts, one must present impeccable completeness in the New Year's Eve group photo.
* **Auspiciousness** and **bringing fortune**, with many visuals associated with symbolism and puns. From the New Year greetings of the national leaders to the Spring Festival Gala organized by the government, grand narratives are used to celebrate a **flourishing era**.
* The Spring Festival is a manifestation of **Confucian 'rituals.'** The seating arrangement for the New Year's Eve dinner, the order of elders and juniors in paying New Year visits, and the ancestral worship ceremonies all center around Confucian values of **hierarchy, filial piety, and harmonious order.**
* **The Chinese identity**. The aesthetics of the Spring Festival constructs an common community among heterogeneous Chinese cultures (such as Cantonese, Hakka, and Hokkien) through symbols such as lion dances and Spring Festival couplets, especially for overseas Chinese.

### Lanterns

Large lanterns are hung under the eaves on the two sides of the door, on lampposts, and scenic areas. Hand held lanterns are hung from a stick. The *deng-hui* (灯会) lantern fairs are held on Yuanxiao, the final day of the Spring Festival celebrations, with lantern art of motifs described in the "Patterns" section above, and rows of hanging lanterns written with different riddles.

**The big red lantern** is characterised by an oval shape, vertical frames, and usually yellow tassels.

***Gong-deng* (宫灯) lanterns** are characterised by hexagonal or octagonal frames of wood, painted silk/glass panels, and multiple tassels at the corners

A ***zouma-deng* (走马灯) lantern** is a gong-deng decorated with patterns that spins.

## Foods

Many of the dishes are known for being auspicious because of their names being puns that sound like phrases that bring luck.

* **New Year Eve's dinner**: Dishes of the New Year's Eve vary among regions and the listed foods below may not be consumed in all Chinese communities, but are simply widely recognised as iconic New Year food.
  + Dumplings（餃子; the shape resembles the *yuanbao*)
  + Fish served whole
  + Nian gao (年糕）
  + Whole chicken
  + Eight treasure rice (八宝饭)
  + *Tangyuan* (汤圆) or *yuanxiao* (元宵)
* Snacks:
  + Togetherness tray (全盒)
  + Oranges and other citrus fruits
  + Spring rolls
  + Tanghulu (糖葫芦）

## Fashion

*To be improved*

Most merchants will launch Spring Festival-themed clothing every year. Chinese clothing is often worn at this time, such as with fancier *Qipao*, *Tangzhuang*, and the rising *Hanfu*. Traditional Chinese Spring Festival symbolism is the most important aspect of the fashion. Silk is seen in most traditional Chinese aesthetic. As the festival is at the turn of spring and winter, warmth is another main motif. Woven wool, down feathers, faux fur, and thick cotton are seen. This also makes the Ming-style *Hanfu* a widely accepted part of the aesthetic. Wear clothes that are easy to move in. The mix of ancient and modern styles, such as *[Guochao](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Guochao "Guochao")* and [Neo-Chinese](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Neo-Chinese_Style "Neo-Chinese Style") styling, would be appreciated. The Chinese New Year fashion especially involves dressing up one's children in newly bought or made traditional clothing.

## Activities

On Chinese new year, people typically attend festivals with all sorts of things, like **lion dancing, lighting fireworks/fire crackers, and light lanterns.** Young children also receive **Red Envelope**, or **Hongbao (红包）**from elders, aunts and uncles, and parents. Inside a Red Envelope is usually currency.

At home, family and friends gather around for dinner as they share food.

Children sometimes **light sparklers and throw cherry bombs**.

On the night of Yuanxiao, lanterns, usually lotus lanterns, are set afloat on rivers to honor ancestors and send blessings for the future.
