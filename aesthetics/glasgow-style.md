# Glasgow Style

_Also known as: Glasgow School_

The **Glasgow Style** (also known as **Glasgow School**) was a distinctive regional variant of the [Art Nouveau](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Art_Nouveau "Art Nouveau") movement that flourished in Glasgow, Scotland, from the 1890s to the start of World War I. Centered around the Glasgow School of Art, its most famous proponents were a collaborative group known as "The Four": Charles Rennie Mackintosh, Margaret Macdonald Mackintosh, Frances Macdonald, and Herbert MacNair. The style synthesized a wide range of influences, including the [Arts and Crafts](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Arts_and_Crafts "Arts and Crafts") movement, the Celtic Revival, and [Japonisme](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Japonisme "Japonisme").

While it is part of the broader Art Nouveau trend, the Glasgow Style is distinguished by several differences; Whereas mainstream European Art Nouveau often featured ornate, flowing, and naturalistic curves, the Glasgow Style is characterized by a stronger emphasis on geometric forms, vertical lines, and elongated, ethereal human figures. Its color palette was also more restrained, favoring subtle, symbolic arrangements of purples, greens, and rose-pinks over the more vibrant colors seen elsewhere.

## Key Artists and Works

One of the most prominent artists of the **Glasgow Style** was Scottish architect, designer, water colourist and artist **Charles Rennie Mackintosh** (1868-1928). Working alongside his wife **Margaret MacDonald Mackintosh**, they produced works through a various range of media, from stained glass to furnitures and posters, only to cite a few examples. He created Glasgow School of Art's artists group "**The Four"**, composed of the couple, **Frances MacDonald** (sister of Margaret) and **James Herbert MacNair** (husband of Frances). They are together the most famous artists of the Glasgow Style, internationnaly recognized. Charles Rennie Mackintosh notably designed the new building of the Glasgow School of Art in 1909.
