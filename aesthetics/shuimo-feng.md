# Shuimo Feng

_Also known as: Ink-wash style, Shuimo Feng_

**Shuimo Feng** (水墨风) is an design aesthetic rooted in traditional Chinese [ink painting and literati art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuimo). Centered on ink gradation, *xieyi* (写意) freehand brushwork, negative space and minimalist subtlety, it features muted monochrome tones (black, white, grey) and soft natural textures. It pursues serene, implicit and natural artistic conception.

## History

*Needs Work*  
Ink-wash Feng originates from traditional Chinese ink wash painting. In the Tang Dynasty, Wu Daozi and Wang Wei established the systematic techniques of ink wash art, marking the formal formation of the ink-wash style.

During the Republic of China era (1912–1949), Chinese art underwent a period of rapid modernization and cultural experimentation. The [Lingnan School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingnan_School) became one of the most influential movements of the period, combining classical ink painting with Western realism, perspective, and contemporary subject matter. At the same time, artists such as [Xu Beihong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_Beihong) promoted the integration of European academic techniques into Chinese art. The resulting fusion of expressive ink painting and modern artistic ideas laid much of the foundation for the aesthetics later associated with Shuimo Feng.

After the founding of the People’s Republic of China in 1949, state-sponsored art programs encouraged innovation that honored cultural heritage while embracing modern media. In the 1960s and 80s, pioneering animators at the Shanghai Animation Film Studio began adapting classical ink wash aesthetics into short films, emphasizing fluid brushwork, and atmospheric landscapes. These works became some of the most celebrated achievements of Chinese animation and are widely regarded in China as cultural classics.

The continued popularity of ink-wash-inspired aesthetics in modern Chinese media can be traced in part to the lasting cultural impact and nostalgic appeal of these landmark animated films, which helped introduce traditional brush-and-ink imagery to generations of viewers in a modern context. With digital development in modern times, it further crossed boundaries, expanding to films, graphic design, fashion, cultural creation and all modern design fields worldwide. Ink-wash Feng greatly inspired the [Neo-Chinese Style](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Neo-Chinese_Style "Neo-Chinese Style").

## Philosophy

The core value of ink wash painting lies in its *xieyi* freehand spirit. It pursues not literal likeness of objects, but the expression of verve and emotion. It substitutes emptiness for solid form and leaves imagination to viewers through negative space. Modern Ink-wash Feng design aligns with minimalist, low saturation and blank layout trends, fitting diverse design scenarios for wide acceptance.

## Visuals

Ink-wash Feng takes **black** and **white** as the basic palette, sometimes using soft, **muted** traditional mineral colors like ochre, indigo and cinnabar as accents.

For layout and space design, it focuses on **leaving blank areas**. Borrowing from **ink wash blending** methods and the five-scale ink tones, the designs create a strong contrast between empty space and solid painted elements.

The themes come from Chinese natural scenery and cultural symbols, such as **mountains and rivers, unique rocks, slender bamboos, flowers and birds, floating clouds** and **ancient temples**.

On top of that, it sometimes uses traditional **seals** and short poetic **calligraphy** lines as decorations.
