# Nyashka

_Also known as: Няшка, Drain-Nyashka (дрейн-няшка)_

**Nyashka** (Russian: Няшка) is an alternative style originating in Russia and Ukraine, centered around a highly specific makeup look drawing from anime aesthetics and applied against the backdrop of post-Soviet urban life. The style bears surface resemblances to the Western [E-Girl](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/E-Girl "E-Girl") and the Japanese [Jirai Kei](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Jirai_Kei "Jirai Kei"), sharing their doll-like facial construction and alt-fashion sensibility, but it developed independently within Slavic internet and street subculture rather than as a derivative of either.

The term "няшка" is Russian internet slang for "cutie" or "adorable person," derived from the Japanese onomatopoeia "*nya*" (にゃ), the sound a cat makes, combined with the Russian diminutive suffix "*-shka*," giving the word an intimate and affectionate register common in Russian naming conventions.

## History

### Etymology

The word "няшка" entered Russian youth internet slang through anime fandom communities, where the Japanese "nya," a cat's meow used expressively across manga and animation, was absorbed into vernacular use and given the Russian diminutive treatment. By the early 2010s, it was broadly used as a compliment meaning something like "adorable little one," applied to people, animals, or characters perceived as exceptionally cute.

A 2010 Russian forum thread records early debate over whether the term described a subculture or merely a borrowed slang word, with many respondents insisting it was the latter: simply an anime-influenced term of endearment for a girl who dressed in a cute, doll-like way with bows and baby-doll makeup. Over time, however, a recognizable fashion and makeup style coalesced around the term, particularly as Russian alt subcultures like Drain absorbed and transformed it into the more specifically styled phenomenon documented here.

### Origins

The style's clearest visual antecedent within Russian internet culture is the broader anime fandom community, where the term "няшка" was already circulating as a compliment before any coherent subculture formed around it. As [Drain](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Draincore "Draincore") aesthetics spread through Russian VKontakte communities and Telegram from around 2021 onward, a hybridized figure emerged: the "drain-nyashka," in which the melancholic Drain style combines with cute, doll-like elements such as enlarged eyes, full lips, lace, and bows. This fusion gave the makeup look its distinctive tension: the cuteness of the kawaii-influenced face against the bleakness and apathy of Drain's underlying ethos.

The peak of nyashka's domestic visibility in Russia came in 2023, coinciding with the broader subcultural conflict between [PMC Ryodan](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/PMC_Ryodan "PMC Ryodan") (Редан), a loosely organized anime-affiliated youth movement taking its name from the criminal organization in the manga *Hunter x Hunter*, and "*offniki*," a term for football-hooligan-adjacent youth whose dress code centered on Adidas tracksuits and Stone Island tags. The conflict made it to the national news after a fight at the Aviapark shopping center in Moscow in February 2023, after which a wave of similar altercations spread across Russian cities.

Nyashka girls were socially positioned within the [Ryodan](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/PMC_Ryodan "PMC Ryodan") milieu, alongside "[dead inside](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dead_Inside "Dead Inside")" (мертвые внутри), another style within this circle characterized by a similar black-and-white palette but without the canonical makeup. One Russian commenter in the TikTok comment section of @wearydoll's viral makeup tutorial noted that nyashkas were already declining in number domestically by the time Western audiences discovered the style, describing the international attention as belated.

The style was introduced to English-language audiences primarily through TikTok in 2024–2025, via makeup tutorials by creators such as @wearydoll (Scarlett) and @addyharajuku, which collectively generated hundreds of thousands of views and sparked debate about cultural context and appropriation.

## Fashion

The nyashka look is built first and foremost around the face. The foundation is applied extremely pale, approaching white, intended less as a color match to the wearer's skin and more as a blank porcelain base from which the rest of the makeup is constructed. The eyes are the most characteristic element: large black scleral or enlarging circle lenses dramatically increase the apparent size of the iris, producing the wide and unblinking quality of an anime character or antique doll. These are framed by heavy exaggerated winged eyeliner, often extending far outward from the outer corner of the eye, and dense false lashes above and below. The eyebrows, by contrast, are kept thin, straight, and flat, running nearly horizontal across the brow bone rather than arching, which contributes significantly to the characteristic blankness of the expression. Lips are typically finished in deep burgundy or black. Some practitioners draw additional markings on the face, such as small dots, stars, or symbols, and facial piercings, particularly around the nose and lips, are common.

The clothing associated with nyashka is largely inherited from the broader Drain subculture and Russian alt fashion more generally. Oversized black and white hoodies, wide-leg trousers, and Adidas-brand tracksuits appear frequently, grounding the look in the everyday aesthetic of Eastern European youth rather than the more costumelike register of its Japanese counterparts. This contrast of a porcelain doll-like face against mundane post-Soviet streetwear, often photographed in brutalist housing blocks or underpasses, is one of the most visually distinctive qualities of the style.

On more dressed-up iterations, lace-trim skirts, fishnets, and tennis skirts appear alongside the hoodies, and accessories run to chunky silver chains, cross pendants, chokerstyled necklaces, and rings. Hair is almost always long and straight, usually dyed a deep black or occasionally bleached, sometimes with a blunt fringe framing the face.

## Criticism

Following its spread to Western TikTok, Nyashka became the subject of accusations of "Asian fishing"; a term used to describe non-Asian people adopting makeup techniques or styling associated with East Asian beauty trends in order to appear Asian, or to approximate an Asian appearance for aesthetic effect. Critics argued that the extreme eye-enlarging techniques and pale base used in nyashka makeup were directly borrowed from Korean or Japanese beauty practices and constituted a form of racial imitation when practiced by white or non-Asian women.

Responses to this criticism were varied. On a YouTube comment section, Asian commenters pushed back on the accusation, arguing that the makeup's goal was to produce a doll-like appearance rather than an ethnic one, and that the style's anime-derived origins were broadly shared across Eurasian cultures rather than belonging to any specific ethnic group. Several Russian commenters also challenged the premise on geographical and demographic grounds, noting that Russia is a transcontinental state with a substantial indigenous Asian population, and that Japanese and broader East Asian cultural influence had been woven into Russian youth culture for decades through the anime fandom. One Russian native noted in the comment section that the nyashka style was considered self-evidently Russian in its country of origin and that its anime inspirations reflected longstanding cultural exchange rather than appropriation.
