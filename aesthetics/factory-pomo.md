# Factory Pomo

**Factory Pomo** (sometimes stylized as **Factory PoMo**) is an aesthetic that was prevalent from the late 1980s to mid-1990s. Emerging from the 80s-90s postmodern explosion launched by [Memphis Design](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Memphis_Design "Memphis Design"), it was prominently featured in industrial and graphic design of the time. This aesthetic often overlapped with [Early Cyber](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Early_Cyber "Early Cyber"), [Cyberdelia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cyberdelia "Cyberdelia"), [Silicon Dreams](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Silicon_Dreams "Silicon Dreams"), [Memphis Lite](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Memphis_Lite "Memphis Lite"), [Decoplex](https://cari.institute/aesthetics/decoplex), and [Wacky Pomo](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Wacky_Pomo "Wacky Pomo").

## Visuals

Factory Pomo was heavily influenced by [emerging CAD computer graphics](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Silicon_Dreams "Silicon Dreams"), and combined PoMo ideas with early-to-mid 20th century visuals (i.e. [Art Deco](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Art_Deco "Art Deco"), [Constructivism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Constructivism "Constructivism"), [Bauhaus](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Bauhaus "Bauhaus"), [Raygun Gothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Raygun_Gothic "Raygun Gothic"), [Streamline Moderne](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Streamline_Moderne "Streamline Moderne"), WPA imagery). The resulting aesthetic typically featured futuro-industrial elements like gears and metal ridging, contrasting colors, a certain emphasis on symmetry often combined with inverting colors, and strong use of simple shapes.

### Rides

Theme park attractions of the 1990s often used the Factory Pomo aesthetic. One prominent example of Factory Pomo was *New Tomorrowland*, which opened at Walt Disney World's Magic Kingdom park in December 1994. Located in the Tomorrowland section was *The ExtraTERRORestrial Alien Encounter*. Disney would further capitalize on this in 1998 with DisneyQuest, a virtual reality entertainment center located in Walt Disney World's *Downtown Disney*, with a second location in Chicago, Illinois. They also had the *ESPN Zone* dining and interactive experiences in major cities.

On the other side of the pond, Sega opened Sega World Sydney in Darling Harbour, Australia, and Sega World London at the Trocadero in London. Sega also partnered with *DreamWorks* to establish *GameWorks*, a series of arcades found at major cities.
