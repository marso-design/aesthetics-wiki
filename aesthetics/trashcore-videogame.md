*This page is about the videogame aesthetic that often mixes oversaturated colours and crude artstyles with unrealistic horror and gore. For the aesthetic relating to urban pollution and technological decay, see Trashcore.*

**Trashcore** is an aesthetic that describes videogames with a particularly harsh or bizarre visual style, palette or story that is designed to make the player feel uncomfortable, confused or out of their depth. Some Trashcore games use these aspects and this term with the intent of making something bizarre, though many games are also labelled as trashcore due to their creators deeming them to be literally or figuratively trash, regardless of if they match the previously-mentioned description.

Games that follow the Trashcore aesthetic tend to contain violent, sexual and shocking language/content that ranges from completely unserious and silly to unnecessarily descriptive and vulgar. This content is not required for a game to be Trashcore, though it is an aspect that many trashcore games have since their goal is to bizzare, confuse or frustrate the player.

## History

Games that fit the Trashcore style have existed since at least the early 2000's. One of these is revenge of the sunfish, a game made in 2007 by Jinxtengu. The game follows the journey of the player as they attempt to stop an invasion of evil alien sunfish from the planet Xenitropoxiqouqinx. The game is a mishmash of various partially-completed platformers and top-down shooters, most of which have vastly different (but equally garish) appearances to one another. The game's soundtrack contains various midi rips from other videogames, such as Castlevania and Mega Man, as well as some original music that makes an earnest attempt to deafen the player. It is impossible to die in Revenge of the sunfish, as doing so either restarts the room or progresses you to a new area after a certain number of fails. This means that the game has somewhat of a branching storyline, though you always get the same ending.

The earliest game to describe itself as "Trashcore" on Itch.io was WHY NOT by Tak, a text adventure where you'd use the left and right arrow keys to choose between two options in order to progress. The plot of the game is entirely nonsensical, with the player going from location to location without much explanation as to how they ended up there. the constantly-shifting rainbow graphics and the music that sounds like it's playing from another room only adds to the disorientation. This game released in 2014 and recieved its last update in 2017. A majority of Trashcore games during this timeframe were made by Tak.

The most well-known game that falls into the aesthetic of Trashcore is Cruelty Squad by the solo developer Consumer Softproducts, a first-person shooter where you play as a hitman working for a death squad that kills corporate rivals for its host company. The graphics of Cruelty Squad are done using painfully bright colours alongside miserably dingy ones, as well as a variety of strange textures in both its environment and UI. Cruelty Squad released in 2021 and is the most well-known example of a game with the Trashcore aesthetic.

## Visuals

Games that follow the trashcore style tend to use very harsh colour palettes that are uncomfortable on the eyes; using palettes with dark, desaturated colours like greys, browns and dark yellows alongside highly-contrasting neons, such as reds, greens and magentas. The developers who make them may use set palettes with very few inbetween colours (or colours at all), or intentionally avoid details and shading.

Photobashing, overcompressing, stretching, or simply incorporating detailed textures/images alongside low-poly or pixellated environments/sprites is also a large part of the visual aesthetic. Some games are intentionally made in game engines that require tilesets, and so using sets with different levels of detail from tile-to-tile adds to this visual disproportion that many of the games use.

Trashcore games often feature an amount of blood and gore that is entirely unrealistic to any situation, with some characters or NPCs existing in a constant state of injury that has no effect on their quality of life. There is no set genre for Trashcore games, though most involve combat or violence in some fashion; most commonly through turn-based scripted fights or by pressing a key to shoot a projectile.
