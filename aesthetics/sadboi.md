# Sadboi

_Also known as: Sad Boy_

**Reason for Warning:** This page discusses and depicts themes of mental health, including depression, anxiety, and the romanticization of self-destructive behaviors. Imagery may include references to cigarettes, alcohol, and drugs. This content is not intended to promote or glorify self-destructive behaviors. **Sadboi** (also written as **Sad Boy**) is an internet aesthetic and masculine archetype that emerged on [Tumblr](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/2014_Tumblr "2014 Tumblr") and SoundCloud in the early 2010s. The terminology originates from the "Sad Boys" music collective, founded by Swedish rapper Yung Lean and producers Yung Gud and Yung Sherman in 2012.

The aesthetic visually materializes themes of loneliness and anxiety through a synthesis of streetwear, early 2000s raster graphics, and Cloud Rap iconography. Unlike the parallel [Sadgirl](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Sadgirl "Sadgirl") aesthetic, which draws from a long tradition of feminine literary melancholy and [Soft Grunge](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Soft_Grunge "Soft Grunge"), Sadboi relies on a sense of irony, commercial brand appropriation (e.g., Arizona Iced Tea), and lo-fi imagery.

## History

The Sadboi aesthetic achieved peak popularity between 2016 and 2019 across Tumblr, SoundCloud, and YouTube. This internet subculture's origins trace to the 2012 formation of the Sad Boys collective. Yung Lean and his producers pioneered a stylistic synthesis consisting of melancholic [Cloud Rap](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cloud_Rap "Cloud Rap") paired with the ironic appropriation of American consumer commodities, prominently featuring Arizona Iced Tea and Fiji Water branding. This presentation was a rejection of traditional hip-hop materialism.

During its peak, the aesthetic incorporated high-contrast, black-and-white VHS overlays of 1990s broadcast animation alongside data-bending artifacts. Collectives like Trash Gang ([Trxsh](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Trxsh "Trxsh")) expanded the aesthetic's visibility through Anime Music Videos (AMVs) and branded merchandise. The visual style later migrated into gaming communities, gaining prominence on *Roblox* via specific social hangout games like "*Lost*."

## Visuals

The aesthetic utilizes high-contrast juxtaposition, placing imagery of emotional distress alongside aggressive streetwear and reappropriated corporate logos (a practice inherited from [Vaporwave](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vaporwave "Vaporwave")). A primary visual motif involves isolating characters from mainstream broadcast animation, predominantly Bart Simpson, and applying VHS tracking distortion, broken heart emojis, and eye-censoring black bars.

Images rely on severe compression artifacts, pixelation, and chromatic aberration to simulate degrading physical media. Japanese typography (typically Katakana or Hiragana) regularly accompanies introspective text and the standard frown emoticon (☹).

## Fashion

Sadboi fashion consists of oversized minimalist streetwear and athletic apparel. The wardrobe typically includes windbreakers and tracksuits from Nike or Adidas, specifically favoring monochrome palettes. The bucket hat is the primary accessory, heavily popularized by Yung Lean's early music videos. Brands commonly associated with the style include FTP (Fuck the Population), Anti Social Social Club, Vlone, and Thrasher, regularly paired with [Trash Gang](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Trxsh "Trxsh") merchandise.

## Philosophy

The aesthetic is a subversion of traditional masculine stoicism. Unlike [Sadgirl](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Sadgirl "Sadgirl"), Sadboi operates through severe irony, utilizing heavily edited images to represent internal anguish. The movement centers on the reclamation of male sensitivity, rejecting conventional social expectations regarding emotional restraint. The Sadboi aesthetic transforms vulnerability into a performative visual style by pairing depressive ideation with the traditionally assertive posturing of hip-hop and trap music.

## Criticism

The primary controversy surrounding the Sadboi aesthetic involves its romanticization and commercialization of depressive imagery. The style frequently depicts motifs of mental illness, isolation, and substance abuse directly into retail merchandise, such as streetwear branded with anti-depressant logos, crying faces, or self-deprecating slogans. This practice generated immediate backlash across internet culture blogs and forums, which documented how the trend transformed clinical depression into a fashionable commodity.

The aesthetic's highly repetitive visual style became the subject of extensive internet mockery. By 2017, platforms like Reddit and Instagram were heavily saturated with parody edits that weaponized the aesthetic's own motifs against it. Users mass-produced hyper-distorted images of cartoon characters overlaid with melodramatic text to mock the movement's sincerity. This wave of satire made the Sadboi archetype look not like a genuine expression of male vulnerability, but like a performative strategy for social media validation.

## Music

The Sadboi aesthetic was associated with the development of [Emo Rap](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Emo_Rap "Emo Rap") and Cloud Rap, genres that pair emotional vulnerability with trap beats. The associated music expanded in the late 2010s to incorporate tracks from dark electronic and alternative rock that matched its themes of isolation, such as "Cradles" by Sub Urban.
