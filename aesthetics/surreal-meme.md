# Surreal Meme

**Surreal Memes** are a subgenre of internet humor and visual aesthetic that originated in the mid-2010s, characterized by a deliberate rejection of relatable humor in favor of incoherence, bizarre visuals, and non-sequiturs. The style is frequently described as a form of "Neo-Dadaism," deconstructing the standard formats of internet memes (such as Top Text/Bottom Text) to create images that appear devoid of intended meaning or context.

Visually, the aesthetic is distinct from other ironic genres like [Deep Fried Memes](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Deep_Fried_Meme "Deep Fried Meme") through its use of smooth [early-CGI](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Silicon_Dreams "Silicon Dreams") textures, "void" backgrounds, and specific recurring characters, most notably "Meme Man." The humor typically derives from the dissonance between the mundane nature of the subjects (vegetables, cubes, pillars) and the existential or cosmic dread in which they appear.

## History

The roots of the Surreal Meme style trace back to the mid-2010s experimental meme culture on platforms like Facebook, Tumblr, and 4chan. Precursors to the style appeared around 2015 on the Facebook page "Special Meme Fresh," which popularized the character of Meme Man, a poorly modeled 3D head that would become the mascot of the genre.

The aesthetic solidified into a cohesive movement with the creation of the r/surrealmemes subreddit in 2016. This community codified the visual style of the style, moving away from random "randomness" toward a structured form of nonsense with its own internal logic and lore. By 2017, the genre saw a surge in popularity, evolving from static images into complex narratives involving recurring antagonists like "Orang" (an anthropomorphic orange) and the "Vegetal" (vegetables treated as cosmic threats). While the aesthetic began as an underground reaction to mainstream meme culture, elements of it eventually entered the broader internet lexicon, particularly through the "Stonks" macro in 2019.

## Visuals

The visual composition of a Surreal Meme relies heavily on the "[uncanny valley](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Uncanny_valley "Uncanny valley")" effect produced by low-fidelity 3D graphics placed against hyper-realistic or nonsensical backgrounds. A primary element is the use of "Meme Man," a gray, metallic 3D head often rendered without a body, serving as the protagonist or observer of the image.

The environment of these images often depicts "The Void," represented by starfields, checkerboard floors, or solid gradient colors that suggest a dimension outside of standard reality. Into these spaces, creators insert banal stock photography objects, such as chairs, cups of water, or vegetables, which are then treated as entities with immense power or sentience. Text is frequently applied using standard serif fonts or distorted "Zalgo" text, and the grammar is intentionally broken to simulate a failure of language (e.g., "resonkling," "angery").

Unlike the aggressive "deep-fried" visual noise of the closely related [Deep Fried Memes](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Deep_Fried_Meme "Deep Fried Meme"), Surreal Memes often maintain a clean and smooth appearance. The aesthetic borrows the consumerist critique of [Pop Art](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Pop_Art "Pop Art") and the nonsense of [Dadaism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dadaism "Dadaism"), mixing high-concept sci-fi tropes with low-quality internet artifacts.

## Philosophy

The driving philosophy of the Surreal Meme is the exhaustion of "relatable" content. While traditional memes rely on shared experiences or recognizable templates, Surreal Memes operate on "layers of irony," where the humor comes from the audience's inability to parse a traditional punchline. This aligns the aesthetic with the art movement of [Dadaism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dadaism "Dadaism"), which similarly used absurdity to critique the rationality of the modern world.

Recurring narrative themes include existential dread, the fear of specific shapes (such as octahedrons or cubes), and the danger of mundane concepts like "the color red" or "vegetals." The humor is often fatalistic, presenting characters who are trapped in infinite loops or void dimensions, yet react to their situation with bureaucratic detachment rather than terror.
