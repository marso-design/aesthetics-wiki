**Día de Muertos**, or **Day Of The Dead**, is a Mexican holiday traditionally celebrated on November 1 and 2.

## Visuals

The visuals of Día de los Muertos (or Day Of The Dead) is vibrant, colourful and deeply symbolic, blending celebration with remembrance. Streets and homes are often decorated with bright orange marigolds, known as cempasúchil, whose petals are believed to guide spirits back to their families. Skulls and skeletons, especially the famous calaveras, are shown in playful and artistic ways rather than frightening ones, symbolising that death is a natural part of life. Traditional clothing, painted faces inspired by La Catrina, candles, papel picado banners and carefully made altars create a warm and spiritual atmosphere. Instead of mourning in silence, the holiday focuses on honouring loved ones through music, food, photographs and joyful gatherings, giving the celebration a unique mixture of beauty, memory and life.
