# New Money

_Also known as: Nouveau Riche, New Rich_

Candidate for Deletion 🗑️

This page is a candidate for deletion.  
Remember to check [what links here](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Special:WhatLinksHere/New_Money "Special:WhatLinksHere/New Money") and [the page history](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/New_Money?action=history) before deletion.  
**Reason:** This page is a candidate for deletion and a "scope creep" (e.g., assuming that because there is a [Old Money](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Old_Money "Old Money") aesthetic, there must be a New Money one) that misclassifies a socioeconomic status as a visual style. It lacks a cohesive identity, instead dumping opposite styles (like Minimalism and Maximalism) into one article. The "flashy" visual identity described here is already better documented by [Boujee](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Boujee "Boujee"), [McBling](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/McBling "McBling"), and [Yuppie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Yuppie "Yuppie"), which are legitimate aesthetics.

**New Money** is an aesthetic and cultural archetype centered on the conspicuous display of recently acquired wealth. The aesthetic is defined not by a fixed style, but by the intentional and often maximalist performance of status, relying heavily on expensive brand logos, current luxury trends, and highly visible consumption to signal success.

Unlike the restrained, inherited style of [Old Money](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Old_Money "Old Money"), the New Money aesthetic is fluid and trend-driven, frequently adopting flashy colors, oversized accessories, customized vehicles, and cutting-edge technology. This visual strategy is driven by the values of materialism, ambition, and the celebration of financial ascendancy, serving as a public display of social mobility.

## History

The idea of an aesthetic of the newly wealthy which is distinct from the established wealthy dates back to [Ancient Greece](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hellenic "Hellenic"). New Money is most visible during times of economic change, both extreme success and extreme poverty, with many people joining New Money circles during times of economic success and extreme spending being more notable during times of poverty.

New Money is looked down upon by Old Money and [Preppy](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Preppy "Preppy") people, usually for being attention seeking. Extreme loudness, thrill seeking, flashy jewelry, and spending sprees are also common among New Money. Those with New Money tend to fall under suspicion due to how quickly the person becomes successful. It may turn out that the money was earned in a less than legal way.

In the [2010s](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/2010s "2010s"), the Rich Kids of Instagram trend led to robberies as well as investigations into the finances of the "Kids" parents.

## Types of New Money

New Money is difficult to define as an aesthetic. It is visually striking, which can manifest in several ways:

### Gold

Excessive amounts of gold and gold plated items, especially things that are not usually gold plated, like toilets. Food with gold leaf is popular. Versace is brand which uses this style. Many people consider it gaudy.

### Jetset

This is the jet set lifestyle - private jets, yachts, vacations in Monaco. Visuals are similar to Boujee - gold, diamonds, expensive champagne. Males can exemplify the [Playboy](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Playboy "Playboy") aesthetic. Most common among musicians and athletes.

### Bourgeois [Bohemian](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Bohemian "Bohemian")

Think Gwyneth Paltrow's Goop, wellness retreats, and New Age spirituality. Feng Shui layout or modern [Scandinavian Design](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scandinavian_Design "Scandinavian Design") is used for decor. The color palette is light and airy: white, natural blonde wood, pale blue, light tan, gold, rose gold, and cream. Succulents are common.

### [Minimalist](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Minimalism "Minimalism")

Color palette is neutral: gray, black, white, and tan, with primary colors used as accents, especially red. Furniture is usually [Mid-Century Modern](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Mid-Century_Modern "Mid-Century Modern") and paintings and sculptures are [Cubist](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cubism "Cubism"). It is more masculine and common among men who made their money in Silicon Valley or on Wall Street.

### New ‘Old Money’

Often mistakenly called Preppy, this style a trendier version of Old Money. Think Cher in *Clueless* (1995). Closely related to [2000s Preppy](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/2000s_Preppy "2000s Preppy").

## Visual

The New Money aesthetic is popular in advertising, as it uses much more visible signs of wealth than other wealth related aesthetics like Old Money. Less obvious are the bohemian influenced (think Gwyneth Paltrow's Goop) and minimalist influenced New Money visuals.

* Brand names and logos
* Gold and silver
* Huge diamond rings
* Fur coats
* Private jets
* Yachts
* Luxury cars
* Marble floors
* Loud patterns like zebra stripes and leopard print
* Huge houses and mansions
* Luxury apartments
* Shopping bags, usually of luxury brands

## Fashion

Fashion follows trends but it generally is more revealing and sexual than Old Money styles. Local culture can drastically change fashion aesthetics - parts of California have a more bohemian vibe while Manhattan tends to be more [Chic Modernist](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Chic_Modernist "Chic Modernist").

### Gold and Jet Set

* Visible brand names and logos
* Dresses with plunging necklines and high slit skirts
* Skimpy bikinis
* Shirts worn open on men
* Sequins and metallic fabrics
* Fur coats
* Designer sneakers and athleisure
* Extremely high heels

### Bourgeois Bohemian

* Light airy garments made from organic cotton, linen, and silk
* Kaftans
* Muumuus
* Sandals
* Espadrilles
* Light wash denim jeans and shorts

### Minimalist

See [Chic Modernist](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Chic_Modernist "Chic Modernist")

* More gender neutral and androgynous
* Color palette consisting of black, gray, white, and tan
* Simplistic patterns and graphics

### Gold Makeup

* Metallic lipstick, eyeshadow, and nail polish

### Jet Set Makeup

* Long "coffin" nails
* Heavy contouring
* Dark fake tan
* Brightly colored lipstick, eyeshadow, and nail polish
* Fake eyelashes

### Bourgeois Bohemian Makeup

* Natural makeup or none worn at all
* Organically produced products

### Minimalist Makeup

* Bold red lipstick
* Darkened eyebrows
