# Supergraphic Ultramodern

_Also known as: 70s Ultra-Modern_

**Supergraphic Ultramodern** is a futuristic design aesthetic that was popular from the 1970s to early 1980s, succeeding [Raygun Gothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Raygun_Gothic "Raygun Gothic"), [Space Age](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Space_Age "Space Age"), [Googie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Googie "Googie"), [Atompunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Atompunk "Atompunk"), and [Mid-Century Modern](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Mid-Century_Modern "Mid-Century Modern") and overlapping with [Earth Tones](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Earth_Tones "Earth Tones") and [Cassette Futurism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cassette_Futurism "Cassette Futurism").

## History

Supergraphic Ultramodern was first seen in late 1960s New Hollywood films, such as *2001: A Space Odyssey*. By the 1970s it grew to become a dominant aesthetic in sci-fi, seen in movies such as *Logan's Run*. In the mid-1980s, Ultramodern was supplanted by newer aesthetics such as [Cyberpunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cyberpunk "Cyberpunk")/[Laser Grid](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Laser_Grid "Laser Grid"), [Cassette Futurism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cassette_Futurism "Cassette Futurism"), and [Memphis Lite](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Memphis_Lite "Memphis Lite").

## Visuals

Supergraphic Ultramodern focuses on interiors that are often white, orange, or rainbow-colored, flat graphics, iconography, inflatable furniture, and materials like plastics, plush, fiberglass, and shiny metals. Ultramodern contains curvy/rounded designs, similar to its predecessor Mid-Century Modern, but often with more earth-toned colors such as brown or orange.

## Ultramodern Revival

With nostalgia for the 1970s taking over pop culture in the mid-late 1990s, Supergraphic Ultramodern would be included in this general revival. This style's return was retroactively named [Ultramodern Revival](https://cari.institute/aesthetics/ultramodern-revival), and was relevant ca. late 1990s to the mid 2000s. It had similarity to [Millennium Disco](https://cari.institute/aesthetics/millennium-disco) and [Gen X Soft Club](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Gen_X_Soft_Club "Gen X Soft Club") through their shared nostalgia for the 1970s.

Supergraphic Ultramodern, [Mid-Century Modern](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Mid-Century_Modern "Mid-Century Modern"), and [Space Age](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Space_Age "Space Age") would serve as major influences to the [Y2K Futurism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Y2K_Futurism "Y2K Futurism") aesthetic.

Ultramodern, Earth Tones, and Laser Grid would go on to serve as inspiration for [Cassette Retrofuturism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cassette_Futurism "Cassette Futurism") in the 2000s to 2020s. In 2023, musician Chapelle Roan released the song "Super Graphic Ultra Modern Girl", which featured 1970s–80s [Glam Rock](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Glam_Rock "Glam Rock") influences in its music video.
