# Rare aesthetic (trend)

**Rare aesthetic** (often stylized as "**Rare aesthetic:**" followed by a specific scenario) is an internet meme and social media trend that categorizes mundane, hyper-specific, or ironic life experiences as "aesthetics."

Unlike traditional aesthetics, which are defined by visual cohesion, fashion, and art (e.g., [Cottagecore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cottagecore "Cottagecore") or [Cyberpunk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cyberpunk "Cyberpunk")), the "Rare aesthetic" trend repurposes the term to describe shared "core memories" or universal feelings. The trend often utilizes a format of low-quality or "ugly" photography to validate unglamorous experiences, effectively satirizing the internet's obsession with categorizing every aspect of life into a visual label.

## History

### Origins (2020)

The trend first emerged on TikTok in early 2020. Initially, the hashtag #rareaesthetic was used sincerely by users attempting to document legitimate but niche visual styles (such as "[Retrofuturism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Retrofuturism "Retrofuturism")" or "[Witch House](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Witch_House "Witch House")"). However, the trend quickly mutated into irony. Users began posting videos set to the song "*Crimewave*" by Crystal Castles, captioning them with hyper-specific but relatable scenarios that were decidedly non-aesthetic, such as "The Spanish Teacher Aesthetic" or "Middle School Basketball Practice on a Winter Night." This early wave established the trend's humor: the juxtaposition of the high-minded word "aesthetic" with the mundane reality of everyday life.

### The 2025 Resurgence

In late 2025, the trend experienced a massive resurgence on TikTok and Instagram Reels, driven by the platform's new Photo Mode (carousel) feature. This second wave was distinct from the 2020 iteration in two key ways; the 2025 resurgence was almost exclusively soundtracked by the song "*Gorof (Elixir)*" by the Somali funk group Dur-Dur Band. Users abandoned video montages in favor of "reportage-style" photo dumps. These images were intentionally low-fidelity (often blurry, flash-lit, or awkward) to mimic the texture of a real memory rather than a curated mood board.

## Characteristics & Irony

The defining characteristic of a "Rare aesthetic" post is its specific relatability. The humor often relies on "trauma bonding" or the recognition of a shared and unspoken experience.

### The Mundane/Nostalgic

Focusing on sensory details of childhood that are rarely discussed (e.g., "Rare aesthetic: The smell of the carpet in your elementary school library" or "Rare aesthetic: Drinking water from a hose in 2008"). This content frequently visually overlaps with styles such as [Liminal Space](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Liminal_Space "Liminal Space"), [Dreamcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dreamcore "Dreamcore"), [Weirdcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Weirdcore "Weirdcore"), and [Nostalgiacore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Nostalgiacore "Nostalgiacore").

### The Ironic/Specific

Satirizing specific personality types or uncomfortable social situations (e.g., "Rare aesthetic: Your Balkan dad who talks too much about the Yugoslav Wars" or "Rare aesthetic: Being the only sober person in the backseat of an Uber at 3 AM").

### "Vibe" Checks

Describing a feeling that has no name (e.g., "Rare aesthetic: The specific loneliness of a grocery store trip on the first night of a vacation").

## Meta-Commentary

The "Rare aesthetic" trend is frequently seen as a meta-joke about the internet's obsession with micro-labeling. By 2025, the word "aesthetic" had been used so exhaustively to describe visual styles (like [Cottagecore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cottagecore "Cottagecore") or [Clean Girl](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Clean_Girl "Clean Girl")) that it lost its original meaning. This trend satirizes that exhaustion by applying the high-concept label of "aesthetic" to things that are decidedly not aesthetic.

Culturally, this marks a shift where the term "aesthetic" no longer describes a visual cohesion, but rather a "vibe check" for shared experiences. It functions similarly to the "POV" trend or Corecore; it is less about art direction and more about the dopamine hit of recognizing a hyper-specific and unglamorous memory that you didn't realize millions of other people also had.
