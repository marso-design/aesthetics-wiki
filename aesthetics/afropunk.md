# Afropunk

**Afropunk** is a cultural movement and music genre encompassing the involvement of Black individuals in [punk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Punk "Punk") and [alternative](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Alternative "Alternative") subcultures. While participation dates back to the origins of punk in the 1970s, the term itself gained prominence following the 2003 documentary *Afro-Punk* by James Spooner and Matthew Morgan.

The movement is notable for its presence in the United States and the United Kingdom, and it has expanded globally through various artistic expressions and events. Key elements often include punk rock and alternative music styles, distinctive fashion, and a focus on social and political themes relevant to the Black experience.

A significant manifestation of the movement is the annual Afropunk Festival, founded in 2005, which serves as a gathering point for the community and a platform for diverse artistic expression.

## Media

### Films

* **Afro-Punk** (2003 documentary): Directed by James Spooner, this film is widely credited with popularizing the term "Afropunk." It explores the experiences of Black individuals within predominantly white punk scenes across the United States, addressing themes of alienation, identity, and belonging.
* **Afropunk Presents: Triptych** (film series): Produced by Afropunk, this series has featured interviews and insights from prominent Black artists like Wangechi Mutu and Sanford Biggers.

### Books

* **Black Punk Now** (2023 anthology): Edited by James Spooner and Chris L. Terry, this collection features nonfiction, fiction, illustrations, and comics that describe contemporary Black punk experiences and history. It aims to provide a wider frame of reference for Black punks and decenter whiteness in punk narratives.
* **Dreadnaught: King of Afropunk** (2013 memoir): A memoir by D.H. Peligro, drummer for Dead Kennedys and Red Hot Chili Peppers, detailing his experiences as a Black musician in the punk scene.
* **Zines:**
  + **Shotgun Seamstress**: A notable zine created by Osa Atoe, specifically "by and for Black punks," which aimed to support Black individuals in predominantly white subcultures and reinforce Black identity within punk.
  + **Black and Now: RESIST**: A zine produced in collaboration with the Afropunk Festival, focusing on Black British resistance through various artistic contributions.
