# Australian Gothic

> This article is part of a series on [Regional Gothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Regional_Gothic "Regional Gothic")

**Australian Gothic** is an aesthetic, literature and art movement based upon the darker side of the Australian identity and history. Contemporary instances can be similar to [Suburban Gothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Suburban_Gothic "Suburban Gothic") and [other Regional Gothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Regional_Gothic "Regional Gothic") aesthetics, but older pieces tend to have a more distinct feel to them (as early colonised Australia was vastly different from the rest of the world).

In the article *[More than Something Weird: Teaching Australian Gothic in the classroom](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworkspost2013/11978/),* Australian Gothic is introduced with the quote "[Australian Gothic] has established itself as an effective approach and developed some clear strategies for tackling some aspects of colonial legacy which are otherwise difficult to articulate. There is also within the Australian Gothic genre an opportunity to explore the dark side of the Australian experience."

## History

Australian Gothic emerged with the colonisation of the country, as settlers brought fear of the unknown land and knowledge of European Gothic with them. The gothic genre was perfect for expressing the newfound challenges, fears, and issues that accompanied a new sense of Australian life and culture.

One of the most notable recent instances of Australian Gothic is the urban legend (and media adaptations) of the story of *Picnic at Hanging Rock.* The story involves a group of girls going missing at *Hanging Rock,* a distinctive location in Victoria. There is often debate about whether or not the story has any basis in actual events. Many other stories and folklore also hold some connection to the aesthetic, such as the story behind *Waltzing Matilda* or the tale of how *"Mount Disappointment"* got its name. (Usually told as a group of early explorers having climbed it, then realised they could not see any view due to the trees, hence the name.)

* Injured or dead animals

* Rural isolation (people alone in the desert, in the bush, by the sea.)
* Ghosts

* Floods
* Insects and bugs
* Mythology and legends
* Abandoned buildings
* Dangerous animals, such as snakes and spiders

## Philosophy

"The Gothic genre gave early Australian writers and artists a way to explore the dark side of the Australian experience. This included the perceived hostility of the natural environment, the violence of colonisation, convicts’ experiences of exile and entrapment, settlers’ feelings of alienation, and European fears of the racial Other." "Anxieties about Australia’s colonial past have also been explored more recently in Gothic literature and film."

## Music

There is no specific musical genre connected to Australian Gothic, but old bush songs tend to lean towards and include the themes. Many more recent songs also include ideas that relate to the aesthetic. Musicians involved with other regional gothic aesthetics may also have songs relating to the genre, notably the newer interpretations of it.

## Criticism

In the early years of colonised Australia, many early art movements (such as *[Australian Impressionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_School))* were laughed off and highly criticised, and Australian Gothic was no exception. In his 1856 essay, *"The Fiction Fields of Australia",* Frederick Sinnett wrote, *"the foundations of a second 'Castle of Otranto' can hardly be laid in Australia during our time".*
