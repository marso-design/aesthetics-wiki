# Corpcore

_Also known as: Corporatecore, Corporate Chic_

**Corpcore** is an aesthetic that combines business attire and office wear with a more casual, street wear vibe. The term "CorpCore" was first as the title of designer brand HUGO BOSS' Fall/Winter 2023 fashion collection, which kickstarted the corpcore trend.

Corpcore is characterized by its mixture of old business wear trends with current casual wear fashion. It is heavily inspired by 90s workwear and tailoring, borrowing oversized and boxy fits from that era, as well as professional wear in general, and combining it with more casual, modern pieces like jeans and sneakers. In this way, it both elevates the basics and adds a unique pop of personality to professional clothing. Common visuals are exaggerated shoulders, cropped blazers, wide-legged trousers, layers, pleats, bows, pinstripes, and glasses, paired with sneakers, t-shirts, straight-legged jeans, baseball caps and jewelry.

## History

The term "CorpCore" was first coined by the fashion brand HUGO BOSS as the name of the collection showcased in their 2023 Fall/Winter show, BOSS: Techtopia, held on September 22nd, 2023 in Milan. In this show, BOSS highlighted deconstructed office wear designs -- belts emphasizing boxy cuts, deep slits bringing a sensual edge to the professional looks -- and "playful but functional" accessories. Even further casual stylings were juxtaposed with the workwear on display as models touted deep V-necks, chest-revealing tops, hoodies, and crocodile-embossed leather pieces. BOSS then released their Spring/Summer 2024 collection with a campaign titled "#BeYourOwnBOSS", a continuation of their Techtopia theme.

Following these collections, many brands began including reworked and deconstructed office staples in their shows: Prada's Fall/Winter 2024 Ready-to-Wear show exhibited boxy, loose-fitting office silhouettes alongside bows, colorful hats and shoes, and more casual pieces like sweaters and varsity jackets. New York label Retrofête released their Fall 2024 collection, paying "homage to the duality of the working woman. Both sophisticated and powerfully seductive..." This collection, similarly, included oversized, boxy silhouettes with emphasized shoulders, leather pieces, and pinstripe-covered office wear paired with sheer shirts, sequins, shorts, and blue jeans. Even Michael Kors' Fall 2024 Ready-to-Wear collection featured a blend power tailoring (with strong shoulders and wide-leg pants) and structural tailoring (defined waists and tight pencil skirts with slits) with much more casual elements, such as jeans, turtleneck sweaters and hoodie dresses.

Meanwhile, during a majority of 2024, Corpcore was becoming popular on social media sites like TikTok, a spiritual successor to its sister aesthetics [Office Siren](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Office_Siren "Office Siren") and closely tied to the tag "librariancore," after the success of these fashion campaigns and and partially due to celebrities like Bella Hadid wearing casualized workwear on the streets.

## Visuals

The color palette Corpcore is often neutral, earthy and subdued, ideal for a professional setting: black, white, and gray are most common to the style, as is green, navy and light blue, and brown. Office-appropriate patterns are used often -- pinstripes, houndstooth, tweed, plaids, and small polka dots are customary to the aesthetic.

Since it is a product of professional wear, the typical setting for Corpcore is professional, "white-collar" environments -- this includes (but is not limited to): office spaces and cubicles, corporate settings, libraries, city streets during working hours and academic settings (generally college-level and above). Due to its more relaxed leanings, however, this look is also perfect for places professional workers go to once they clock out -- bars, city streets during bar crawls, restaurants and even certain cubs will have people dressed in Corpcore attire. And of course, due to its origins in high fashion, you will see this look on the runway and in fashion shows, generally more during the Fall/Winter season.

There are a few aesthetic movements that are closely associated with Corpcore. Due to its structured, blocky shapes and skew towards minimalist design, Corpcore is similar to [Brutalism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Brutalism "Brutalism"), which also incorporates these details. Since the aesthetics debut was during BOSS' "Techtopia" era, and since many of the jobs associated with the look involve technology, Corpcore is related to [Abstract Tech](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Abstract_Tech "Abstract Tech"), [Cyberprep](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cyberprep "Cyberprep") and [Robotcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Robotcore "Robotcore").

Items commonly found incorporated into Corpcore looks are generally things that are related to white-collar jobs and city living -- laptops, over-the-ear headphones, cellphones, briefcases, notebooks and other office supplies can be supplemental to Corpcore outfits and are often used as props in Corpcore-themed photo shoots.

## Fashion

Corpcore fashion is characterized by the synthesis of casual wear and workwear, often with exaggerated silhouettes as a reference to the [Power Dressing](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Power_Dressing "Power Dressing") of the 1980s. This aesthetic aims to modernize professional attire in two ways: by making it more relaxed and accessible to the modern worker through the addition of casual wear, and by enhancing it to high fashion standards through thoughtful tailoring and using quality materials. Thus, Corpcore outfits usually incorporate both ends of the style, mixing everyday clothing with eye-catching fabrics and silhouettes.

Fabrics such as wool, linen, denim, and cotton are common to the style, as are "edgier" materials like leather and suede, and soft, high-quality fabrics like cashmere, silk and Merino.

Layering is a key aspect of Corpcore and is one of the easiest ways to blend both casual and professional attire. Overcoats, suit jackets, sweaters, and vests are typical components to layer over outfits, sometimes with several layering components in one look. To add a more relaxed vibe, specific articles of clothing can be used -- such as basic tees, denim jeans, shorts, skirts, or sneakers -- but it is also acceptable to wear workwear that is modified in a more "inappropriate" way to achieve this as well. For example, low-cut shirts, pencil skirts with high slits in them, oversized layers and even rolled up sleeves can be utilized to turn regular workwear into a Corpcore look.

For accessories, there is a lot of variety: things you'd expect to see in the office, like ties, glasses, belts and bags are frequent, but more unconventional accessories work well too, such as bows, gloves, headphones, corsets, high boots and statement jewelry.
