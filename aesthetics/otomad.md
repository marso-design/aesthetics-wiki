# OtoMAD

_Also known as: 音MAD, Sound MAD, Kichiku (China)_

**OtoMAD** (Japanese: **音MAD**; lit. "Sound MAD") is a video remix style and meme aesthetic and that originated on the Japanese video sharing site Nico Nico Douga in the late 2000s. The genre consists of the rhythmic synchronization of visual elements to a musical track, effectively treating video clips as musical instruments to construct a melody.

Unlike standard "MADs" (which are generally Anime Music Videos focused on narrative or mood), OtoMADs prioritize the audio experience, utilizing hyper-active editing, pitch-shifting vocals, and a specific set of visual effects designed to mimic the beat of the song. The genre is the Japanese progenitor to the Western YTPMV (YouTube Poop Music Video) aesthetic, sharing a similar lineage of transformative remix culture but maintaining a distinct visual identity rooted in [Otaku](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Otaku "Otaku") subculture.

## History

The aesthetic emerged around 2007 and 2008 on Nico Nico Douga, evolving alongside the rise of "*Kumikyoku*" (medley) culture. Early OtoMADs focused heavily on *Touhou Project* music and remixes of viral commercials, such as the "Ran Ran Ru" McDonald's commercials. The genre established a culture of cooperative chaos, where massive collaboration videos would feature dozens of creators contributing short segments to a single medley, creating a shifting video that united various source materials under a single tempo.

The aesthetic spread to China via the site Bilibili, where it acts as a foundational subculture known as "*Kichiku*" (鬼畜, lit. "Ghost Animal," implying something fiendishly repetitive or intense). In the West, it evolved parallel to and eventually merged with the YTPMV community. This cross-cultural exchange was solidified by major collaborations such as "The Glorious Octagon of Destiny," which featured over 30 participants from both the Eastern and Western scenes blending the specific visual styles of both regions.

## Visuals

The visual aesthetic of OtoMAD is strictly tied to the audio waveform, creating a synesthetic experience where the viewer "sees" the sound. A primary technique involves **Symmetrical Rhythmic Mirroring**, where the video frame flips horizontally or vertically in perfect time with the drum beat. This creates a strobe-like effect that transforms a standard character movement into a frantic dance-like loop.

Another central component is **Pitch-Shifted Distortion**. Since the audio of the source clip is often tuned to match a specific melody, the visual aspect is frequently manipulated to correspond with these pitch changes. Creators use warping tools to stretch a character's face wide during low notes or squash them thin during high notes, creating a rubbery, elastic visual texture that reinforces the musicality of the clip.

The background composition of an OtoMAD often utilizes high-contrast **Chroma Key Patterning**. Subjects are cut out from their original context and placed over rapidly scrolling, geometric backgrounds. The "Red Zone" background (a scrolling red and black grid derived from the beatmania IIDX song of the same name) remains one of the most recognizable visual elements of the genre. In collaborative medleys, the screen often adopts a **Grid Composition**, dividing the frame into squares where different characters or creators occupy specific slots, activating only when their specific "instrument" plays.

OtoMADs rely on a specific canon of "source materials" (*Sozai*), which creators choose for their absurdity or distinctive audio qualities rather than their mainstream popularity. Commercial advertisements are a staple of the genre, particularly Japanese McDonald's spots (focusing on the character Donald McDonald) and Z-Kai educational cram school ads, which are remixed to sound frantic or intense.

The genre also draws heavily from ACG (Anime, Comic, Games) culture. *Touhou Project* music and characters are foundational to the aesthetic, alongside *The Idolm@ster* and the *Kirby* series. A distinct sub-genre involves the use of "Gachimuchi" (muscular wrestling content, often featuring Billy Herrington), which developed its own complex lore and visual elements within the OtoMAD scene.
