# SoundCloud Rap

[Sensitive Content Notice](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Aesthetics_Wiki:Content_Warning_Policy "Aesthetics Wiki:Content Warning Policy") ⚠️

The following article contains and discusses content that may be distressing to some readers.  
**Reason for Warning:** This page documents a cultural era heavily associated with substance abuse. Content includes references to drug addiction (specifically prescription drugs and codeine), fatal overdoses of prominent artists, and themes of mental health struggles. Viewer discretion is advised.

The **SoundCloud Rap** era refers to a distinct cultural period in [Hip-Hop](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hip-Hop "Hip-Hop") that occurred roughly between 2015 and 2019. It was characterized by a wave of young independent artists who utilized the audio distribution platform SoundCloud to bypass traditional music industry gatekeepers.

Culturally, the era established a unique and cohesive visual aesthetic that blended the aggression of [Punk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Punk "Punk") and [Metal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Metal "Metal") with the digital nostalgia of the internet. It normalized a specific "rockstar" image characterized by face tattoos, dyed hair, and high-end streetwear mixed with thrifted grunge fashion. Musically, it was defined by "lo-fi" production—distorted bass (808s), unpolished vocals, and samples drawn from emo, pop-punk, and indie rock.

While the term "SoundCloud Rapper" was initially used as a pejorative to describe amateurish or "mumble" rappers, the era is now retrospectively viewed as a major paradigm shift that birthed global superstars (such as XXXTentacion, Lil Uzi Vert, and Juice WRLD) and permanently altered the sound and look of modern pop culture, as well as spanning or influencing multiple internet aesthetics.

## History

### Roots: The "[Trillwave](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Trillwave "Trillwave")" and Cloud Rap Era (2011–2014)

The foundations of the SoundCloud era were laid in the early 2010s by the [Trillwave](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Trillwave "Trillwave") and Cloud Rap movements. Collectives like Raider Klan (SpaceGhostPurrp) and Sad Boys (Yung Lean) pioneered the "internet rap" formula: lo-fi, atmospheric beats, VHS-style music videos, and a lyrical focus on depression and drug use. This era established the "[Sadboi](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Sadboi "Sadboi")" archetype that would dominate the later scene.

### The Explosion (2015–2017)

The scene exploded into the mainstream around 2015-2016. Artists like Lil Uzi Vert, Lil Yachty, and Playboi Carti brought a colorful, melodic, and "happy" energy to the platform. Simultaneously, a darker, more aggressive wave emerged from Florida and the underground, led by XXXTentacion, Ski Mask the Slump God, and Lil Peep. This schism created a diverse ecosystem where "mumble rap" and "screamo rap" co-existed. The visual language solidified during this time, heavily influenced by anime edits (such as "[Trxsh](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Trxsh "Trxsh")") and the rise of the [Hypebeast](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hypebeast "Hypebeast") fashion subculture.

### Peak and Decline (2018–2019)

The era reached its commercial zenith in 2018, but also faced a rapid series of tragedies that signaled its end. The deaths of Lil Peep (2017), XXXTentacion (2018), and Juice WRLD (2019) removed the scene's most charismatic leaders. By 2019, major labels had co-opted the sound, and the "SoundCloud" platform itself lost relevance to Spotify and TikTok. The original raw punk energy dissipated, evolving into the polished "[Aesthetic Rap](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Aesthetic_Rap "Aesthetic Rap")" of the [Early 2020s TikTok](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Early_2020s_TikTok "Early 2020s TikTok") era.

## Main Aesthetics

The era was not defined by a single sound, but by a collection of microgenres that shared a "DIY" philosophy.

### [Emo Rap](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Emo_Rap "Emo Rap")

The emotional heart of the era. Artists like Lil Peep and Juice WRLD fused trap beats with samples from 2000s pop-punk and emo bands. Lyrical themes focused heavily on heartbreak, depression, and drug addiction.

### [Trap Metal](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Trap_Metal "Trap Metal")

The aggressive, distorted side of the scene. Influenced by industrial music and metal, artists like Scarlxrd and Ghostemane utilized screaming vocals and abrasive, blown-out basslines. This style was often visually linked to horror imagery.

### [Meme Rap](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Meme_Rap "Meme Rap")

An ironic subcurrent that used humor and internet virality as a marketing tool. Artists typically had "gimmicks" (e.g., Ugly God's lizards, Yung Gravy's retro sample style) and utilized absurdity to gain traction on social media.

### [Phonk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Phonk "Phonk")

A revival of 1990s Memphis rap production styles, characterized by dark, jazzy samples and "chopped" vocals. While it remained underground during the main SoundCloud era, it later exploded on TikTok in the form of "[Drift Phonk](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Drift_Phonk "Drift Phonk")."

* *Key Figures: DJ Smokey, SpaceGhostPurrp, Soudiere, Doomshop Records*

## Visual Aesthetics

### [Trxsh](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Trxsh "Trxsh") and Anime Edits

The definitive visual format of the era was the "AMV" (Anime Music Video). Editors would synchronize gritty rap tracks with clips from anime series like *Naruto*, *Tokyo Ghoul*, or *Berserk*, applying VHS filters, glitches, and melancholic subtitles. This style, popularized by channels like Trash Gang, became the standard "music video" for artists who could not afford live-action filming.

### [Sadboi](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Sadboi "Sadboi")

The male archetype of the era. Visually defined by bucket hats, Arizona Iced Tea bottles, Japanese text on clothing, and a general demeanor of apathy or sadness. This aesthetic drew heavily from [Vaporwave](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vaporwave "Vaporwave") and the earlier Yung Lean era.

### [Hypebeast](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hypebeast "Hypebeast") Fashion

The fashion of the era was dominated by "Hypebeast" culture. The status uniform included Supreme box logos, BAPE shark hoodies, Off-White belts, and expensive sneakers (Yeezys or Jordans). This was often mixed with "anti-fashion" elements like thrifted flannels or band tees to create the distinct "SoundCloud" look.

## Legacy and Evolution

While the "SoundCloud Rap" era formally ended around 2019, its influence morphed into several modern aesthetics:

* **[Opium](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Opium "Opium"):** The "Rage" sound and avant-garde "Vamp" aesthetic popularized by Playboi Carti is a direct evolution of the SoundCloud scene's experimental side.
* **[Haunted Mound](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Haunted_Mound "Haunted Mound"):** A rural horror-influenced aesthetic that revives the "deep-fried" visuals and True Religion fashion of the early 2010s [Chicago Drill](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Chicago_Drill "Chicago Drill") era.
* **[Hyperpop](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hyperpop "Hyperpop") / [Glitchcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Glitchcore "Glitchcore"):** The chaotic digital energy of SoundCloud rap influenced the vocal processing and hyper-online culture of the Hyperpop scene.
* **[Aesthetic Rap](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Aesthetic_Rap "Aesthetic Rap"):** The "lo-fi" and anime-obsessed side of the genre was polished and optimized for TikTok, creating a smoother and less abrasive successor.
