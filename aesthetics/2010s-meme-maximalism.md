# 2010s Meme Maximalism

_Also known as: Retroslop/Rainbowslop (pejorative, primarily on Roblox)_

*This article is about an aesthetic that took 2010s memes and turned them into a cohesive art style. For the original era, see [Early Web 2.0 Nostalgia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Early_Web_2.0_Nostalgia "Early Web 2.0 Nostalgia").*

**2010s Meme Maximalism** is a revivalist internet aesthetic and art style that originated approximately in the early 2020s, characterized by a nostalgic celebration of the internet culture and memes from the late 2000s to mid 2010s. It uses brightly colored, densely layered collages that reinterpret the "random" humor and visual style of that era. The aesthetic is the compositional style itself (the [maximalist](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Maximalist "Maximalist") collage) rather than the individual memes it features.

The style is a retrospective phenomenon, created by Gen Z looking back on what they consider the "classic era" of [meme culture](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Memes "Category:Memes"). The aesthetic has been subject to criticism, pejoratively nicknamed "Retroslop" or "Rainbowslop" on some platforms due to its overwhelming and visually loud nature.

## History

The aesthetic itself began to take shape in the early 2020s on platforms like TikTok and Pinterest, where users began creating these maximalist compositions as a form of nostalgia for their "internet childhood" (often described in the form of "POV: you had unrestricted internet access as a kid" anecdotes). The source material for these collages originated on websites like 4chan, DeviantArt, Newgrounds, Tumblr, and early YouTube between roughly 2005 and 2014.

The aesthetic is often mislabeled as [Scene](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scene "Scene"), but it is a distinct phenomenon. While Scene was a music-based subculture with roots in post-hardcore and metalcore that often emphasized a lifestyle of internet fame, 2010s Meme Maximalism is a purely internet-native visual style focused exclusively on memes. However, it does have a significant overlap with [Scenecore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scenecore "Scenecore"), a 2020s TikTok-based revival that blends elements of the original Scene subculture with the pop culture-heavy visuals of this aesthetic.

### Coinage

The term "2010s Meme Maximalism" was coined on this wiki in 2025. The name is a combination of "2010s" (here referring to the decade it draws its primary inspiration from, rather than its original timespan), "Meme" (for its connection to meme culture) and "[Maximalism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Maximalism "Maximalism")" (to highlight its visually loud style) and was created to avoid the negative connotations of pejorative terms like "Retroslop."

### The Great Meme Reset

The intense nostalgia for 2010s internet culture has generated a counter-phenomenon known as the "Great Meme Reset." This is a meta-meme, widely circulated on platforms like TikTok, which predicts that on a specific date (often cited as January 1, 2026), internet culture will undergo a forced stylistic reversion. The movement explicitly calls for the death of modern "brainrot" memes and highly saturated meme trends, demanding a return to the simpler, "dank" meme style of the 2010s.

## Visuals

The visual foundation the 2010s Meme Maximalism aesthetic is the dense collage with little to no negative space. The background is typically a vibrant, chaotic pattern, such as a rainbow gradient, a [galaxy print](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Galaxy_Print "Galaxy Print"), or brightly colored splatters. This canvas is then layered with a multitude of iconic internet memes from the late 2000s and early 2010s. The most prominent motifs are rage comic faces, including Trollface, Me Gusta, Forever Alone, and the "Y U NO" guy. Other essential visual elements include rainbows (see also [Internet Awesomesauce](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Internet_Awesomesauce "Internet Awesomesauce")), elements of [MLG](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/MLG "MLG"), Nyan Cat, the Epic Face, early image macros (typically using the Impact font), and "Advice Animal" memes. The aesthetic embraces the often crude, low-resolution, and MS Paint-drawn quality of its source material, valuing nostalgic authenticity over polish.

A common trope within fanart in this aesthetic is the anthropomorphization of iconic internet figures (such as Derpina, Nyan Cat, or the old *Roblox* Noob). These characters are often reimagined as human or human-adjacent figures wearing exaggerated versions of 2000s alternative fashion, particularly [Scene](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scene "Scene"), [Scenecore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scenecore "Scenecore"), or [Kandi Raver](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Kandi_Raver "Kandi Raver") inspired clothing. This includes bright, dyed, choppy hair, studded belts, striped stockings, large bows, and heavy makeup, all rendered in the aesthetic's signature vibrant, chaotic colors.

## Criticism

As this aesthetic gained more visibility, it also faced significant criticism for being perceived as an anachronistic and inauthentic representation of the past. The style is often pejoratively labeled as "**Retroslop**," a term used to describe what critics see as a messy, overwhelming, and inaccurate attempt to replicate a past era's internet culture, often by people who did not experience it firsthand.

However, followers of the aesthetic contend that the "Retroslop" critique is often a straw man. They argue that the style's goal is not to faithfully recreate the past, but to transmute existing low-fidelity and nostalgic assets into something new and "silly" (in the modern meme context) as a valid form of creative self-expression.

A prominent example of this backlash occurred in early 2025 with the viral song and animation meme, "*Don't Touch My Pizza*." While initially popular, the song became a target of widespread mockery on TikTok, where it was frequently labeled as "corny" and one of the "worst songs of all time." The negative reception of the song's self-consciously "random" lyrics and 2010s-inspired visual style was a direct manifestation of the "Retroslop" critique, showing a cultural shift where these overt and maximalist displays of nostalgia are viewed by many as forced and inauthentic rather than genuinely appealing.

## Music

The music of 2010s Meme Maximalism is not a specific genre, but a collection of songs that became iconic through their association with viral videos, Flash animations, and the "random" humor of the era. The sound is typically high-energy, electronic, and repetitive, and frequently includes elements of nightcore (speeding and pitching up an audio).

## Media

The media listed below do not themselves share the 2010s Meme Maximalism aesthetic. Instead, they represent the primary source material from which the aesthetic draws its visual components, characters, and sense of humor. The iconic characters and art styles from these works were adopted and heavily remixed by the online communities that defined the maximalist collage style.

### Memes and Viral Phenomena

* **Rage Comics:** A series of crudely drawn MS Paint faces (e.g., Trollface, Me Gusta, Forever Alone) used in simple webcomics to depict relatable situations.
* **Nyan Cat:** A viral YouTube video of an animated pixel-art cat with the body of a Pop-Tart, flying through space and leaving a rainbow trail.
* **Advice Animals:** A specific format of image macros featuring a character's head on a colorful background, with top and bottom text in Impact font.
* **Domo:** The mascot of the Japanese public broadcaster NHK, who became an early internet meme through a viral "Every time you masturbate... God kills a kitten" image.

### Web Series and Videos

* **Charlie the Unicorn (2005–2024):** A Flash-animated series known for its surreal humor, involving the titular character who is repeatedly hoodwinked by two other unicorns.
* **asdfmovie (2008–present):** A series of short, animated sketches known for its rapid-fire, surreal, and non-sequitur humor.
* **YouTube Poop (YTP) (c. 2006–present):** A genre of video mashups where existing media is edited to create surreal, nonsensical, and humorous new content.
* **[Major League Gaming](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Major_League_Gaming "Major League Gaming") (MLG) Montage Parodies (c. 2014–2017):** A video meme genre that satirized professional gaming culture with chaotic, over-edited videos featuring dubstep, hitmarkers, and other visual noise.

### Television

* **My Little Pony: Friendship is Magic (2010–2019):** The show's bright, colorful art style and expressive characters became a massive source of fan content, memes, and remixes within the original decade's internet culture.
* **Invader Zim (2001–2006):** The hyperactive and chaotic character GIR became an icon of the "epic random" humor popular in the era and was a staple of the related [Scene](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scene "Scene") subculture.

### Music and Software

* **Vocaloid:** A singing voice synthesizer software whose character mascots, especially Hatsune Miku, became global internet icons and the subject of countless fan-made animations and music videos.
