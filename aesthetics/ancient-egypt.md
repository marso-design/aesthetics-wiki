**Ancient Egypt** was a historical civilization in Northern Africa that lasted from 3150 BC to 30 BC. The boundaries of the period are set by the beginning of the dynastic monarchs (pharaohs) who ruled Egypt and Rome's conquest of Egypt.

The aesthetics of Ancient Egypt have influenced art and design for thousands of years. In recent centuries it was most prominent during the [Victorian Era](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Victorian "Victorian") through the Egyptian Revival style in design and architecture, due to early archeology in Egypt. In the 1920s, after Howard Carter and his team opened the tomb of King Tutankhamun (King Tut), Egyptian art influenced the [Art Deco](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Art_Deco "Art Deco") movement, causing an 'Egyptomania'.

## History

The Old Kingdom (c. 2686–2181 BCE) was the golden age of pyramid building and naturalistic royal sculpture. The Great Sphinx and Menkaure with a Goddess show idealized, powerful forms. Reliefs in mastabas and pyramids depicted daily life as provisions for the afterlife.

The Middle Kingdom (c. 2055–1650 BCE) saw more expressive, somber art reflecting political upheaval. Statues show careworn faces. Rock-cut tombs like Beni Hasan feature vibrant, fluid painting.

The New Kingdom (c. 1550–1069 BCE) was the empire-era of grand temples. The Amarna Period briefly broke tradition with relaxed, naturalistic, even intimate scenes. Tutankhamun's tomb treasures represent a return to classicism.

The late & Ptolemaic periods saw archaizing styles mixed with Hellenistic influences.

## Philosophy

While Ancient Egyptian art spanned over 3,000 years, it remained relatively consistent for its ties to religion, funerary practices, and the concept of [ma'at](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maat#Principle).

## Revivals and later influence

### Egyptian Revival

➔ Main article: [Egyptian Revival](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Egyptian_Revival "Egyptian Revival")

There were revivals of the aesthetic of Ancient Egypt during the 18th and 19th centuries due to Napoleon Bonaparte's attempt to conquer Egypt, which led to an international Egyptomania. Architecture and objects in this style are called "Neo-Egyptian" or "Egyptian Revival". An example of an Egyptian Revival structure is the Egyptian entrance of the Hôtel Beauharnais in Paris, circa 1804, L.E.N. Bataille. Later in the 19th century, some [Academic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Victorian#Art "Victorian") artists made history paintings with scenes from Ancient Egypt, like *The Egyptian Widow* from 1872 by Lawrence Alma-Tadema, or *Cleopatra Testing Poisons on Condemned Prisoners* from 1887 by Alexandre Cabanel.

### Art Deco

➔ Main article: [Art Deco](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Art_Deco "Art Deco")

The style had another wave or popularity in the 1920s, thanks to the discovery of the tomb of Tutankhamen, opened in November 1922. Because of this, there are some examples of 1920s and 1930s [Art Deco](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Art_Deco "Art Deco") mixed with Neo-Egyptian motifs. The aesthetic of Ancient Egypt mixed well with Art Deco due to the fact that both use simplified motifs, geometric shapes, symmetry and repetition. The combinations of intense colours found in Ancient Egyptian jewellery were reused in the 1920s.
