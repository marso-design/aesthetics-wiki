# Heidelberg School

_Also known as: Australian Impressionism_

**Heidelberg School** (also sometimes referred to as **Australian Impressionism**) is an art movement that originated in Australia in the late 19th century, and is often regarded in history as one of the nation's most pivotal art movements that advanced the nation into the world of modern western art; even described by historians to be Australia's first significant and mainstream art movement.

Named after a small group of painters that completed their work on the rural outskirts of the Heidelberg city in Melbourne, Australia, the term has since evolved to include other painters who worked together around the rural parts of Melbourne and Sydney throughout the 1880s and 1890s, in what were known as "artists' camps". The Heidelberg School art movement places a strong emphasis on capturing the harsh yet romantic and tranquil environments of [the bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_bush#The_Australian_bush), a term to describe Australia's countryside and hinterlands, as well as capturing the peaceful daily lives of Australian citizens. Drawing inspiration from [Impressionism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Impressionism "Impressionism") and the [Barbizon School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbizon_School) sub-category of natural [Realism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Realism "Realism"), Heidelberg School art is usually characterised by it's lush landscapes, loose brushwork and it's palette of warm, pastel and harmonious colours made to represent the sunny climates of the nation.

Even after the introduction of modernist artists into the country during the early 1930s, the Heidelberg School movement was still embraced as a pioneering staple of not just Australia's art scene, but Australia's strong nationalist ideas and sentiments. Since it's inception, the movement became a heavy influence on other Australian artists of the modern era, and many major works that came out of Heidelberg School can be viewed to this day in multiple galleries across the country. The literature and film scene in Australia would also draw heavy inspiration from the Heidelberg School's visual and subject matter, including the rise of [Australian New Wave cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_New_Wave) in the 1970s and 1980s.
