# Fairy Grunge

_Also known as: Grunge Fairycore, Grunge Fairy_

**Fairy Grunge** is a fashion internet-based aesthetic. It is primarily a mix between [Fairycore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Fairycore "Fairycore") and [Grunge](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Grunge "Grunge"), and one of the many aesthetics which originated during the pandemic. This aesthetic combines elements of fairy mythology and natural elements of [Fairycore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Fairycore "Fairycore") with elements of [Grunge](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Grunge "Grunge") and [Kinderwhore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Kinderwhore "Kinderwhore") fashion. Similarly to other pandemic-born aesthetics, Fairy Grunge's appeal stems from natural elements and an ethereal atmosphere, which allow for escapism. It juxtaposes the distressed, layered silhouettes of [Grunge](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Grunge "Grunge") and [Kinderwhore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Kinderwhore "Kinderwhore") with fantasy-centric motifs. Fairy Grunge uses a mute color palette, earth tones, fishnets and flowing or distressed textures, and has recurring motifs of forests, fairy wings, moss and abandoned, overgrown buildings such as attics.

## History

**Fairy Grunge** mainly takes inspiration from previously existing aesthetics such as the [Grunge](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Grunge "Grunge") movement in the 1990s and the [fairycore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Fairycore "Fairycore") aesthetic which appeared roughly at the same time, as well as fashion aesthetics such as [kinderwhore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Kinderwhore "Kinderwhore") and [Mori Kei](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Mori_Kei "Mori Kei"). The aesthetic also had roots in 2000s YA fairy media, influenced by the works of Holly Black, and the folklore involving pixies and fairies, specifically the illustrations by artists such as Arthur Rackham, Amy Brown and Jessica Galberth. Fantasy films such as "Labyrinth", "The Dark Crystal" and "Bratz: Fashion Pixiez" have also taken part in shaping the aesthetic and the overall atmosphere.

The aesthetic first surfaced and gained popularity in the 2020s on social media platforms such as Pinterest, Tumblr, and TikTok. Earlier versions of Fairy Grunge were more focused on the fashion element and the natural element. In the present, it still focuses on the fashion and natural aspects, but has incorporated other elements into it such as D.I.Y and leaned more into the musical aspect. After gaining popularity, similar aesthetics became popular, such as and including Fairy Grunge's predecessors, Goblincore and Fairycore and inspired the development of other aesthetics such as Elf Punk and [Twilightcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Twilightcore "Twilightcore") fashion. The aesthetic has also gained attention in the UK and has been popularized as a subgenre among teenagers.

## Philosophy

Fairy Grunge presents itself as a form of escapism through its fantasy elements, natural surroundings and distressed clothes. The aesthetic is considered a form of rebellion against convention because it romanticizes nature and whimsical elements such as fairies and woodland creatures, while also romanticizing chaos and imperfection. It also involves themes of self-expression, since it allows for its enjoyers to dress however they find practical or comfortable, as well as providing space for creativity to experiment with texture and style.

## Visuals

Fairy Grunge imagery usually involves a dark, ethereal and dream-like atmosphere. This aesthetic mostly features a cool colour-palette, dark shades and earth tones, as well as soft lighting and grainy textures. The signature elements of Fairy Grunge are outdoor elements such as forests, foot-paths, rocks and mushrooms, as well as whimsical motifs like butterflies, birds, crystals and fantasy creatures including fairies and elves. Meanwhile, dark elements such as torn clothes, abandoned buildings and old statues to contrast with the whimsical atmosphere. The environment often has a Gothic atmosphere, which the aesthetic extenuates by using night-time or cloudy weather. The overall mood is dream-like and dark, offering a mysterious atmosphere.

## Fashion

### Tops

Fairy Grunge often relies distressed, layered or loose-fitting silhouettes. The aesthetic takes heavy inspiration from Grunge, relying on practicality. Tops often include: oversized graphic **t-shirts, blouses**, button-down **shirts** and corduroy **shirts**, as well as tank **tops** and halter **tops**. grandpa sweaters and oversized cardigans**,** as well as knitted jumpers, which are sometimes hand-made since Fairy Grunge encourages DIY similarly to Grunge.

### Bottoms

When it comes to bottoms, the aesthetic also uses distressed fabrics and textures, while also being androgynous in terms of what to wear. Fairy Grunge bottoms are considered to be mostly loose-fitting, and often rely on flowing fabrics. Common clothing items include: maxi or mini **skirts**, prairie **skirts**, large cargo **trousers**, **shorts**, ripped-up **jeans**, corduroy **trousers** and **overalls**. Bottoms also include knee-high **socks,** funky **socks** and **tights**, especially **fishnets** or patterned mesh ones.

### Outerwear

Like the tops, Fairy Grunge outerwear also uses distressed textures, materials and loose-fitting silhouettes focusing on both practicality and whimsy at the same time. Similarly to Grunge, its predecessor, DIY among followers is encouraged. **Sweaters, hoodies, vests**, oversized **cardigans**, plaid **shirts** and knitted **jumpers** (hand-made or thrifted) are frequent clothing items seen in the aesthetic. More whimsical outerwear such as **corsets** is often present in looks as well.

### Dresses

Like with tops, dresses are often long, flowy and have loose-fitting silhouettes for an ethereal look. So, Fairy Grunge relies primarily on maxi **dresses** or **nightgowns**. Other styles are also used, as long as they have distressed textures and earthy tones present throughout the aesthetic.

### Footwear

In terms of footwear, Fairy Grunge doesn't focus on a specific type of footwear. Followers of this aesthetic often focus on practicality by wearing **converse**, **Doc Martens**, army or combat **boots**. Other shoes include platformed **Mary-Janes**, **sandals** or normal **Mary-Janes** as well as **boots** with fur-lining.

### Accessories

Accessories are considered very important in the Fairy Grunge aesthetic and its followers frequently wear and add accessories to complete their look. They can either be hand-made or thrifted. Accessories in Fairy Grunge usually have a whimsical style, and they have been compared to something fairies would wear. So, **necklaces,** **bracelets**, and **earrings** are often worn. Other accessories include **hats, leg warmers, scarves**, **mittens** or wooly **gloves**, patterned fingerless **gloves** and **wrist-warmers**. To achieve a more fairy-like appearance, **elf ears** and fairy **wings** are sometimes added to the look.

### Hair and Makeup

There is no specific look for hair and makeup in the Fairy Grunge aesthetic. Hair is usually made messy or let down for a natural, more earthy look, but some people style it into braids or up in a bun. In terms of makeup, Fairy Grunge often uses black or white eyeliner to define the eyes, as well as eyeshadow in dark, or earthy colours. The eyes are usually the key part of Fairy Grunge makeup. But, some people still go for a natural look.

## Subgenre(s)

### Fairy Hippie

Like Fairy Grunge, Fairy Hippie is a fashion-oriented, hybrid aesthetic. Unlike Fairy Grunge, Fairy Hippie is mainly a combination between Fairycore and fashion associated with [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie"). Like Fairy Grunge, Fairy Hippie is focused on romanticizing nature, as well as leading a whimsical lifestyle. Unlike Fairy Grunge, Fairy Hippie focuses more on the Hippie lifestyle and directly follows the philosophy, while also featuring elements of Fairycore. Sometimes, Fairy Hippie directly takes inspiration from other aesthetics such as Whimsigoth.

## Criticism

Fairy Grunge has been frequently criticised for directly associating itself with the [Grunge](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Grunge "Grunge") movement. It is argued that Fairy Grunge was only inspired by Grunge because of the clothing commonly worn by members and icons of the subculture, such as Kurt Cobain. This is considered to go against the Grunge philosophy by simplifying a music-focused, anti-consumerist subculture based in Seattle into a simple fashion aesthetic, ignoring the elements that made the original subculture subversive and iconic in the first place.

This choice is further criticized for falsely associating the aesthetic with Grunge only, despite the fact Fairy Grunge is influenced by other styles such as Y2K, Fairycore and Cottagecore. This may lead to further confusion regarding the aesthetic, since those who've never heard of the Grunge aesthetic may be confused by Fairy Grunge and assume that everyone in the Grunge subculture dresses in a certain way and is fashion-oriented, indirectly hurting the original subculture.

Fairy Grunge has also been criticized for its commercialization, with its members seen frequently buying clothes from unethical brands such as Shein. This has been criticized for supporting unethical practices such as fast-fashion, as well as child labour. This is considered to be in poor taste towards the inspirations behind Fairy Grunge, as the main inspiration like Grunge encourages DIY and thrifting as a way of non-conformity. So, over-consumption from brands like Shein and Romwe is considered to be ignorant of the entire point of the aesthetic, while also being wasteful and harmful to the environment in general.
