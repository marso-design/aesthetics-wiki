**Tropideco** is a design aesthetic prominent in the 1990s and early 2000s. It is characterized by the commercialized simulation of tropical environments, commonly found in shopping malls, water parks, and themed restaurants like Rainforest Cafe. Unlike the [Tiki](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Tiki "Tiki") style of the 1950s which focused on Polynesian pop-culture, Tropideco utilizes varied and frequently exaggerated motifs such as neon lighting, fiberglass foliage, and cartoonish marine life to create a hyper-saturated and synthetic "vacation" vibe.

## Visuals

Tropideco, while still rooted in tropical themes, diverges from traditional tropical aesthetics by emphasizing the artificial recreation of tropical environments.

Common motifs include:

* Artificial tropical foilage (Palm Trees, Ferns, Flowers, etc.)
* Resort architecture and decor
* Contained tropical wildlife
* Beach/Pool toys
* Synthetic oceans and pools
* Stereotypical Hawaiian/Polynesian imagery
* Smooth and polished surfaces
* Faux wood and plastic
* Tiki/Moai Statues
* Oversaturated Tropical Colors

## Music

Tropideco music focuses on nostalgic and slowed down songs with the ukulele being a very common instrument in the aesthetic. Some songs include:

* Sweet Lagoon by Koday Jackson
* Aloha Oe (Slowed & Reverb)
* pua nani o hawaii (SLOWED & REVERB)
* Holiday Dream (slowed down with reverb)
* Spongebob Ost Hawaiian Happiness (slowed + reverb)
