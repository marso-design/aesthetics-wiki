# Hippie Goth

_Also known as: Boho Goth, Bohemian Goth, Gypsy Goth_

*Not to be confused with the [Whimsigothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Whimsigothic "Whimsigothic") interior design aesthetics.*

**Hippie Goth**, also known as **Boho Goth** or **Bohemian Goth**, is a substyle of the [Goth](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Goth "Goth") and [Hippie](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Hippie "Hippie") subcultures characterized by the blend of Gothic and Hippie or [Bohemian](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Bohemian "Bohemian") fashion. Singer-songwriter Stevie Nicks is often cited as the pioneer of this style, as she is known for her mixture of long, draped and layered fabrics, Victorian aesthetics, and dark jewel tones.

The Hippie Goth style mixes classic Goth fashion staples like black clothing, heavy accessories, boots and fishnets, with earthy, mystical elements of the Hippie movement, such as maxi skirts, bell sleeves, crochet, and fringes. The color palette of the style still skews darker than most, but expands itself to earthy and jewel tones like lush greens, browns, deep purples, and intense reds. As it is still a substyle of Hippie, Hippie Goths tend to value environmentalism, anti-materialism and spirituality, with many often identifying as vegetarian/vegan or witches. For this reason, you will frequently find many spiritual motifs arising in this style, like astrological patterns, crystals, and the usage of tarot or other divination devices.

## History

Though both separate subcultures found their start in the 70s, the Hippie Goth aesthetic didn't come into fashion until the 80s, alongside the rise to fame of pop rock band Fleetwood Mac and their lead singer, Stevie Nicks, who was notorious for her "hippy goth wear". By the early 2000s, it was solidified into Goth subculture, with online pages listing it alongside other Goth subtypes like techno and Industrial Goth as early as 2005.

## Visuals and Philosophy

Hippie Goth pairs the dark, dreadful and dreary elements of Goth with the free-spirited nature and environmentalist themes of Hippies. Gothic imagery such as bats, crosses, ravens, and skulls are often paired with more nature-related motifs, like moons and stars, flowers, ferns and similar botanical patterns. This pairing represents the main philosophy of the subculture, that there is beauty in both the light and darkness of life; nature contains both growth and decay, and that balance is meaningful and beautiful. To Hippie Goths, life is what makes death meaningful and, vice versa, the inevitability of death is what gives meaning to life. Many Hippie Goths extend this thought process to animals and use is as the basis of their vegetarianism or animal-rights activism: every life is important and every death is sacred, and humans have no right to interfere with that.

Many Hippie Goths are also mystics and are interested in Pagan traditions, folk magic, and other spiritual ideas. This belief stems from the idea of being able to connect to something larger than themselves, something otherworldly and divine. Spiritual symbols and even rituals are common in the Hippie Goth aesthetic, and it isn't unusual to meet a Hippie Goth who partakes in Wicca, witchcraft, divination, or other occultist practices.

## Fashion

Common materials include lace, fishnet, mesh, velvet (velveteen, crushed velvet), velour, fur and faux fur, leather and faux leather, crochet and knit fabric, and sometimes suede. While the foundation of the fashion is often black or brown, it is often layered with white and gray, earthy tones like deep greens, terracotta, rust ochre, and moody, jewel tones such as fuchsia, garnet, plum, merlot -- various shades of red, purple, blue and green. Layering fabric in general is a key aspect to this style, as is patterned clothes and layered jewelry.

Common prints, patterns, and motifs you will see are: Celestial motifs (stars, suns, moons, planets), floral motifs (flowers, often vintage-style, autumnal prints, damask, botanical, and sometimes ditsy or liberty prints), tie dye, spiritual motifs, often Pagan or Christian in nature (crosses, Triple Moons, the Witch's Knot, pentagrams/pentacles, Triskelions, Tarot cards, etc.), and abstract and geometrical patterns (swirls, vermicular, stripes, checks, Celtic knots, Peace Signs, etc.).

As it is very similar if not essentially the same as Whimsigoth, there will be little variation between the fashion listed here and the Fashion category on the [Whimsigothic](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Whimsigothic "Whimsigothic") page.

## Music

Hippie Goths are cited as listening to "...dark folk (eg. Faith and the Muse), ethereal (eg. Cocteau Twins), Pagan rock (eg. Inkubus Sukkubus), and the more psychedelic end of the 80s spectrum (eg. Tones on Tail), in addition to regular folk and New Age." Singer Julianne Regan and her band All About Eve was deemed "gypsy goth," by AllMusic in 2001, and praised by AllMusic writer Chris True for her "unique, folk-rock-influenced take" on 80s Goth rock.

Music associated with the Hippie Goth subculture generally includes ethereal goth, psychedelic, 90s rock and alternative/indie, as well as the music of the artists most closely associated with the style (Stevie Nicks, Florence Welch, etc.).
