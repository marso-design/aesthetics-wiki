# Dreamcore

[Sensitive Content Notice](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Aesthetics_Wiki:Content_Warning_Policy "Aesthetics Wiki:Content Warning Policy") ⚠️

The following article contains and discusses content that may be distressing to some readers.  
**Reason for Warning:** This article includes imagery that may be unsettling to some viewers.

**Dreamcore** is a surreal internet aesthetic that emerged in the early 2020s focusing on visuals inspired by dreams. Its imagery is often settings which can be computer-generated or edited to evoke a dream like quality, using a bright, surreal, or hazy atmosphere to create nostalgia and comfort. While its settings are comprised of [liminal spaces](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Liminal_Space "Liminal Space") these spaces can be edited to elicit a dreamy look.

The expansion of Dreamcore on platforms such as TikTok led users to incorporate motifs such as eyes, angels, text, mushrooms, and stitched characters into these settings. These specific motifs led to self-expression through cosplays/fashion and original characters, as well as through music artists like Jack Stauber.

However, this aesthetic had gotten criticism, with some perceiving Dreamcore as "cringe" or a "watered down" version of the related [weirdcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Weirdcore "Weirdcore") aesthetic. While it is often confused with weirdcore, with both aiming to evoke feelings of nostalgia and unfamiliarity, Dreamcore specifically aims at evoking comfort and familiarity, while weirdcore focuses on feelings of confusion and alienation.

Despite Dreamcore's reliance on some horror elements, it intends to make the viewer feel both simultaneously comforted and unsettled. The use of characters and or certain elements from other aesthetics subverts the individual's expectations of familiarity, yet the backgrounds and liminal spaces provide comfort and nostalgia. This dissonance can be confusing or unsettling to some individuals however.

## History

The term "Dreamcore" wasn't used before the early 2020s, however images seen in Dreamcore related media traces back to 2018 on Tumblr, where user [@gbrltvrst](https://jaaaaaim.tumblr.com/post/678238116792893440/gbrltvrstgabrielle-traversat) shared visuals featuring surreal landscapes and 3D elements. Over time, specifically around the COVID-19 pandemic, the aesthetic saw a significant rise in popularity, with creators such as "[@lizardKisses](https://www.tiktok.com/@little.lizard.kisses)" creating comedic videos that included stitched characters. Which would eventually become a common factor in Dreamcore images.

The expansion of Dreamcore was also fueled by compilations of TikTok shorts, user expression, and YouTube channels like "[mother is watching](https://www.youtube.com/@motheriswatching9298)," as well as Backrooms videos from creators like Kane Pixels. Additionally, Dreamcore's influence indirectly contributed to the emergence of [nostalgiacore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Nostalgiacore "Nostalgiacore"), an aesthetic focused on the melancholic appreciation of childhood memories.

Dreamcore began to gradually decline after 2021, as new trends began to emerge and take its spot. However, Dreamcore has seen a resurgence in popularity in 2025-2026 after the release of the highly anticipated "Backrooms" (2026) by Kane Pixels.

## Visuals

### Settings

The settings of the Dreamcore aesthetic typically feature bright and hazy visuals, which enhance the dreamlike quality of the image. These can convey feelings of nostalgia, comfort, or anemoia. Dreamcore settings draw inspiration from liminal space but are notably more colorful and include other elements like surreal landscapes and architecture, rainbows, clouds, and unconventional contexts like skies or flowers replacing walls or floors.

Dreamcore settings can encompass imagery related to the Backrooms or Poolrooms, which reflect the emotions and sensations that Dreamcore aims to evoke. Users on social media would use grainy, distorted text-to-speech narration to state "rules" for entering a surreal world, often parodying [@jgretznerd's](https://www.tiktok.com/@jgretznerd) video, with the phrase, "there exists a world, that you can only reach between 4:20 and 4:44 am."

### External Motifs

During the peak of Dreamcore's rise, users on platforms such as TikTok and Picsart would create object headed original characters (usually sporting cardboard cutout masks) and creatures such as winged-eyed angels, or silhouettes with facial features that usually reflected the "weird" and "surreal" feeling of dreams.

In addition to these object-headed characters, users would also subvert the nostalgic settings using voids, cryptic read text and overlay, organs, animals, mushrooms, and motifs from other aesthetics, especially [Yami Kawaii](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Yami_Kawaii "Yami Kawaii") where users implement pills, bandages, and syringes, and [Kidcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Kidcore "Kidcore") stickers.

## Criticism

A part of the criticism around Dreamcore was due to inherent irony centered around the "core" suffix and the overcategorization of certain aesthetics. Communities like [FlopTok](https://floptok.fandom.com/wiki/Floptok) viewed Dreamcore as "cringey" or a joke, and other communities believed that Dreamcore (and other related aesthetics, such as Traumacore and Weirdcore) were merely "buzzwords" or terms used to validate and romanticize "fake" or "undiagnosed" mental health issues.

Dreamcore's visuals were also a significant factor of criticism since many people would call out the "repetitive" visuals of Dreamcore. Such as mushrooms, eyes, OC's, cryptic text, and angels with wings. Without any "unique" change to the aesthetic. The conflicting names of Weirdcore and Dreamcore also led to further criticism of Dreamcore, causing some viewers to yearn for the "old" Weirdcore or to label Dreamcore a "watered-down" version of Weirdcore.

## Differences from [Weirdcore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Weirdcore "Weirdcore")

| **Concept** | Weirdcore | Dreamcore |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core nature** | Aims to evoke feelings of confusion, disorientation, dread, alienation, and nostalgia or anemoia. | Aims to evoke feelings of a dream, often questioning reality and identity, evoking feelings of anemoia, comfort, and nostalgia. |
| **Visual feel** | The visuals are low-quality and confusing, with a simple, uniform style. The text often doesn't match the images, featuring distorted objects, unusual borders, word art, and liminal spaces, sometimes with surreal geometry. | The visuals are dreamlike and hazy, evoking nostalgia through kitschy and often cheap edits of characters. The text creates a sense of wonder while creating detachment. Common elements include webcore pop-ups, mushrooms, organs, voids, suburban scenes, pills, surreal dreamy liminal spaces, smiling faces or stickers, rainbows, eyes, clouds, silhouettes, flowers, CRT or TV motifs, and angels. |
| **Impact** | Spawned small communities and YouTube videos, growing through articles. Its visuals are often confused Dreamcore or other similar aesthetics. | Formed via TikTok and became more "mainstream" than Weirdcore, ultimately overlapping with it. This attention allowed for user expression and cosplays, indirectly leading to [nostalgiacore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Nostalgiacore "Nostalgiacore"). |
| **Music** | Uses ambient, disjointed, glitchy music. Used to create a surreal, uncanny atmosphere when accompanying Weirdcore images. | Uses chaotic, experimental, dreamlike music. These songs often reflect narratives and motifs in Dreamcore. |
