**Maximalism** is a design style that encourages excess. It is the polar opposite of [Minimalism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Minimalism "Minimalism").

## History

The visual aesthetic of maximalism has existed throughout history as a method of displaying status, wealth, and religious grandeur. While the specific term is modern, the practice of *horror vacui* (the fear of empty space) was a defining characteristic of the [Baroque](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Baroque "Baroque") and [Rococo](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Rococo "Rococo") periods. During the 17th and 18th centuries, architecture and interiors were designed with extreme ornamentation to evoke awe. The [Victorian](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Victorian "Victorian") era further established this through the practice of eclecticism, where domestic interiors were filled with dense collections of commodities, furniture, and textiles drawn from various global cultures and historical periods.

In the contemporary era, maximalism experienced a significant resurgence following the 2008 recession. While the 2010s were largely dominated by minimalist trends such as "Millennial Minimal" and [Scandinavian Design](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scandinavian_Design "Scandinavian Design"), the late 2010s and early 2020s saw a pendulum swing back toward excess. This modern iteration is often less focused on the display of expensive luxury goods and more centered on personal expression, nostalgia, and comfort. It manifests in internet aesthetics such as [Cluttercore](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cluttercore "Cluttercore"), [Dopamine Dressing](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dopamine_Dressing "Dopamine Dressing"), and [Gen Z Maximalism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Gen_Z_Maximalism "Gen Z Maximalism"), which encourage the curation of sentimental objects and the rejection of sterile living spaces.
