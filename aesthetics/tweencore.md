# Tweencore

_Also known as: Justicecore, Mall Tween_

**Tweencore** describes the highly commercialized visual culture marketed to pre-adolescent girls largely between the late '90s to 2014. Unlike the broader demographic concept of "tweenhood," Tweencore refers to a cohesive set of maximalist design tropes found in North American suburban mall retailers of the era, most notably Justice (formerly Limited Too), Claire's, and Aeropostale's P.S. line. The aesthetic is characterized by a high-saturation color palette (typically consisting of rainbow, violet, and pink), the extensive use of "animal print" (specifically zebra and leopard print in unnatural colors like lime green or hot pink), and in its 2010s iterations, "random" humor motifs (such as mustaches, tacos, and slogan tees).

Visually, it acts as the "lite version" of [McBling](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/McBling "McBling"). It borrows the glitter, sequins, and consumerist themes of McBling and the layered neon accessories of [Scene](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scene "Scene"), but sanitizes them for a younger mainstream audience, replacing the subcultural or sexualized elements with a polished and commercial "girl power" image.

## Visuals

Tweencore relies heavily on bold, clashing patterns, specifically zebra and leopard print, recolored in unnatural shades of hot pink, electric blue, lime green, and purple. In the aesthetic's typography, clothing frequently featuring "text speak" (e.g., LOL, OMG, BFF), "sassy" slogans, or peace signs rendered in sequins, glitter, foil, or puffy paint. Imagery often includes specific "random" humor motifs associated with the late 2000s and early 2010s, such as mustaches (often on fingers or necklaces), tacos, bacon, and cupcakes with faces.

Unlike the [Old Web](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Old_Web "Old Web") aesthetic, Tweencore's character iconography is strictly commercial, favoring mall-brand mascots like *Bobby Jack* (the monkey), *Paul Frank'*s Julius, *It's Happy Bunny*, and *Ty Beanie Boos*. Celebrity imagery is also prominent, but strictly in the form of official merchandise; specifically graphic tees and posters featuring "teen idols" like Justin Bieber, Britney Spears, One Direction, or Disney Channel stars.

## Fashion

Fashion was mainly inspired by the popular [McBling](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/McBling "McBling") and [2000s Preppy](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/2000s_Preppy "2000s Preppy") aesthetics of the time. More feminine styles had flowers, rainbow, butterflies and heart graphics and patterns alongside quotes. Less girly tweens wore Scene-influenced outfits, which combined bright colors with black.
