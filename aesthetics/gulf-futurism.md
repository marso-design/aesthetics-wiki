**Gulf Futurism** is a hypermodern art movement emerging from the Gulf states, centering primarily on the futuristic vision and technological development of the Arabian Gulf region, as well as the region’s role in globalization and the technological revolution.

On the one hand, the scenarios depicted in Gulf Futurism are largely exaggerated. Through the excessive deployment of technology, they interweave rampant consumerism and urban expansion with the alienation of the individual. On the other hand, Gulf Futurism blends tradition and the future to create a brand-new artistic language, infusing new vitality and imagination into the cultural confidence of the Arabian Gulf region.

## History

Sophia Al Maria’s online video The Gaze of Sci-Fi Wahabi (2008) marked the birth of this aesthetic. Inspired by the theory of [Afrofuturism](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Afrofuturism "Afrofuturism"), Qatari-American artist Sophia Al Maria and Kuwaiti composer and artist Fatima Al Qadiri jointly put forward this concept in 2012. They advocate for integrating the history, culture and social context of the Gulf states in the Middle East with modern technology, science and futuristic imagination, to explore the future of the Arabian Gulf region through this integration.

## Visuals

Artistic works of Gulf Futurism typically feature **bold colors** and **geometric forms**. They often employ ostentatious modern technological elements and landscapes — such as **glittering skyscrapers, petroleum industries, off-road vehicles, hyper-masculinity**, and **Baroque-style interior designs** — while integrating cultural symbols and forms native to the Arabian Gulf region, including **traditional ornaments, handicrafts** and **architectural styles**.
