# Avantropop

_Also known as: Avant Garde Prefuse_

**Avantropop** refers to a graphic design style of [Recession Pop](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Recession_Pop "Recession Pop"), seen in other pieces of media during this time, like for example in the "Noods" (2008-2010) and "CHECK it" (2010-2016) eras of Cartoon Network. The style was named by Evan Collins as a portmanteau of "[avant-garde](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Avant-garde "Avant-garde")" and "electropop."

## History

Avantropop emerged in the late 2000s as a branch of the broader [Recession Pop](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Recession_Pop "Recession Pop") design language that dominated Western animation networks, youth advertising, and digital branding during the post-2008 economic recession. While [Recession Pop](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Recession_Pop "Recession Pop") is characterized by vivid colors, geometric motifs, and sleek digital simplicity, Avantropop pushes these ideas into a more experimental, high-energy space.

The term “Avantropop” was coined by Evan Collins to describe a specific visual movement within the era, one that blended avant-garde graphic sensibilities with the glossy futurism of electropop music culture. This style became highly recognizable in Cartoon Network’s Noods (2008–2010) and CHECK it (2010–2016) eras, serving as an evolution of the network’s identity during a period of rapid digital transition.

By blending early-2010s web design, music video aesthetics, and youth-focused branding, Avantropop developed into a distinct, self-contained aesthetic that remains nostalgic for people who grew up with mid-late 2000s TV and internet media.

## Visuals

The visuals consist of asymmetrical geometric shapes, diagonal lines, CMYK color palettes, soft gradients, triangular or square patterns, and colorful polygonal designs. The *"*ITC Avant Garde Gothic*"* typeface was commonly used during the Electropop era (although other typefaces, like display typefaces such as *"*Lot*"* and *"*Val*"* for example, were also commonly used at the time). Avantropop also shares some visual elements and aspects with [Vectordelia](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vectordelia "Vectordelia"), [Superflat Pop](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Superflat_Pop "Superflat Pop"), and [Vectorheart](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Vectorheart "Vectorheart"), like vibrant color, flat designs, geometric shapes and vector-based graphics.

## Fashion

Although Avantropop is primarily a graphic design aesthetic, certain fashion trends of the late 2000s and early 2010s parallel its overall vibe:

* Neon-colored accessories
* Geometric prints
* Digital or holographic textures
* Electropop-inspired streetwear
* Leggings, graphic tees, and colorblocked pieces
* Futuristic metallics

This overlaps with styles like [Cyberpop](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cyberpop "Cyberpop"), [Scene](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Scene "Scene") fashion, and Nu-Electro fashion aesthetics.

## Media

Avantropop frequently appears in:

### Television Branding

* Cartoon Network’s Noods rebrand (2008–2010)
* Cartoon Network’s CHECK it era (2010–2016)
* Other youth-targeted network bumpers and motion graphics from the time

### Music Videos

Electropop and dance-pop videos from the late 2000s–mid 2010s often share this graphic styling, particularly those employing neon backdrops, vector overlays, or abstract digital shapes.

### Commercials

Avantropop motifs were common in advertising campaigns for:

* Tech products
* Soft drinks
* Mobile carriers
* Early app store branding
* Youth-oriented social platforms
